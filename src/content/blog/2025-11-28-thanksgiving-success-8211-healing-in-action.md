Had a wonderful Thanksgiving with both family and food.  There are many ways to handle Thanksgiving as a diabetic or near diabetic – I choose to eat all my favorite foods but I employed a strategy to reduce the harm.  It was a smashing success both because of the strategy and because I have indeed been healing.  Not healed, definitely not cured, but measurable progress has been made.

I ate carbs – I ate a LOT of carbs.  I intended to enjoy all my traditional holiday favorites yet do so in a way that harmed me less.  I ate a premeal with protein, fat, fiber and minimal carbs.  (Veggie sticks and dip, meat and cheese on crackers).  Carbs yes, but low volume.  Fiber, protein and fat were the overwhelming content and formed a “slow down the carbs” layer.  Then for the main event, I piled turkey, ham and green bean casserole on my plate first.  Ate those, THEN went back for the stuffing, potatoes, macaroni and cheese, and cheddar bay biscuit.  I ate all of those, as much as I wanted.   Thing is, I became STUFFED.  Filled to the gills such that I COULDN’T eat that much of the carby food — didn’t WANT to eat that much. It took no will power, no self denial, no longing after what everyone else was enjoying.  I had a wonderful meal of all my favorites to being comfortably stuffed.  I just ate in a way to minimize the desire to over eat the carbs.

I had a nice food coma in front of the Tv as I watched the Lions lose.  Wonderful nap of contentment and then – pie.  Two slices of pie that were delicious. Then off to the movies to watch Wicked with the family and I had popcorn. Pie and popcorn when 7 weeks ago my A1c was 7.1 — my glucose was averaging above 154.  So what did my glucose do with all this eating?  

My glucose rose about an hour after the meal and then took off after the pie. It never went above 180!  Then it came down an hour or so after the movie popcorn.  This is the glucose response of someone who’s never been diabetic.    Next post I’ll deal with the downsides – like the amount of insulin my body had to produce.  But for now, let’s focus on how this represents progress.  This is healing in action.  How do I know?  At the beginning of my health recovery journey in 2020, a quarter pounder with cheese and a medium fry sent my glucose over 400 and it didn’t come down under 200.  I’m not cured, nor am I back to my personal base level – but I have healed a good deal from where I started.

I’m thrilled.  I enjoyed the day, the food was delicious.  No sense of deprivation or being “the dieting one” on a family food fest.  I set myself up for success and the plan worked.  Now – tomorrow I’ll write the other side of this.  Quick tease:  if I eat like this all the time, I’ll become diabetic again.   Handling one meal or one weekend is not a green light to eat this way normally. 

But that’s for tomorrow.  For now I’m basking in the glow of improving health and family connection with good food.

p.s. Always consult your health team for your specific needs.

p.p.s. Educate yourself so you can fully participate in your health strategy.

p.p.p.s. Prior to this feast was 7 weeks of fasting (18 days) and carnivore/near zero carb eating the rest of the time.
