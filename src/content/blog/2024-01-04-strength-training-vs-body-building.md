I will start off by admitting a prejudice I have had since childhood. I do not react well to jocks, athletes, the heavily muscled types. I was a nerd and they were my tormentors. I know. I’m 58 years old, and I should be past my childhood traumas. And yet, I find myself turned off by the talk of “body building” as I’m reading and listening to books and videos on strength training. I shouldn’t be. It’s a wonderful thing to improve your looks and and the confidence that comes from having a nice shape. But nothing about my current journey to regain my health and abilities has anything to do about looks and body image.

I’m sharing this after/before photo again even though every time I do people get confused. The fit me with the tiny bit of muscles is both an after and a before image. I was just 20lbs shy of the fat me before I started working on my health in my mid fourties. I had a health scare and it dawned on me that I wasn’t just going to die young, I was going to live sick. A switch clicked in my mind and I embarked on the first Less of Lee journey. I lost 80lbs, and went from walking to running and then did the P90 workout. I went from not being able to do a single push-up, to being able to do 25 with my feet up on a chair.

The photo on the left was peak Lee. I looked great, felt great and I received all manner of encouragement from others. Look at him, I truly had “rebuilt my body”, I was a body builder. Even back then, though, it was about health for me.

I lost my fear and gradually went back to my old ways. I regained all the weight plus 20lbs more. I lost all of my fitness…back to not being able to do a push up. Worse, I became diseased with Type 2 diabetes and osteoarthritis amid other related ailments. And now I’m 10 years older, in my mid-fifties and having to reclaim my health yet again. That’s “peak sick Lee” on the right.

I’m more than two and a half years into Less of Lee round two. I’m no longer diabetic, and I’ve gone from walking with a cane to running 5ks. This year I’m focusing on tackling my excess fat and building strength. Like always, I’m reading books to learn how to accomplish my goals. You don’t know what you don’t know, but you can learn. At least, that’s my attitude.

I find myself reacting negatively to the “macho” aspects of bodybuilding. Strength training and bodybuilding aren't exactly the same thing but they both need you to lift weights (or other forms of resistance). And the folks who teach you about that like to talk about what a nice body you will be building and about the sex appeal and confidence that comes with it. Apparently, that’s why they were motivated to put in the hard work. For me, it’s like I’m listening to my childhood bullies.

And yet, they also make the real points about the health benefits. Muscle is health and vitality. You want to lower your insulin resistance? Build more muscle. You want to burn more fat all of the time? Build muscle. You want to walk better and easier on those arthritic knees? Build muscle. You want to lower your inflammation, sleep better, do everything you need to do around the house and yard? Build muscle.

The fact that your body is going to LOOK better and younger too…is a bonus. It’s not my goal but I look at the old photo of me and think…d@mn, it sure would be nice to bring that body back and make it even more muscular. Why not? Why shouldn’t I embrace that aspect as well.

I write this thinking that perhaps I’m not the only one who has misunderstood the importance of building muscle. I like “fat and weak Lee”. Seriously, I have enough areas of my life where I draw my self esteem that I never felt the need to “build my body”. What I don’t like is “sick and weak Lee” who lives in pain and who’s world was growing smaller and smaller with “things I can no longer do”.

And so I am owning up to “body building”. I am going to lose this fat and build muscle because I want to be “healthy Lee” who can do all the physical things he needs to do. I want to be the Lee free of all diseases that I have control over. That Lee is worth working towards. I’ve done it before and I can do it better this time. I have a bit more than a year to go before I’m sixty. Sixty year old Lee is going to put all the younger versions of me to shame.

It can be done. I will do it. Come join me with your own health building.
