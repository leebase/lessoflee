“You don’t need to run, walking is good enough”. Depends on your health goals. When I started working on my health, walking itself was a challenge. This isn’t a post to knock walking, or take away from how much of a challenge just to walk is for some people. But there is a form of “damning by low expectations” I want to address.

How much more? A little more. It’s in my mantra and it encapsulates an important health reality. If you want to improve, you have to do more. Not a lot more, but a little more. If you want your body to adapt, you have to tell it to do something that it can’t currently do. Walk a bit faster or a bit further than is comfortable. Lift a weight that is heavy for you. Any form of resistance will do: body weight, isometrics, resistance bands.

Your body is declining with age – but that’s not fate. It’s a negative feedback loop. You feel tired or sick or have pain and you don’t move. You don’t move so your body thinks it doesn’t need that muscle as muscle is expensive to keep around. You lose muscle so you aren’t as able to do as much as you used to. And on the process goes until you are weak and frail when you didn’t NEED to be.

“You don’t need to run”. Do you need to walk up and down stairs easily? Do you need to be able to pick up your grandchild? Do you need to walk on a sidewalk in the winter, and across your lawn in the summer? Do you need to be able to get in and out of the shower without fear of falling? You might not need to run but you will need to do MORE. How much more? A little more. But always.

If you are always pushing the boundaries that little bit, the day may well come when you are running as that next step more. It took almost a year for me to run my first few steps from the time I thought I might try. I didn’t get off the couch, put my cane aside, and then go out on the trail running a 5k with sprints. All those things I listed above are abilities I had lost and since have regained. There are other ways to push your body to built up your heart health, your lung capacity and your muscles. The same mindset of “you don’t need to run, walking is good enough” also works against any of those other forms.

If you are going to regain your health, build your body back up, you will have to do that which is uncomfortable and hard. What “hard” looks like will change. Walking around my neighborhood with my cane for 15 minutes was hard. Every form of exercise I’ve engaged in was hard. That’s the point. It has to be hard.

It doesn’t have to be overwhelmingly hard. You don’t need to risk injury by making a big leap from where you are. How much more? A little more. A LITTLE more. More…but only a little more.

Always. Don’t forget the always. What represents more for you today won’t be more for you tomorrow. You push and push back the boundaries as you build your body up. It’s never good enough. You never arrive and no longer need or benefit from “more”. Your body will decline and you are always fighting against that.

Walking is good for your health and your mental health. Walking is an excellent PART of a healthy life style. I love my walks. I simply have other health goals that walking will not get me to. I do not run for the love of running. I push more and go further for all the benefits I mentioned – to regain my abilities to do other things. My world had become smaller and more painful. I am pushing back the boundaries with each ability I reclaim.

I encourage you to walk – and to do more than just walk. I want for you to reclaim your abilities to make your world larger and you more able. You get there by doing more and going farther.

How much more? A little more.

How much father? A bit father.

Always.
