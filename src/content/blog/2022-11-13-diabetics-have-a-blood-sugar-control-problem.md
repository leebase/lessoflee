Type 2 diabetes is the inability to control the level of glucose in the blood. The demarcation is an A1c of 6.5 – which represents how much of your red blood cells have been glycated with sugar over their life span (which is about 3 months).

What do I eat or drink to lower my A1c or lower my blood sugar?

You don’t have an excess blood protein disease, or an excess blood fiber disease or an excess blood fat disease. The number one, most important step is to not eat sugar in the first place. Carbs are sugar. Doesn’t matter if they are from honey or bananas, table sugar, Coca Cola or oatmeal or whole grain bread or beans.

Glucose is glucose is glucose. If you have trouble with high glucose in your blood, the FIRST thing to do is to stop eating carbs. Slow carbs, fast carbs…one may be WORSE than the other, but carbs are carbs.

Step 2 is “use up the carbs you ate”. If you are highly active, you will use up carbs and that removes them from the blood. But, it’s WAY easier to eat too many carbs than it is to exercise them out of your blood.

The third priority is “how do I get my cells to accept the glucose out of the blood”. The problem isn’t your insulin being deficient. Insulin is the hormone that (among other things) tells your cells to accept the blood sugar, removing it from the blood and converting it to either energy in the cells or storing it in the cells as fat. Your insulin is fine. But your cells have decided they don’t want any more energy so they stop paying attention to the insulin. This is known as insulin resistance.

Every form of losing fat weight will help your cells return to insulin sensitivity. There are likely better and worse ways, but the main thing is to get your cells out of the state of being over saturated with energy. Google the Twinkie diet and find the proof that if you eat few enough, you can even reverse type 2 diabetes eating nothing but twinkies.

There are far better ways to regain insulin sensitivity: exercise, fasting and not eating carbs past your individual tolerance.

How insulin resistant you are….or how over filled with energy your cells are…isn’t the same as the next person. That means what you may have to do isn’t exactly the same as what someone has to do. The more severely diabetic you are, the more radical your changes will need to be.

But it can be done. Health can be regained. Drugs will put temporary band aids and slow the progression of the disease. Take them, of course, if your A1c is in the danger zone. But change what you eat and how (or if) you exercise, and maybe add fasting….and you can heal.

I have. Lots of us have. You can too
