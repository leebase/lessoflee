I intended to lift today, but Mother Nature had other plans for me.

A mere three inches of snow — but I had retired from shoveling years ago. Too many middle-aged men die of heart attacks while shoveling snow. It’s no joke.

But my snow guy had a bad day and didn’t show up.

Great. This is exactly why I’ve been exercising and pushing my body in the first place. I don’t exercise to get good at exercising. I train to become more able.

Three years ago, I couldn’t walk across grass without a cane. Today? I can shovel a relatively light snow.

It would’ve made no sense to do my planned push day workout inside while real work was waiting outside.

I took my time. I paid attention to how I felt. And I stopped when I started to get tired.

Could I have finished it all with just 30 more minutes? Absolutely. But I’d already gone 45 minutes, and that was enough. I’ll go out later and finish the job.

Pushing your body isn’t about heroics or machismo. It’s about building health, strength, and vitality by doing more and going farther.

How much more?

A little more.

How much farther?

A bit farther.

Always.

That’s how I went from walking for 15 minutes with a cane to running a 5K without stopping.

It took years. Years of doing what I could — and a little more each time.

I retired from shoveling snow because I’d lost the physical ability to do it. And because I knew the dangers of being an unfit, morbidly obese, middle-aged man. It was a wise decision at the time.

But I didn’t have to stay in that condition.

And I haven’t.

I’d love to tell you I’ve had nothing but progress and success. The truth is, I have to battle against myself all the time — and I don’t always win.

But today was a win.

Today was the result of years of consistent effort. Years of doing a little more, not being perfect, but staying in the game and never giving up.

Go do your body good.

And if you’ve got a success story — big or small — I’d love to hear it.
