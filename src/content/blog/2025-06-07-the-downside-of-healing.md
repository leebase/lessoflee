Here I am, resident Hope Dealer of Healing, about to confess the downside of healing.  Well, actual healing has no downside, obviously.  I’m speaking of the audacity of saying the truth:  Type 2 Diabetes can be healed for LOTS, if not all, people.

I’m not going to cover the exposition of the ability to heal in this post.  Rather, I’m going to address the emotional downside of hearing that healing is possible.  The intent of the truthful message that Type 2 diabetes can, in many cases, be healed is to empower people to take the actions to have their own bodies healed.  Hard actions, true, but people can do hard things.

The downside of personal empowerment is self-blame.  If my condition can be healed from the terrible consequences I’m experiencing or facing by my own actions – then I am to blame for not having done these actions before.  Or in time to prevent illness. It’s ok if your first reaction to hope is pain.  It just means you are waking up.

Are you to blame?  Certainly not all by yourself.  There is economics, culture, genetics, family history, the highly processed mass marketed food environment, the captured and corrupt sick care system masquerading as health care.  This isn’t even an exhaustive list.  There are a LOT of factors outside your control.  And yet – many tens of thousands of people have indeed healed, or at least improved their health.

Self blame isn’t the point or goal.  It’s simply a natural reaction to being empowered and taking responsibility.  For to be sure, YOU, are the sole person suffering and YOU are the only one who can make changes.  It is not “I am to blame” because there are a LOT of places to point the finger.  Rather it’s, “I am responsible for what to do now”.  There is no role for shame over the past.  If you feel it, forgive yourself.  Don’t shield yourself from the power of what you can do next.

There is no downside to actual healing.  And I must add the following caveat as I hear people misunderstanding what I mean by healing.  You don’t get to eat all the comfort food, junk food, fast food that made you sick.  That’s what injured your metabolism in the first place.   It’s like breaking your leg.  You immobilize it so that your body can heal.  Healing is NORMAL.  But if you kept walking on the broken leg, it won’t heal even though it could.  And having healed, you are not somehow immune from breaking it again.

I’m as guilty as anybody.  I get fat, work hard to lose the weight, then get fat again.  And again.  And again.  I’ve healed from Type 2 Diabetes, needed no meds for a few years.  And developed it again via my own actions.  Knowing better is not the same as doing better.  I’m a living, walking, breathing example that healing is possible.  Also that health is not a steady state, you have to maintain it.

I choose empowerment.  My own diabetes doctor soothed me with the words “Diabetes isn’t your fault” as she piled on a third (and $1000/mo) prescription and planned on me going on an insulin pump.  When I took responsibility, educated myself, did the hard work, and got off all the meds – I asked her:  Why isn’t what I did, what you teach?  Her reply:  not everyone is willing to make the changes you did.  

She didn’t believe in spreading the message of personal empowerment, of hope because she had so little regard for the hard things people CAN do.  So here I am, your hope dealer, letting YOU know – your body is more wonderful and powerful than you can imagine.  There is hope, and with that – responsibility.

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs.

p.p.s. Educate yourself to fully participate in your health strategy.
