I’m living with 3 flat tires:  two sore pinky toes and a fractured thumb from a couple of recent falls.  A “push day” is out of the question.   Can’t lift weights yet because of the thumb.  I went out for a nice 4 mile walk.

I was feeling so good, the sun was shining, and I just WANTED to run.  So I did.  It was glorious!  I’m still recovering so I didn’t try to push myself, but I did run as long as it felt good.  The walked, then run some more.

It’s a bummer being injured and unable to work out.  And yet, I am amazed that I’m recovering much quicker than I used to.  Working out, strength training, and pushing myself over these last three years has made me more resilient. 

Falls are dangerous – much more so in the shape I was when I started, April of ’21.  Back then I was walking with a cane and lived as a shut in during the winters.  I couldn’t walk safely across grass in the summer let alone ice and snow.  Getting in and out of the shower felt dangerous.  I had been shopping around for a chair to put in the shower so I wouldn’t fall.

Getting over diabetes was just the start.  I have a lot more healing and recovery to claim.  It’s worth all the hard work. Medications will NEVER do for you what taking care of your health via what you eat, how you exercise, sleep and manage stress will.  Take them when you need them, but don’t be lulled into a false sense of security.  Do the hard work to take care of your body.

And when you do inevitably face a setback – don’t just “set back”.  Be appropriately active in recovery.  Maybe not the next day or two after an injury, but at some point you get back out doing a little.  And then a little more.

Being active during recovery aids in faster recovery: By promoting circulation, reducing stiffness, and maintaining fitness, active recovery can help accelerate the healing process and get you back to your regular activities sooner.

On push day’s I normally aim for 90-95% of max heart rate.  Today I was happy to hit in the 80-90% range.  My pinky toes felt fine and I didn’t injure myself further.  I don’t think my thumb is ready yet for lifting weights.  But tomorrow I’ll start doing body weight squats.  SOMETHING!

Why?  I’ve come a long way but have a long way to go.  I’m trying to put off that heart attack that is most definitely in my future…or at least survive it.  No joke.  Being obese for decades, high blood pressure and diabetic for years doesn’t heal instantly.  Getting my blood sugar under control is awesome, but I’m not out of the woods health wise.

I did my body good today.  How’d you do?

p.s.  Always consult your own health team for your specific needs

p.p.s. Educate yourself to fully participate in your health strategy
