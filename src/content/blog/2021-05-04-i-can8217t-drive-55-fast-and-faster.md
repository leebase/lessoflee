I will be exploring the benefits to my own body of fasting.   Why fasting?   I have a sugar disorder...or an insulin disorder.  Whichever is the chicken and which is the egg, I'll not address in this post.  What is clear is that, as a diabetic, I need to gain control of my blood sugar.

One way is medication.   I am under care of my diabetes specialist through my health plan and hospital.   I'm not taking insulin, but I am on 3 different meds.  An A1C of 11.6 is shockingly high.  Diabetes is a progressively degenerative disease that medications help manage. Thing is, nobody is cured of type 2 diabetes by taking medication.  They just aren't.   If you don't change your diet, you will be taking diabetes meds your whole life – and most likely – you will face all of the problems that diabetes causes, eventually.  With a change in diet, you can eventually get off your meds and stop the progression of the disease.  Keeping in mind that diabetes destroys every organ system that you have.   So yeah...I'm motivated to both take my meds, and do what I can to not need them.

From my reading/research – the best way to gain control of your blood sugar (reverse insulin resistance) is through a low sugar (low carb) diet.  In addition, low-car diets help lose weight.   Makes sense....if you have too much sugar in your blood, it's because you ate too much sugar.

- [The ADA Endorses Low Carb as an option](https://www.dietdoctor.com/american-diabetes-association-endorses-low-carb-diet-as-option)

- [The ADA Macronutrients Stance – no one way for everybody](https://care.diabetesjournals.org/content/42/5/731#sec-9)

- [The ADA Food Plate](https://www.diabetesfoodhub.org/articles/what-is-the-diabetes-plate-method.html#:~:text=The%20Diabetes%20Plate%20Method%20is,you%20need%20is%20a%20plate!)

You can eat – and just eat food that doesn't have a lot of sugar (low carb diet).  You can also, just not eat.  Let's say you skip breakfast, and stop snacking after dinner.  That's pretty much the definition of 16:8 intermittent fasting.  If you stop eating at 8pm at night and don't eat until noon the next day, you have 16 hours completely sugar free (from your diet, your liver has another thing to say about that).

You get the best benefit from fasting AND eating a low-carb diet.  I have gone "whole hog low-carb" before and am just not willing to start so strict.  So, I'm going to use fasting as a way of supporting the need to get control of my sugar as I convert my diet (excuse me, my "way of eating") to low carb over time.

After finally going to the diabetes counseling my dr told me to do 2 years ago – I have been testing my blood sugar.  It's been in the 240-260 (sometimes higher) range even with the new med added to my 2 existing ones.  I had 3 readings just below 200 in the 2 weeks.   Then I did a 24 hour fast.  Sounds a lot more dire than it is...I just skipped 2 meals and didn't snack.   Trust me, I have PLENTY of food stored all over my body to handle missing 2 meals.   My blood sugar after the fast?   100!

 The first normal blood sugars I've had came after a fast.  I think I'm on the right path — for me.  I'll keep blogging my journey, who knows what lessons I'll learn.
