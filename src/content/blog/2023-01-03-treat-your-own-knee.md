TLDR – The book Treat Your Own Knee by Robin McKenzie is a great resource on how to rehab/heal your knee.

I’ve been on a health reclamation journey the last 18 months and have seen a good amount of progress.  I’ve put my T2 Diabetes into remission and have gone from walking with a cane to running a 5k.  I’ve read a couple dozen books on diabetes and metabolic health and have felt comfortable talking about the science and even giving out recommendations to others.

With my OA arthritic knees I’ve also seen great progress.  However, other than sharing what I’ve done, I’ve been very hesitant to give advice to others as I’ve almost accidentally come to the progress.  I wasn’t even intending to work on my arthritis, I was working to heal my diabetes.  And yet, when I cut the sugars out of my diet, I noticed my knees started feeling better.  Almost the equivalent to the relief I’d get from taking NSAIDs like ibuprofen.  Something I’d stopped doing after I learned about how they harm your kidneys.   I’d tried many lotions and potions and they all helped a bit.  The change in diet helped more.

Exercise is also something I was engaging in to get my health back on track.  All I could do was walk, and not that far and not that fast.  I had to walk on a good sidewalk…I was too unstable to walk across the yard.  I started exercising with a virtual reality headset as a way to keep making progress during the winter.  Couldn’t walk outside with snow and ice and the gyms had been closed due to the pandemic.  The app I used is called Supernatural and it had me (trying) to squat and lunge.   I couldn’t do much at first, even fell over a couple times.  But my legs started getting stronger.  Again – as an accident – I noticed that I was able to walk better and go down stairs better because my legs were getting stronger.  My troubles walking weren’t solely OA pain.  I had become weak.

Spring came and I was out walking so much better.  I began to walk faster, then faster until I was able to do that “butt wiggling” speed walking.  I began to wonder – could I run?  I couldn’t at first.  Not even close.  It was like having to go through rehab after an accident.  I literally had to relearn how to run.  It wasn’t easy and it didn’t come quick.  But I worked at it and took my first successful running steps around March of 2022.  By the end of the year I was able to run a 5k without stopping.  Doing just a little more, going a bit further.   

Is it wise to run on arthritic knees?  Well, it’s not wise to jar your knee and my own knees would very much tell me if I was doing something like that.  I developed a way of running that’s similar to using an elliptical machine.  I was taking the shock with my leg muscles and not my knee.  But as I progressed, I began to be able to run more normally – with a mid to forefoot strike.  I have less pain after a 5k run than a 4 mile walk.  Can I recommend this to anyone else?  That’s where I run into a knowledge gap.  I know what I’m experiencing but I don’t have any theory based on science to advocate this for anyone else.

I am reading the book Treat Your Own Knee – and I wish I had read this years ago.  The author addresses the causes of OA arthritis, and healing, and exercises and pain.   I didn’t follow this exact method, but I will be incorporating the exercises recommended going forward.  

There is hope.  I know I’m not a unicorn.  I recently tweeted my 5k moment and received responses for others who had also gone from needing a cane to walk to regaining a lot of their former ability.
