The supplement industry isn’t regulated. Absolutely anything and everything can be promoted as a supplement. It’s ripe for and rife with…fraud.

But Lee – are you saying you take a “buttload of supplements”. More like a “mouth load” – I’m not into suppositories….but yes, I take a lot of supplements.

One of many reasons I’m quite reticent to give specific recommendations on supplements is that I have no idea whatsoever on which companies you can trust and which you can’t. I try to be wise with my purchasing, but it’s not like I’m out there in the manufacturing plants verifying their processes. Nor, frankly, am I even verifying the claims that some put up about.

I’ve included a photo of the claims for quality that one of the places I buy vitamins from displays on their website. Have I tried to verify that they have actually achieved these certifications? No. It’s nice to see the claim, but anybody can make a claim.

There have been several exposes written about supplements, even though sold in major retailers, don’t even contain the ingredients they claim on the bottle.

Then you have the disparity between “our body needs vitamin ” and the fact that getting that vitamin in a pill won’t actually mean your body is getting the vitamin benefit. Or you read about this or that supplement being studied and shown effective — but are you taking the form of that supplement in the amount used in the study?

I may not agree with those who completely dismiss the entire supplement industry as snake oil – but it’s not like they don’t have a point. There are a lot of bad actors out there and you can’t tell by the quality of their website OR the price they charge for their product. Scammers understand the power of pricing just like fashion designers do.
