How to grapple with the reality that diabetes need not be permanent?

First – diabetes is the state where your body can no longer control blood glucose levels. There is more to it than that, but the lack of glucose control is the part that defines the diabetic.

Consider someone with peanut allergies instead of diabetes. Some people are so allergic that they die if they eat peanuts (or even come into contact). For this analogy we will consider only those who have a problem when they eat peanuts. Eating peanuts means poor health and or death to them. Avoid peanuts and everything is ok.

Type 2 diabetics are like this. We have a sensitivity to carbohydrates. We have an inability to control our blood glucose when we eat carbohydrates. There are medications and insulin to treat the high blood glucose that diabetics need.

There is a cure for this. It has been shown time and again right here by the members of this forum. People who regain the ability to return to proper glucose control without the meds.

Here is the big BUT. Gaining glucose control through lifestyle changes does not change the carbohydrate sensitivity. Regaining your insulin sensitivity does happen. At best, though, you heal back to where you started. Where YOU started.

Just as there are people with no allergic sensitivity to peanuts, there are people with a lot less sensitivity to carbohydrates. Those lucky people can eat anything and not have a problem. Sure, some people only think they are having no problems but will find out later they are wrong.

A side note. That we are different is true. There are people who are heavy smokers and live to very old age without ever developing lung cancer. And yet many smokers do develop lung cancer from smoking.

Back to the carbohydrate sensitive people. Our “allergy” to carbohydrates takes a lot longer to notice than someone who has an acute allergy to peanuts. But like the peanut-intolerant, we only experience a problem by eating what we are allergic to.

This isn’t a perfect analogy, none ever are. For the carb intolerant it’s the amount or level of carbohydrate that matters. As our insulin sensitivity returns, we can tolerate more.

We can reset ourselves. Heal ourselves. Cure diabetes. Yes, I said cure. But that means: cured from the inability to control blood sugar without pharmaceuticals.

Your carbohydrate intolerance isn’t cured. The result of having eaten carbs beyond your individual tolerance for decades….can be reset. Reset to YOUR normal. Your healthy normal.

And you can stay at your healthy normal forever by a lifestyle that should have been YOUR normal all along. If you revert to eating carbs past your tolerance, you can RE-develop type 2 diabetes. You need not fear “my diabetes is going to come back like a cancer at any time”. It’s not like that. You can live a wonderful, healthy life free of the fear of diabetes forever. All by continuing to eat and move healthy. Which is how health works for everyone.
