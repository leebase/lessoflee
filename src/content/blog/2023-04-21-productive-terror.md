It’s April 22, 2021 and I finally met for the first time with a diabetes specialist.  My regular doctor told me a couple years prior to do so, but I dragged my feet.  I wasn’t taking my type 2 diabetes seriously.  I took my metformin and glipizide.  I pricked my finger daily for about a month.  180 … always 180.  And then I stopped.  And now I’m finally facing the music.  My A1c has gone from 8.5 to 11.2!   Terror grips my heart – I could go BLIND!

Blindness is one of my primal fears.  There are a lot of devastating consequences to diabetes…I won’t list them all here.  My goal isn’t to spread the fear but to speak of my own journey. This wasn’t my first encounter with health terror that turned out to be positively productive for me.

In my mid-40’s I was morbidly obese at 315lbs.   I made sure I had good life insurance.  Dying early was a forgone conclusion.  At least in my mind.  I don’t come from a family where the men live to old age.  My grandfathers both died in their 40s.  My father would go on to die at 64.  There are worse things than death…or at least…more motivational to me.  I spent a terrible night in the hospital for what was supposed to be an outpatient surgery.  In the wake of that terrible night it dawned on me – I wasn’t going to die early, I was going to live sick.  That, more than early death, terrified me.

I kicked into gear and lost 80lbs and got fit in about a year. I wasn’t arthritic nor diabetic at that time.  Just morbidly obese.  Of course, I was developing those conditions but they hadn’t yet surfaced.  I was looking good, feeling good and was very proud of what I had accomplished.  And so the fear subsided and over the next 10 years I regained all that weight plus 20 more lbs.

This second moment of terror kicked off a second health reclamation journey.  Older and sicker, the road has been much more difficult.  I had tried to “get back on the wagon” many times over those ten years.  Once that fear hit me, a “click” went on in my brain and I knew – I got this.  

I restarted my blog where I announced to the world that I was going to make changes and reclaim my health:  [https://lessoflee.wordpress.com/2021/05/03/im-back-8-years-later-now-diabetic](https://lessoflee.wordpress.com/2021/05/03/im-back-8-years-later-now-diabetic) .   I hadn’t achieved anything.  Nothing had yet been overcome.  I had never tackled diabetes or osteoarthritis before. I had felt deep terror and used that to get about learning and making changes.

During my first health journey, I made more progress in one year than I have in two years during this second attempt.  I’m hovering around the 60lbs lost (earlier last year I was down 85lbs).  My A1c is 5.2 and I’m off all diabetes meds.  I have more health complications to heal from this time than last.  I’m proud of the progress I’ve made just like I was last time.  I’m committed to not going back to my old ways.  I don’t live with that terror any more.  I know better now how quickly and easily I can lose the ground I’ve worked so hard to cover. I know how sick I can become, how disabled.   Not going back!

I learned lessons from my first journey and built on them and learned more this second.  Maybe it’s a good thing it’s taking so much more work this time around.  I’m trying to change my mindset from “do xyz to fix abc”.  There is no finish line.  I have to eat healthy always.  I need to move healthy always.  I need to manage stress and sleep healthy always.  Fear got me started, but I’ve long moved past it.  I like my new life.  I wish I could put some folks in my mind and in my skin so that they could feel how I feel.  It’s SO good, that if you could just envision feeling so well, you wouldn’t think twice about making the changes.  No food tastes as good as being healthy feels.
