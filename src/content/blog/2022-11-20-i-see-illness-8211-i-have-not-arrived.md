Every now and then I take stock of where I am – independent of where I’ve been.

Why do I push? Why do I strive? Why isn’t walking 15 minutes a day enough? Why am I still fasting? Why do I continue to persist in “dieting”.

Look at the photo. It’s as clear as the nose on my face. Well, truthfully, it’s a LOT easier to see than my nose. It’s metabolic illness. That’s what I see when I am presented with a large waist to height ratio. It’s not a “beer belly”. It’s not “love handles” or a “muffin top” or a “dad bod”. It’s not even “being fat”. It’s illness.

Type 2 diabetes is well down the line of metabolic syndrome. Long before you’ve gotten so unhealthy that your body can’t control blood glucose levels, you’ve been sick and getting sicker.

What is metabolic syndrome?
Metabolic syndrome is a group of five conditions that can lead to heart disease, diabetes, stroke and other health problems. Metabolic syndrome is diagnosed when someone has three or more of these risk factors:

High blood glucose (sugar)
Low levels of HDL (“good”) cholesterol in the blood
High levels of triglycerides in the blood
Large waist circumference or “apple-shaped” body
High blood pressure

[https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/metabolic-syndrome/about-metabolic-syndrome](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/metabolic-syndrome/about-metabolic-syndrome)

I had all 5 of these risk factors. A heart attack was in the cards….may STILL be in the cards.

I’ve gotten my bs under control. Awesome. But that leaves me with 4 of the 5 risk factors.

My HDL and triglycerides refused to budge even as I lost 50lbs, got off my meds, and got my bs to non-diabetic levels. When I went carnivore for a month these 2 factors finally came into normal. But, since then, I’ve not been as strict and so they’ve regressed a bit.

But there remains 2 other factors…one of which you can see in the photo. I have a long way to go to fix my waistline. I still have hypertension…I take medication for that but it’s still elevated.

It does not matter that I USED to be even less healthy. Your body doesn’t grade on a curve. There is no extra credit for trying or making an effort.

Maybe I’ve put that heart attack off a few years. Maybe it’ll happen in my mid sixties instead of my late fifties. Who knows. But I don’t want to die at 64 as my dad did. I don’t want to be sick and unhealthy as I already have been…and didn’t have to be.

I am healing, but I am not healed. I am improving but I have not arrived. I was already dealing with disability and increasingly so. Now I’m pushing back the boundaries, reclaiming abilities along with my health.

This isn’t “being too hard on myself”. Being needlessly sick and in pain and disabled was being hard on myself — when I didn’t have to be that way. I’m optimistic as I look forward to less pain in the future, more abilities coming, and an increased health span for a long life.
