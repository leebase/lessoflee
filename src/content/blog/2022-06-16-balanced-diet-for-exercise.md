I really stepped in it the other day when I declared “walking isn’t exercise”. I have learned that saying “now hear me out” and my reasoning that followed could not overcome the initial shock declaration. So, I have a new mental model. I am aiming for a balanced diet of exercise: walking, aerobics and strength training.

None of the types of exercise should be neglected as each brings benefits unique to them. Books are written about this so I’m not going to try to be comprehensive.

Walking – walking is a fundamental need. A life without the ability to walk is one that is deeply constrained. It’s a life I was experiencing and heading for worse. In addition to just being able to walk anywhere I need to – walking is a mental health practice for me. I just don’t feel right in the head when I can’t get my walks in. Living near Chicago – there are many months of winter where I can’t walk outside, particularly with my arthritic knees. I go stir crazy.

I love my walks – I need no extra motivation to go for a stroll. As the weather is nice now, I was finding that I’d walk to the exclusion of my aerobics exercise. That was when I came up with the lamentable phrase “walking isn’t exercise” – because I didn’t want to stop my aerobics. I needed to put walking into it’s own category of good and healthful thing to do…so that I was still going to say “I got to get my exercise in” meaning, I got to get my heart rate up. I walk without my shirt on and get some good Vitamin D – when I can. Apologies to my neighbors eyes.

I hadn’t done my VR aerobics exercise in awhile. I’ve been playing table tennis and walking instead. Man, I’ve been missing out. It’s hot, 107f and I decided to stay in my air-conditioned house and get a Supernatural workout in. I had a glorious session. My heart rate got up into the 140s. I did 25 minutes of a medium level workout and then did a 15 minute “pro’s only” high intensity workout. I love getting my heart rate up. And yes, one can walk in such a way to get your heart rate up, but that’s not the kind I do with my “mental health” walks.

Then there’s strength training. I know the least about this and so I joined up with a program with a trainer. I go twice week for 20 minutes. It’s been great and I can already feel the benefits from getting stronger. My cholesterol came down when I hadn’t changed my diet but had added strength training. Building muscle helps reduce insulin resistance and amps up your metabolism 24&#215;7, not just when you are exercising. I used to think that muscles were just for the vain or athletes. I now understand that muscle is health, muscle is vitality. And yes, muscle looks a lot better than fat. I’m not there yet, whatever muscle I have is buried under copious amounts of fat. But I’m working on it.

It’s been a bit over a year since I began reclaiming my health. The headline is that my diabetes has been put into remission, I’m off all the meds. Deserving of equal billing – I have so much more vitality. I am walking better. Feeling better. Getting stronger. I’m filled with energy. I don’t have to force myself to workout, I am driven to burn off the excess energy. I am going to wade back into the controversial waters — but walking alone would not have gotten me to where I am. I couldn’t even walk they way I do now if I hadn’t don’t the strength training to build up my legs. My resting heart rate has gone from the 80’s to the 50’s. That means my heart is having to beat 37% less just to live. I walk faster but my heart rate from walking is lower than it was at first – as I’ve become more fit.

I want all the benefits available to those who have a balanced “exercise diet”.
