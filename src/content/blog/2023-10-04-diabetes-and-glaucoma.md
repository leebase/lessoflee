There are several ways that Type 2 Diabetes affects eye health. In fact, diabetes is the #1 cause of blindness. Diabetic retinopathy is the primary way that diabetics lose their sight – but it’s glaucoma that I am dealing with.

A few weeks ago I had a micro-stroke in my right eye. This is also an eye event where diabetes is a risk factor. I am suffering significant vision issues in that eye. But more on that in a bit.

One might ask “but Lee, I thought you had healed your diabetes”. And yes, I have my blood sugar under control with no more need of diabetes medications. Some call this remission, I prefer healing. But – there is far more to “type 2 diabetes” than out of control blood sugar. I have written that I’m far from healthy, I am “less unhealthy”. There are ways I’ve damaged my health that I have not yet healed, and may never be able to. But what can any of us do but press on? Type 2 diabetes is a later stage in the disease “metabolic syndrome”. There is much more to heal even after you get your blood sugar under control.

Today I had my follow up. For the last couple of days I’ve noticed my vision improving. It appears I have two issues at play: blood from the micro stroke/burst blood vessel and inflammation in my retina. It’s the blood that has made reading difficult if not impossible. And that is the condition that appears to be improving. It just takes time for the body to reabsorb the blood. This is good news. I have high hopes that my right eye will be able to read again. I already can see the improvement.

However, what the eye specialist has been focused on this entire time is the inflammation in the retina. He was prepared to start eye injections, but decided to give it another month. The inflammation worsened from the first visit a month ago. This process has the ability to destroy my eye sight.

It is not uncommon for diabetics to need these injections in their eye. For some it helps a lot, for others it slows the progression. And, unfortunately, some will go blind.

It is the fear of going blind that finally kicked off my health recovery journey. My A1c was 11.2, and I was already on 2 meds and a third was added. I was told that I’d be starting insulin soon. Going blind is one of my primal fears. And it’s not an unjustified fear for a diabetic.

We talked again about exercise. The specialist does not think the micro stroke was driven by high blood pressure or clotting. It’s the glaucoma driven by diabetes. Everything I’ve been doing to improve my metabolic health is exactly what I need to keep doing. I will resume my weekly &#8216;push day', while closely monitoring how I feel, my vision, and my blood pressure levels. I will continue my daily walks, regular aerobics and strength training. I will continue to eat low or even no carb (carnivore). Sleep and stress management are also key.

I have improved my health, there is no doubt about that. I have far more to go. If I keep my sight into my 70s and lose it in my 80s, is that not better than losing it in my 50s? If I have a heart attack in my 80s is that not better than my 50s? Yes, yes it is. Health is worth working for even if there is no guarantee.

I was actually encouraged by today. It is scary, no doubt. I am hopeful that the blood keeping me from reading will go away. I am getting treatment for the inflammation and being monitored by an excellent doctor.

I hope everyone who reads this finds their own “moment of inspiration”. It might stem from fear, as it did for me, or simply from a desire to be healthy. Type 2 Diabetes can be devastating to one’s health, but it doesn’t have to be. Put in the hard work of returning to as much health as you can.

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs

p.p.s. Educate yourself and be a full participant in your health strategy
