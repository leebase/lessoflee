Healthy living first and when meds are needed, the least effective amount for the shortest needed time – is now my guiding philosophy.  This is far different from “doctor, I’m dealing with xyz, what medication do I need to fix it”.

I took diabetes meds for years, needing higher amounts and more meds as my disease got worse.  I was planning on getting around to changing my diet, but never did.  Then my A1c hit 11.2 (dangerously diabetic) even though I was on 3 meds I was going to have insulin shots added.  I finally took my health seriously, made the lifestyle changes and was able to get off all the meds and avoid the shots.  The light went on in my head how much health could be regained when one stops the medication mindset.

I still take blood pressure and glaucoma medication.  I hope to not need the BP med one day and may need the glaucoma drops forever.   I’m certainly not anti-med to the point of pro blindness.

Yesterday I pulled a muscle in my back and here is how I worked out the decision to take something for the pain.  In the past I popped NSAID anti-inflammatories like they were candy, unaware of how they can destroy your kidneys.

I was in pain and could barely walk.  However, I’ve learned that inflammation after an injury is part of the bodies healing process.  Taking an anti-inflammatory would reduce the pain but it would also slow the healing process.  I let the pain guide me to rest and not move my back.  I also took a cold shower, used a massage gun, and later a heating pad.

That was the state I was in when I wrote yesterday’s blog.  Thing is, I need to walk my dog 3 times a day lest he soil our carpets.  Gentle movement is helpful for recovery so I took him for a short walk.  I could BARELY walk.  My wife is sick with the flu so it was still on me to walk the dog.

I thus decided to take an NSAID.  It definitely helped at the next time to walk the dog.  I still stayed off my feet as much as possible.  The reduction in pain was also helpful for sleeping.

This morning my back is feeling better, walking the dog was much easier.  I am not going to take any more pain killers if I can manage without them.  The smallest effective amount for the shortest time when the natural health practices aren’t enough.

I also keep in mind that my ability to do more as the pain subsides could well be counter to healing. I will continue to take it easy and stick to gentle movement to help my back heal.  

I’m glad medications exist, but now I am suspicious of them instead of treating them like magical candy.

p.s. Always consult your health team for your specific needs.

p.p.s. Educate yourself. Be your own best advocate.
