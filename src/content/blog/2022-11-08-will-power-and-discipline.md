This topic appears a lot. You’ve seen me improve my A1c. You’ve seen me lose weight. You’ve seen me improve my fitness. You’ve seen me regain abilities from the disability that is osteoarthritis.

Not in a day, not in a week, not in a month. But persistence is having more and more good days and few and fewer off-the-wagon days.

And you marvel at what I’ve accomplished BECAUSE I (appear to) have will power and discipline. Or any other list of “magical” qualities that would allow YOU to have such victories IF ONLY you possessed them.

I won’t deny that I have a certain amount of discipline and a certain amount of self control and other positive qualities. But look at me….look at the me of a year and a half ago. 336lbs, morbidly obese, type 2 diabetic, couch potato, hobbling around on painful arthritic knees.

Such a sad fate, to be disabled more and more. Destined to die early as my father and his father before him (and my mother's father and mother). My greatly improved health in a year and a half is testimony that I didn’t HAVE to be in the state I had got to. If my own actions have turned my health around, then it is assuredly true that my poor health was due, in large part, to my own poor choices and character deficiencies.

I hope to inspire you in this: will power and self control are not qualities you are born with. You develop them just like you develop everything else. It’s no different than getting physically fit.

How do you make muscles grow? You do things that require bigger muscles BEFORE you have bigger muscles. Lifting weights tears down your muscles. Then as you rest and recover your body builds them back a bit stronger.

To build self control and discipline you have to do things that REQUIRE those qualities before you have them. The result will be improvement. Not perfection.

You try and fail. You listen and learn. You try again and do a bit better before failing. You evaluate and reflect. You adjust and keep on moving. Not always forward. Not from success to success. You have victories and failures and you keep going.

You start to have more good hours and fewer bad ones. Then more good days and fewer bad ones. Onward you go…making progress.

How much more? A little more.

How far? A bit farther.

Always

This is true of will power and discipline. It’s true of eating healthy, it’s true of physical fitness, mindset, and sleep.

Every day you are as young as you will ever be again. Every day is the day to start or restart.

You can do it.
