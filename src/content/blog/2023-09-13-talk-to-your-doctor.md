I know I can sometimes come across as someone who “doesn’t listen to doctors”. And for sure, I have my reasons. I would still be diabetic today if I had listened to my doctor who told me, &#8216;Diabetes isn’t your fault; it's genetic. Take these meds, and at the next appointment, we will start you on insulin. However, what I really advocate for is educating yourself and taking part in your own health and not assume that nothing can be done except to take a pill or a shot.

This week “something” happened to my right eye. I noticed an inability to see the tv well. Are my glasses that dirty? A cloudy spot in my eye, and it kind of moved around like a sty…but wasn’t a string like thing any other sty I’d ever had. I had a day of denial. Then a day of increasing fear. Something is wrong with my eye! Will it just get better? Is this my glaucoma now in the middle of my eye sight?

“Hey Stupid!” I tell myself, “go to the eye doctor”. Can you relate to knowing you need to go to the doctor and at the same time not wanting to go because you don’t want to have your fears confirmed? Yep, that’s me too.

Amazingly, my optometrist saw me right away. I had some blood vessel burst (no pain, and no visible blood). She sent me to a specialist who also saw me right away. Apparently I had a “micro-stroke”. This is a blood vessel in the retina issue and not a stroke in your brain. Still, something that made me very glad that I went to the doctor.

Nothing to do at the moment except continue to monitor and go to the emergency room right away if my vision changes. This may heal all on its own, or there may need to be steps to take.

No “Push Day Saturday” today. Not until I talk to my regular doctor to see if high intensity exercise is off the books for awhile. Exercise is how you heal and I’m not jumping to the conclusion that it SHOULD be avoided. I’m just going to make sure to get my doctor’s input. I did go for an hour walk. I had asked the eye doctor also and he had no issues with moderate exercise and directed me to talk to my regular doctor about anything more.

I do know this – everything I have been doing to overcome type 2 diabetes needs to continue. Fasting, Very low carb diet or carnivore (Fung). High insulin drives vascular issues (Bickman). My A1c crept back up into the pre-diabetic range as I haven’t been as diligent in my eating.

And yes, I am doing what I can to educate myself. We should all be as knowledgeable about our own health and health conditions as we can be. It is not “either/or” rather “both/and”. Work with your doctor.

Ps: as always, talk to your own health team for your specific needs and action plan.

Pps: as always, never ONLY listen to pill pushers and illness managers, take control and work on restoring your health

Books to read: Why We Get Sick by Dr Ben Bickman, The Diabetes Code by Dr Jason Fung

ppps: the photo is how far away I parked at the Costco eye doctor. I no longer need a cane or a handicap sticker nor do I bother with driving round and round trying to find a close space. Increased ability is a benefit to diet and exercise that diabetes meds never can give you.
