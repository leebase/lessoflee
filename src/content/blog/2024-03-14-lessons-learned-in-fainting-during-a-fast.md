Last week I fainted – yet without losing consciousness – on the 9th day of my quarterly extended fast.  I’ve been fasting for years.  I am an experienced and knowledgeable faster who has the full support of my doctor.  And yet, I had a moment of loss of control and collapse due to low blood pressure.  Although this is quite rare, there are lessons I have learned and changes I will make going forward.  Here are the take aways my doctor and I came up with.

Contributing Factors?  Or “What was different this time that led to a bad outcome”?

- I donated blood

- I allowed myself some non-caloric drinks that I didn’t add salt too

- I didn’t recognize the signs of low blood pressure

- I didn’t react quickly enough when feeling bad

I donated blood 24 hours into the fast.  They didn’t even want to take my blood that day when I told them I had fasted for a day.  I thought it was ok, and it appeared to as I had no problems at their office or at home.  It was 7 days later, so much later that I hadn’t put the two events together.  My doctor said that while my blood volume was probably already replaced, my red blood cells would not yet have been and thus my ability to get the same amount of oxygen was lowered.

I experimented with allowing myself a single diet soda a day, and ended up having two most days.  This wasn’t “bad” per se, but when I drink water, it’s always with salt when fasting.  So I wasn’t getting as much electrolytes as I usually do when fasting.  My ketone readings let me know the diet soda’s were not impeding the fat burning.  I might stick to water next time, or bone broth.

I’ve had light headedness when standing on fasts before and learned to drink more water and salt.  But the way I felt that morning was different.  It was like my neck couldn’t hold up my head. I was fatigued quite a bit but mostly in my neck.  I didn’t associate this with “feeling sick” or ill.  I’ve already been committed to ending a fast should I not feel well.  That’s a core principle.  I just didn’t connect how THAT felt as “feeling bad”.

I left church early and came home.  I took a couple of salt tabs, and walked the dog.  THEN I measured my blood pressure and saw it was quite low 73/44.  I am not familiar with low blood pressure as I struggle with high blood pressure.  It was then that I started drinking bone broth with salt, but going for the second cup is when the faint happened.  So, I was on the right track but just didn’t catch on soon enough.

Improvements for future fasts.

Yes, there will be future fasts.   Just as when you get injured when exercising, you don’t stop exercising – I am not going to stop fasting.  I’m going to make adjustments.  Together, my doctor and I worked out the following:

- Don’t donate blood anywhere near my fast, whether that’s just before, during or just after

- Take a half dose of my blood pressure med.  I won’t need as much when fasting and even if my pressure would run a little high for the two weeks of the fast, that’s preferable to having “too low” incident.

- Monitor blood pressure more frequently.  Particularly now that I know what low blood pressure feels like.

- I may or may not allow myself a diet soda (it made the “lonely mouth” part of fasting easier), but I will be keen to stay hydrated and always take electrolytes no matter what I’m drinking.  Particularly salt.

After the weight regain phase of the fast, I lost 10lbs in the 8 full days of the fast.  I did my body good.  5lbs of that was pure fat that was doing active harm to my health.  

I had blood work today and everything was in normal range except the BUN score – which was elevated as would be expected after a long fast and a near carnivore / ketovore diet.  My toe and knee are sore but recovering.  My thumb I will get x-rayed tomorrow.  It’s better but still quite sore and sensitive.  It may be fractured.

Why not just quit fasting?

Fasting is an amazing health practice beyond just fast loss.  Though fat loss itself is quite important.  Ever paid attention to the fine print on the pills you take detailing the KNOWN “adverse effects”?  Or the part at the end of the drug commercials where they talk real fast about anal leakage, suicidal thoughts and death?  Is this the safe route?  I don’t think so.  I’m betting on my body’s ability to heal itself with proper nutrition, exercise, strength training, sleep – and yes, fasting.

For context, 3 years ago, my A1c was 11.2, the $1000/mo Trulicity was added to the Metformin and Glipizide I'd been taking for years and I was scheduled to start insulin shots at our next meeting in three month's time. "Diabetes isn't your fault, it's genetic". THAT follows the standard of care. That is "Type 2 diabetes is a progressively degenerative disease". Instead, I read The Diabetes Code by Dr Jason Fung, went keto with intermittent fasting and exercise.
