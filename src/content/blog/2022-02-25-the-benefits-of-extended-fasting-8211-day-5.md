Fasting is an amazing health practice. Intermittent fasting helped me get off all my diabetes meds and put type 2 diabetes into remission. With my doctor’s recommendation I have begun a monthly 5 day fast. This is a series of posts to describe the benefits. I have just finished day 5.

How’d it go? – this was my third time doing this.

Was it easy? No. It was manageable. It was easier than the first two times. The hunger was, at the worst, the same as any day I’m fasting for 18 or 20 hours. No insane cravings this time. But it’s like going without your smartphone for a week plus no tv or internet. I like to eat. I want to eat. I miss eating. So it’s that level of self control.

Was I weak? Absolutely not. I did 2 strength training sessions and walking or aerobics for a half hour every day. Today just before the end of the fast I played VR table tennis with someone a good deal better than me. I was clearly quicker, had more focus and agility. No kidding. I lost, but I played my best games with the best rallies I’ve ever played. Then I did the aerobic Supernatural app workout and stopped after 3 songs. I was exhausted. But it was a glorious half hour hard workout between the table tennis and the aerobics.

Did I have brain fog? No. I was in important client meetings and interviews all week. I was as able as I ever am.

Did I get light headed? Yes, but only at the very end after the hard workout and just before I broke the fast. Last time I was very light headed on day 4. This time I drank even more water and took electrolytes.

Did my bs go too low? Nope. I had one reading in the 80s and the rest were in the 90s except two that were around 105. It is diabetes meds and insulin shots that cause hypos, not fasting.

How were my ketones? I got into ketosis on the second day, a full day earlier than before. I did the 40+ danger zone on the breath meter just like last time. I called my doctor then who had my blood work done and I was fine. He told me to get a blood ketone meter which I now use whenever the breath meter says I’m too high. No problems according to the blood meter.

GKI? What’s that? It combines your bs reading with your ketones to tell you the level of ketosis. I was in either the “good for diabetics” zone or even in the therapeutic “good for epilepsy or cancer zone”.

For more info about GKI: [https://keto-mojo.com/glucose-ketone-index-gki/](https://keto-mojo.com/glucose-ketone-index-gki/)

Weight loss? 14.5 lbs. But that is “weight” and the only thing important is fat loss. Without including exercise, the average size person loses .57lbs of fat per day of fasting. I’m larger than average and I exercised. Put it at 3 to 4 lbs of fat loss. So 10 of those lbs are coming back in the next 2 days. But that's water and the contents in your digestive system and nothing to worry about. I hit the 270s and I started this journey at 336. The 270s will be my “weight” soon enough.

These 5 day fasts are not fun. I don’t think it’s like running where you don’t like it at first and then you really come to enjoy it (or so I’ve heard). I’ve laid out over the last 4 days the health benefits of these extended fasts. It’s truly incredible. Don’t believe me, listen to cardiologist and researcher Dr Pradip Jamnadas: [https://youtu.be/RuOvn4UqznU](https://youtu.be/RuOvn4UqznU)

While not fun – these fasts are not as hard or impossible as they sound. Not if you work up to them. Cut the sugars out of your diet first to get your body able to run on fat. Start intermittent fasting. Start with 12,14 or 16 hours a day. Stop snacking. Then do a 36. And so on. It’s exactly like training your body to run a 5k or 10k and a lot less work than training to run a marathon.

Go back and read about the health benefits. Watch the video I linked.
