A bold claim that I will proceed to back this claim up.  First a little recap of my journey.  In 2020 my A1c was 11.2 and I was on Metformin, Glipizide and the $1000/mo Trulicity.  I was scheduled to start insulin.  I kicked into gear, read The Diabetes Code by Dr Jason Fung and in 3 months was off all the meds with an A1c of 6.5 and in three more months was down below even pre-diabetes at A1c of 5.6.  I remained non-diabetic for two and a half years.  Then had a bad year of compromises and falling back into old eating patterns.  Regained the 85lbs I’d lost and redeveloped Type 2 Diabetes.  6 weeks ago my A1c was 7.1.  After MANY failed attempts to get back on track, I finally got to the point I was ready to stop being diabetic once again.

I had all the tools, knowledge and experience from my years of health recovery journey.  I’m not suggesting that anyone starting from ground zero can just turn on a time.  I mean, they COULD – their bodies would respond, but the knowledge, experience, training and lessons learned aren’t there to make such an outcome likely.  But for me? I simply needed to take the actions.  It was a decision.

Look at that glucose line from yesterday.  Flat, not even a morning spike from dawn phenomenon. This is elite athlete, non-diabetic glucose.  6 weeks ago my AVERAGE glucose was 154.  Means my morning reading would have been even higher.  And my glucose would be going way up an down all day.

To be clear, this result is 2.5 days after a 7 day fast.  And I did a 9 day fast, 2 weeks of BBBE (a simple carnivore diet), 2 day fast that I ended because I’d caught a cold.  Another week of BBBE.  A 7 day fast, and back to eating BBBE.  I’d been eating for 2 days before this result.   In a day or two, my glucose will not be this good as the after glow of the fast will have worn off.  It will still be excellent, and in the non-diabetic range.

How? If you don’t give your body cabs in the diet, either by fasting or eating carnivore – your body doesn’t have to handle the carbs. I know, it sounds too simple.  Type 2 Diabetes could be seen as being allergic to carbs.  It’s not a perfect analogy but it has great truth.  And it’s entirely possible to not eat carbs – and it’s healthy. 

Fasting isn’t easy, but I’ve trained myself and built fasting muscles.  Eating carnivore isn’t easy – until you acclimate to it.  But I’d got to the point of “well, I better start taking meds again” or “do what I know to do to improve my health”.  

I hope that glucose line gives people hope.  5 years ago my average glucose was 250.  It took weeks to get my first reading under 200.  I saw spikes over 400.  I didn’t have the knowledge, tools, experience or track record to know I could transform in a matter of months.

That trend line would not survive me eating the standard American diet.  I never thought it could.  That diet makes me fat and diabetic.  But health is there for the making. The body has the tools if we stop continuously injuring it with food we can’t handle.

The a1c is a three month average so that’s as long as it takes to reflect what is already my reality.  I am not currently diabetic, again.  I am living a lifestyle that has my glucose in the normal range.  I’m wearing a cgm that lets me know.  It is a fact.  A fact brought to life with a decision to do the good things I know to do.

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs.
