“You are on the local, we are going to make every stop between here and Chicago.  The train behind us is the express”.

What?!!

OMG, I’m on the slow train.  I hop up, dash down the aisle and down the stairs from the upper car, and proceed to hop off the train just as the doors are closing.

Which I can do now.  Before the pandemic came and turned so many of us into “work from home” people, I was walking with a cane.  “Range anxiety” was what I’d feel as we would get close to my stop.  Will I have time to get off the train before the doors close?  How high will the step off the train be?

From down town it’s about a mile walk to the office.  A nice brisk walk.  I’m no longer a slow moving object in the way of the sea of humanity rushing around me.  At the end of the day I notice that my train leaves in 20 minutes, and I’m still at my desk.

Off I go with something akin to my former “Chicago stride”, quick walking across the city.  Not the fastest person, but it’s only a couple people passing me by.    And I make it with about a minute to spare.  Not only that, I have just a bit of perspiration on the brow.  In times past I’d have been sweating profusely the whole train ride back home.

I’m sure the young person I sat next too on the packed train was quite happy that there was  60lbs less of Lee.  Not that she knew how worse it could have been for her ![🙂](~/assets/posts/2022-07-06-nsv-the-great-train-escape/1f642.png)

The scale told me this morning that I was up a few pounds.   But the scale doesn’t tell the whole story.  It was a great day filled with non-scale victories.
