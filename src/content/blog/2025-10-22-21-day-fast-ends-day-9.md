My grand plans for a 21 day fast ended on day 9.  I had a queasy stomach for a couple days.  Bone broth, green tea and Tums all helped temporarily.  I had a slight tiredness in the back of my neck and then I had light headedness when I stood up.  I immediately checked my blood pressure – it was low.  Given that I feinted on day 9 last time – with more severe versions of the same symptoms – I ended the fast.  You can’t fail a fast, you merely end it earlier than planned.  

I broke the fast with bone broth, then a couple scrambled eggs.  Eating dropped my blood pressure further – this is a known phenomenon.  I kept seated with my feet up and drank salted water.  My bp was fine after two hours.  I checked through the night a couple times and had normal bp.  It’s the next day and my bp is back to MY normal, which is high (in the 130’s/140’s.

My lessons learned from last time helped me handle things very well this time.  Except for one.  I donated blood a week before the fast.  I wasn’t intending to fast at the time.  So, no more extended fasting sooner than 8 weeks of donating blood.  

Onto a version of the carnivore known as BBBE – beef, bacon, butter and eggs.  It’s simple for shopping, cooking and I like all of these foods.  Missing is my beloved cheese.  It’s an elimination diet – and dairy gives a good number of people problems.  I’m doing without for a month to determine if I have a problem.  I don’t think I do, but then, I’ve never gone without.

I’m suspecting that having few choices might make this actually easier.  One of the reasons I like fasting is that I’m doing the very best thing with a lot less effort.  No shopping, no cooking, no reading labels, no worrying if this beef is better than that etc.  When you have too much fat on your body, fasting is the perfect diet.

I’ve had my re-feed meal, and two others since (sausage and eggs both times). My blood glucose 2 hours after the last meal is 100, my blood ketones are 6.1, higher than when I was fasting.  I’m still in the highest level of therapeutic fasting and still burning body fat.  If I can keep away from the carbs, I should be able to maintain myself in ketosis.  

That’s the plan.  I didn’t just fast, I did a good amount of work on my mindset and discipline.  Hopefully it was enough to put me back on track.  I feel great about how this fast went.  There were obstacles and difficulties that I worked through and experimented with.  And I had added checking my blood pressure to my protocol and ended the fast rather than flirt with feinting again.  Some people might consider me extreme, but I take safety seriously.  I’ve just built up tolerance, skill, and experience for years.  It’s like judging a marathon runner who built up to it, verses the guy at work who signed up, never trained, and then tries to run for 26 miles.
