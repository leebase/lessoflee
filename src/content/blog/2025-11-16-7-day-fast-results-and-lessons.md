I’m currently doing a cycle of 2 weeks of BBBE (Beef, bacon, butter and eggs: a simplified carnivore diet) followed by a week long fast.  I’ll write more about that protocol later and why I came up with it.  I’m putting together the lessons, and want to do at least a few more cycles before presenting it.  It is not a beginner protocol.  Like when I was doing my Saturday push days and running sprint intervals to get my heart rate up into zone 5.  I had worked up to it.

This past week I fasted for 7 days.  Unlike any of my fasts before, I went into this fast already in ketosis.  I had done a 9 day fast followed by two weeks of BBBE.  The fast got me into deep ketosis, and the BBBE kept me always in at least a low level of ketosis.  

Also helping with the lessons is that I’m wearing a CGM again – the Abbot Freestyle Libre 3 Plus.  I also test my ketones with the Keto Mojo blood test (you have to prick your finger) and the Ketoscan breath ketone meter: no pricking your skin and much cheaper per test cost.  These are unnecessary, but I wanted to test some theories.

- I just wrote a post about one result – the “magic knees” I get on day 4 of a fast didn’t go away.  BBBE keeping me in ketosis kept my inflammation down and my osteoarthritic knees feel the best ever for a month and counting.

- Starting in ketosis did NOT get me into deep ketosis any faster.  One of the reasons I do long fasts is it takes me until late into day 3 to get into deep ketosis.   Then you want to stay there awhile to benefit from all the hard work it took to get there.  Alas, my body took the same amount of time, so I’ll still need the long fasts.

- Hunger was MUCH diminished starting in ketosis.  I was already a fat burner before the fast.  I’m not saying there wasn’t hunger, but it’s muted.  The body isn’t starving for energy, it has a direct pipeline to an incredible store of fat and I started the fast already fat adapted.  I have altered my protocol to say START with BBBE then fast a week. 

- Of course my body creates glucose when fasting – that’s not a new lesson.  I include it for those who don’t realize that your liver is plenty capable of creating what glucose you need.

- I lost 14 lbs in 7 days.  I’ll weigh again Wednesday, 4 days post fast to check the “water rebound”.  I will likely have lost 7lbs of weight, and 4lbs of fat. 

- Eating a lot of protein does NOT kick you out of ketosis.  The day I broke my fast, I ate a LOT of protein.  Some 200g (estimate, I didn’t count).  My ketones are still pegging the breath ketone meter.  This backs up what I’ve been hearing from the science:  Gluconeogenesis (converting protein to glucose) is demand driven.  Just because you eat a lot of protein, does not mean you’ll create a lot glucose if your body doesn’t need it.

- The deep ketosis of a long fast will last 3 to 4 days after the fast if you don’t eat carbs.  So even as I’m eating, I’m burning copious fat.

- Cramps and fatigue during a fast are about electrolytes – drink more water with salt.  Supplement magnesium and potassium.  Don’t drink plain water as it flushes salt.  Drink salted water.

- Salt is controlled by insulin.  When insulin is high, your kidneys retain salt in your blood, this draws water to increase volume and raises blood pressure.   When your insulin falls, your kidneys release the salt and a lot of water.  Water always follows the salt (osmosis).

- CGM’s aren’t accurate when you are fasting. My readings were a good 15pts or more too low.  This is known.  I wasn’t wearing the CGM so much for the fast but for AFTER the fast.  And getting baseline going into my Thanksgiving planned 4 day “enjoy regular food with family and then get back on track with a fast”.

No one needs to do this kind of measurements.  I like to learn and test on my own body to see if my understanding is born out.

Celebrated a belated anniversary with my wife.  I made these amazing ribeye’s and lobster tails via sous vide.   I could only eat half the steak.  My glucose stayed in the 80s and 90s even after the meal.  This is a hold over from the fast and, of course, the minimal carbs in the green beans, onions and mushrooms.  That spike on the graph?  That was a hot shower in the morning.  Thank cortisol and epinephrin for that.
