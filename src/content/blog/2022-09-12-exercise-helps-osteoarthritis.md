I’m early in developing an understanding of OA and the benefits and risks of exercise.

My ortho told me that use of my knees, even though it hurt, was vital. I wasn’t causing further damage by using my knees. But that was a conversation years ago.

Last year I began reclaiming my health from the context of diabetes. What I did for diabetes just happened to help my knees. Removing sugars from my diet greatly reduced my knee pain. I no longer need pain remedies of any kind on a daily basis.

But what about my efforts to run a 5k. Surely that’s out of bounds. But is it? Don’t take my word as gospel on OA…but here is why I run and what I’ve learned from experience.

I couldn’t run at first. I mean – like a new born dear struggling to walk…it’s like I’d forgotten how to run. The thought of the pain…my body just wouldn’t go through the motions. I had to relearn as if I was learning how to walk for the first time.

I've never been in love with running…but who wants to be disabled? I want to reclaim that ability.

Like walking, running is a load bearing exercise. Your bones get stronger when there is load on them. All the “easy on the knee” exercises are non-load bearing. And I engaged in them first to built up my leg strength.

I love walking. Yet there are benefits to higher intensity levels of exercise that walking doesn’t give. Running is free…no gym membership or pool required.

Here is what I’ve learned as I’ve progressed to being able to run intervals, even recently to sprint. Keeping in mind that I run in a similar way to using an elliptical machine. I couldn’t possibly run normally as the shock of landing on a straight leg would be excruciatingly painful. I crouch down a bit so that my leg is always bent at the knee. Less so now than at first. It’s harder to run this way as the leg muscles must carry that force.

Anyhow….

Running doesn’t hurt me. It’s tiring and somewhat exhilarating after. When I get back home I have an easier time walking up the steps after a run that the same distance as a walk. Part of that may be that the walk takes longer. I think it’s more that I walk and take the force through my knee…unlike my crouch run.

Hardest and most painful of all? Standing. Just standing for 5 or 10 minutes is a lot more painful. I can walk for an hour. Sure, I’m feeling it by the end. I’m not sure I can stand for an hour.

I go further before the pain gets too bad. I used to walk with a cane. I was so unstable that I couldn’t walk across grass and feared falling getting in and out of the shower. Now I’m running sprints and have no problems walking up and down stairs.

Something good is happening. It’s some combination of

⁃ my muscles in the leg and around the knee are getting stronger

⁃ I have lost a good amount of weight

⁃ my inflammation is lower due to dietary changes

⁃ I am learning to live with and through the pain

Here is what MAY be happening. My cartilage may be rebuilding. I can’t prove it.

⁃ my diet is no longer causing chronically high insulin levels (the root cause of inflammation and possibly OA)

⁃ my diet and supplements are giving my body the materials to rebuild

⁃ fasting and exercise have initiated autophagy and stem cells

⁃ exercise is helping the circulation in the sinoviial fluid in the knee which brings in nutrients

I’m getting better – that’s a fact.
