Type 2 diabetes is a battle against uncontrolled high blood sugar. The original battle is to get control of one’s blood sugar. It’s fairly easy to monitor with a blood glucose meter.

What happens, though, after you get your blood sugar under control? Your blood sugar readings stop telling you “the whole story”.

Consider the many years before you are pre-diabetic or diabetic. You are eating more carbs than your individual tolerance. But you can’t tell because your body deals with the sugar by elevating insulin levels. This is the beginning of the disease process – yet you can’t tell because there is no home insulin meter to show you. If you took a bs reading you’d think “that soda pop and cake didn’t bother me – look at my bs reading”.

Meanwhile the elevated insulin you couldn’t measure is harming your body. Eventually, years later, the disease state has progressed and now when you drink that coke and eat that cake…your blood sugar isn’t brought quickly back to normal. Only then, as a diabetic, is there a relationship between “I shouldn’t have eaten that” measurable with a blood glucose monitor.

I reflected on this because I had a few off plan meals this weekend. A whole large pizza. Then today a bowl of soup that had 32g carbs, and some breaded toaster oven snacks. I didn’t even look at the carbs but you know they were high.

A year post-diabetic, and my bs was fine. Seriously, both times I checked my bs at the 2 hr mark and my values were in the normal range. I have been healing. I can handle carbs a lot better than when I was diabetic.

And yet – I remind myself, that bs readings aren’t telling the whole story. I can’t see what my insulin levels went up to. I know that I haven’t healed all the way back to my starting point. If I indulge regularly, I can put myself back in the diabetes state.

Also, the sugar I’m eating isn’t just evaporating. The fact that my body can handle it, removing it from my blood in a timely manner – means that it’s being stored away as fat. I’m much more active than I used to be, so I’m burning through more energy (although that is somewhat debatable, read the book Burn by Pontzer). Do I want my activity to be used to indulge in carby food, or do I want all that hard work to continue burning the excess fat off my still obese body?
