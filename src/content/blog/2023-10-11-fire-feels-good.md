No – not THAT kind of fire. I’m talking about the kind of exercise that strains your muscles so that you feel the burn ![😁](~/assets/posts/2023-10-11-fire-feels-good/1f601.png)

Since the micro stroke in my eye, I’ve been working my intensity level up slowly and I continue to feel great. I plan to resume my Saturday Push Days this week.

I have gotten back to the high-level routines of Supernatural – the VR workout app that I find so fun and challenging. Man, I am really feeling the burn in my arms – and It is glorious.

You might want to keep in mind that I have been working on my fitness for a couple years now. Long gone are the days I was walking with a cane and barely able to do the shortest low-level routine.

I remembered a day many years ago when a friend asked me to come over and work out together. I wasn’t sick back then, but I was a couch potato. We lifted weights and maxed out every muscle group. Lifted to failure for my legs and arms, all the muscles.

OMG – the next day I felt like I had been hit by a truck. I didn’t lift again for many years…as I gained more weight and continued to be sedentary. This is not the kind of burn I’m talking about. It is, however, what so many people have in mind when someone talks about exercising intensely.

The difference between how I’m feeling after pushing my body now, and that one day years ago – is that I built up to it this time.

It’s called progressive overload. A gradual increase in the load and stress you place on your muscles that signals to your body to kick off the processes that build muscle and improve your circulatory system.

Instead of one day of intense exercise that left me so sore that I didn’t exercise again for years, I have been pushing more and going farther for more than two years. And it feels wonderful to have that burn in my muscles after a good workout.

How much more? A little more.

How much further? A bit further.

Always.

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs

p.p.s. Educate yourself and be a full participant in your health strategy
