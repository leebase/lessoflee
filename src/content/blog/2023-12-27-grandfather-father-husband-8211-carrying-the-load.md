This past Christmas celebration was wonderful.  It was our grandson’s first Christmas, my daughter’s first Christmas as a mother, and my wife and my first as grandparents.  The little man is a crawling champ, pulls himself up, he’ll be walking early.  It’s so exciting to watch him develop.   My daughter hosted all of us, including the aunties in her one bedroom apartment that is on the third floor – no elevator.   As I made multiple trips up and down those stairs carrying all the heavy boxes and food, I couldn’t help but reflect on how important my health journey as been to my family.

Not quite three years ago, I was walking with a cane and driving with a handicap sticker.  I was highly diabetic with an A1c of 11.2 and had many other related ailments.  I struggled just going up and down the stairs in my own home.  When winter arrives I would be all but a shut in as I was unable to walk safely across snow and ice.  My ability to care for my family, literally unable to “carry the load”, was in decline.   This whole weekend would have been different had I not put in the work over these last few years to turn things around.  I might not have been able to come.  Who knows if all my daughters would have been able to make it home at the same time.  

I brought the subject up with my wife, about how good it was to be able to be the one carrying the heaving things up three floors of stairs.  She told of one of our last vacations before, when I needed the cane.  She had to carry all the heavy things from the car to the cabin.  She hurt her back and was in pain the rest of the vacation.  When I can’t carry my own load, that puts more on her.    I had forgot that story and it stung to hear it.

I contribute to the family in more ways than just carrying heavy things, but this is representative of how my declining health had affected my ability to meet my family's needs.  A decline that didn’t have to happen.  Better yet, a decline I could do something about.  You will often hear my write about my health recovery journey as “reclaiming my abilities”.  This is what I mean.  I don’t care about being a good exerciser.  I don’t care about running 5ks.  I do care very much about taking care of my family.  They need me, and for a lot more than just carrying things in from the car.  

Did I have to change my diet?  Yes.  Was that easy?  No.  Was it fun?  No.  Do I still want to eat all my old foods in all the old quantities?  Yes.  Could I bring back my diabetes and my disability if I do?  Yes.

Did I have to exercise?  Yes.  Did I have to exercise in ways that are hard and uncomfortable?  Yes.  Did I have to push myself to get out there when I was tired and achy?  Yes.   Can I rest on my accomplishments?  No.  I have to keep on building my body up or it will decline.

Did I have to fast?  Maybe.  I could have just stayed sick longer, gotten better more slowly.  But seeing as I’ve been at this for two and a half years and have a lot more health to reclaim, I’m glad I have fasting in my tool box.

I really dislike the term “lifestyle changes” – as it feels dismissive.  I put in a LOT of hard work, and my body is responding because it has been designed/evolved to rebuild and repair when you stop feeding it modern “food” which are slow poisons.  You are designed to move.  And yes, you have evolved with feasting and fasting as normal.  It’s not a miracle that I’ve improved my health and no longer need a cane.  It’s actually nature.  What’s unnatural is the “Standard American Diet”.  

I hope there are people that will hear me and will have just enough faith and desire to start their own progress to recovering their health and abilities.  You just have to start.  You will learn, you will develop will power, you will become all you need to be along the way.  As long as you start.  I wish I had started sooner.  My family deserves to have me carrying the heavy things.

p.s.  Always consult your own health team for your specific needs

p.p.s. Educate yourself to fully participate in your health strategy
