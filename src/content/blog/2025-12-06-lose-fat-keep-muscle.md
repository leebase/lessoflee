Can one lose weight without losing muscle and lean tissue?  Yes.  Most people don’t know that **almost every popular way of losing weight — including plain keto and plain carnivore — still costs you muscle**.  Let me explain and be assured I'm not aiming for anyone else's toes. We all are working out our individual journey. My thoughts are not a commentary one someone else's plans.

A quick reminder: this is **my** optimization phase. I have 150+ lbs of weight to lose including deadly visceral fat, T2D to put back into remission, and a heart attack I’m trying to outrun at age 60. What works for me may be overkill for you — and that’s perfectly fine. Do what fits your body and your life.

Here’s the method to my madness:

To lose fat you must be in a catabolic (breaking-down) state.  To lose weight your body needs to burn material for energy.  We want that to be as close to “only the fat” as possible.  That’s why **almost every weight-loss method also eats some of your muscle** — unless you deliberately stop it. To lose weight you have to take in less energy than you use.  This doesn't equate to "it's all about the calories" – hormones, insulin, it all matters.  But – at the end of the day, your body must consume your fat, and the same deficit that coaxes your body to do so, also has your body break down lean tissue and muscle.

Muscle loss during fat loss is **not** inevitable. It is **well documented** that you can avoid it. The science is well established:

• 30+ years of clinical nutrition research  

• Every serious body-recomposition study  

• Bariatric-surgery guidelines  

• NASA bed-rest studies  

• Stuart Phillips, Layne Norton, Eric Helms, Brad Schoenfeld, etc.  

• The entire protein-forward movement

The single most powerful lever you control? **Protein intake.**

The evidence-based sweet spot for preserving (or even gaining) muscle while aggressively losing fat:

**≥ 1.6 g protein per kg of body weight  

or roughly 1 g protein per pound of your estimated lean body mass.**

For me at ~310 lbs with ~170 lbs lean mass → **170–190 g protein per day.**

Below that threshold in a big deficit and your body starts cannibalizing muscle along with fat.  Hit (or slightly exceed) it and the body says, “Cool, I’ve got plenty of amino acids — I’ll just burn the fat.”

Meta-analysis (105 studies, 2022) confirming this exact range for optimal muscle retention during energy deficit + resistance training:  

[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8978023/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8978023/)

That’s why I went from “eat BBBE till stuffed” to **protein-forward carnivore**:   delicious food, just deliberately hitting 170–190 g protein and letting my own body fat supply the rest of the calories. BTW, that's my own name.  There may be a published protocol with a more marketing friendly name. I'm simply taking BBBE, the wonderfully simple and powerful protocol that Dr Berry has taught me – and boosted the protein and stopped aiming for 80% fat. Not because fat is bad or causes heart disease. This is not a low fat diet. It's adequate fat (for the body and to make the food palatable) and protein emphasized per the desire to keep the muscle but lose the fat.

I have found a predicted but unexpected bonus. The satiety is off the wall. I'm having to remind myself to eat to hit my protein target. For the first time I can easily follow Dr Fung's "don't snack" advice. For ME – this was unexpected though folks like Ted Naiman have been preaching it for years. Uggh, I had to say his name and give him credit but I do NOT like his diet nor do I think he understands the insulin mechanism correctly. I like having satiety as the result of giving your body what it needs and then it shuts off hunger verses seeing "satiety as a lever" leaving to ridiculous (my judgment) diet suggestions.

I know this – I am in a deficit – my keto mouth (metallic taste) tells me I'm burning fat. My CGM (continuous glucose monitor) shows a flat line around 85 to 95 — a HUGE improvement from 2 months ago when my A1c was 7.1. You can't even tell when my meals are by looking at the glucose graph.  I took thanksgiving in stride as was normal only 4 days after the carb fest eating this way.  2 days after surgery and and I'm back to this graph.  My FACE tells me I'm on the right track.  My clothes are beginning to tell me the same story. 

Yes, I'm paying attention to macros and calories, weighing my food, tracking. All things our ancestors never had to do.  I won't have to either once I dial in on what this way of eating looks like. I'm not drinking protein shakes, egg whites, or boiled potatoes.  I am adding chicken, turkey, ham, tuna and salmon to my beloved fatty beef and eggs.
