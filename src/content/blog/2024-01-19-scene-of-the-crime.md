Welcome to the &#8216;Scene of the Crime,' a place where the iron and steel of my bench and weights orchestrate a relentless assault on my unsuspecting muscle fibers. Each lift, a calculated strike; each rep, a deep incision into the very core of my muscular structure. Here, the dumbbells lie in wait, innocent to the untrained eye, but accomplices in the ruthless scheme of tearing down to rebuild. Sweat on the bench, the echoes of exerted grunts, and weights clanking in the aftermath – all telltale signs of the &#8216;muscle massacre' that has just transpired. A brutal plot unfolds set by set, where strength is both the villain and the hero, leaving behind a battlefield strewn with the echoes of hard-won exertion.

lol! I love ChatGPT which helped me with the metaphor. A little drama to highlight that lifting weights is the tearing down process. This is a message to your body to build back stronger. This rebuilding happens AFTER you lift. Thus the need to have recovery days and quality sleep in between lifting.

I pushed harder than I intended to with my aerobics yesterday. I’m learning as I go and paying attention to how I feel and respond. I have to remind myself that the rest is equally as important. Feeding yourself quality protein is also vital if you want to build muscle.

And why build muscle? Almost nothing about muscle building from my high school years apply. It’s about ability to walk, to carry heavy things in from the car. It’s about going into my 60s strong and remaining so the rest of my days. Strength is needed for independant living. Muscles ramp up your metabolism making fat loss easier and healing insulin resistance.

I don’t want type 2 diabetes to ever return. I am not currently diabetic but I want to move further away.

I want to continue healing my arthritic knees.

And so I committed a crime against my muscles tonight. And now I rest so that they come back stronger.

That’s how I did my body good today. Let me hear how you did your body good. Did you…do more and farther?

How much more? A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.
