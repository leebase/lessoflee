I no longer need a cane.  I walk an hour or more every day.  I can run a 5k (I may need to work back up to this).  I don’t take any pain meds, not even natural ones.  I’m not free of OA, just living life with far less pain and more ability. 

First a quick history about my OA:  I walked with a cane, drove with handicap sticker.  Used the shopping cart like a walker and still struggled to buy groceries.  Struggled with stairs in my own home, struggled to cross Wacker Drive in Chicago before the light changed.  Became essentially a shut in during the snowy, icy Chicago winters.  My ortho told me I had OA and wanted to replace my knees.  I had pain too, not just disability.  I tried every non-pharma remedy and anti-inflammatory tablet, lotion and potion.  Some helped.  None all that well.  Diclofenca was a wonder drug – but it’s a drug and destroys the kidneys. 

I am working on two health conditions simultaneously:  Type 2 Diabetes and Osteoarthritis.  What I accidentally learned in my health journey is that they have the same root cause – insulin dysregulation brought on by excessive carbohydrates in the diet.  

I was also morbidly obese and highly diabetic.  My A1c was 11.2 on three meds and I was scheduled to go on insulin.  I weighed 335 lbs.  My story is common.  What happened next was not.

Everything I did to reverse my type 2 diabetes – helped my OA.  Dropping the sugars out of my diet for my diabetes lowered my knee pain.  I was shocked.  I didn’t expect it.  The exercise I started doing for my metabolic syndrome (the root cause that leads to T2D) – started strengthening my legs.  THAT led to me walking better, handling stairs better.  The light dawned – I wasn’t just arthritic, but the pain made me move less and that made me weak.  My disability was largely muscle weakness that greatly improved with exercise.

Did I lose weight?  Yes, but the pain went down well before I lost much weight.  The ability to walk was helped by the reduction in pain but MOSTLY by building back my muscle strength.  People were saying to me “you need to protect your knees” – but by putting load on my knees – I was protecting them.  I was building them and all the supporting muscles and ligaments.  “Motion is lotion” is one phrase that encapsulates this.  Moving your knees, putting load on them, moves the synovial fluid which helps distribute the nutrients.  I didn’t know this at the time, I just knew that what I was doing for T2D was reducing my OA pain and bringing back ability to walk and even run (came a couple years later through much effort).

By now I’m no longer using the cane at all.  I still have OA pain but it’s much less.  I walk just fine in ice and snow – but I am careful.  A few flights of stairs are no longer obstacles.  But there is more to go.

I started extended fasting – fasts longer than 2 days.  I did this for my metabolic syndrome and to lose fat.  On day 4 of a fast, I got “magic knees”.  No pain at all. WOW – was this unexpected and amazing.  Once I resume eating, the magic knees went back to my new normal.  Pain but at a level I didn’t need pain meds for.  I was happy.

I did a year of once a month 5 day fasts.  And every time I got magic knees on day 4.  How could this be?  It can’t be that my cartilage regrew and fixed itself.  I’m sure I do have some cartilage wear, that’s how the Ortho diagnosed me as having OA and wanted to replace my knees.  I’m not claiming my cartilage has fixed at all.  Probably some, but without x-rays, I don’t know.  What I DO know, is that I don’t need my knees replaced.  I have so much ability back, and the pain is nowhere near what it used to be.  This “magic knees” was something else.  Near complete absence of pain.

At this point – keeping in mind that I didn’t do ANY of this FOR my knees.  They were improving while I was working on metabolic syndrome and fat loss.  Clearly – the root cause had to be related.  That’s why working on the one made vast improvements in the other.  As for my “magic knees” – I continued to think of them as a feature of long fasts.

Well, I had a bad year – lost my eating discipline, regained all my weight and redeveloped T2D.  Amazingly, I did not lose my improved knees.  I still walk fine, no cane, and pain levels that don’t need lotions and potions.  Then I kicked into gear with a long fast of 9 days.  Like clockwork on day 4, I got my magic knees.  But I made a change.  After the fast, I ate carnivore – specifically BBBE (beef, bacon, butter and eggs).  I did this to keep the fat loss going.  I wanted to stay in ketosis.  I am still highly insulin resistant such that even “keto diet carbs” are too much for me to stay in ketosis. 

And what do you know – I KEPT my magic knees.  The only thing that changes my knees on day 4 of a fast isn’t cartilage or weight — it’s insulin and the inflammation it drives.  There are surely mechanical issues as well, but the pain is inflammation.  Cutting the sugars out dropped the pain as it dropped the inflammation.  Extended fasting dropped inflammation to rock bottom freeing me from pain.  Eating carnivore has kept me in ketosis and is keeping me pain free.

That is how I know that fasting is directly pertinent to any discussion of osteoarthritis.  I don’t know how many people will respond as my body has.  I have since done research and found that my experience matches the science.

Here is the science: 

Insulin Dysregulation as a Shared Root Cause for T2D and OA

- Insulin resistant mechanisms in OA mirroring T2D: A review linking IR to cartilage breakdown and synovial inflammation, suggesting it's a key driver.[PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261331/)

- IR indices strongly tied to knee OA prevalence: 2025 study showing triglyceride-glucose index (a simple IR marker) predicts OA risk, supporting metabolic interventions.[Nature article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91526-7)

- IR "releasing brakes" on joint inflammation: Explains how T2D-related IR worsens OA via immune pathways in the synovium.[PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5371020/)

- Metabolic syndrome aggravating OA cartilage damage: Covers how IR, obesity, and hyperglycemia create a pro-inflammatory joint environment.[ScienceDirect study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2665913125000500)

Low-Carb/Keto/Carnivore Diets Reducing OA Pain and inflammation

- Low-carb diet outperforms low-fat for knee OA pain relief: 2019 clinical trial showing reduced pain intensity and self-reported symptoms in weeks.[UAB study](https://www.uab.edu/news/health-medicine/study-low-carb-diet-provides-relief-from-knee-osteoarthritis)

- Keto diet protects cartilage by blocking inflammasomes: Rat OA model where KD reduced inflammation and preserved joint structure via NLRP3 inhibition.[Arthritis Research & Therapy](https://arthritis-research.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13075-022-02802-0)

- Keto's anti-inflammatory effects on arthritis and CVD: Overview of how beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) from ketosis dials down systemic inflammation, relevant to OA.[PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8712653/)

- Carnivore success story for joint pain: Anecdote mirroring yours—keto to carnivore eliminated arthritis pain and boosted mobility.[Carnivore Diet testimonial](https://carnivore.diet/jim-improved-weight-loss-treated-arthritis-carnivore-diet/)

- Caveat note: A 2025 mouse study found keto worsened OA pain despite weight loss (possibly due to microbiome shifts)—human trials are mixed, so worth mentioning for nuance.[OMRF findings](https://omrf.org/2025/02/27/keto-diet-may-worsen-osteoarthritis-omrf-study-finds/)

Extended Fasting for "Magic Knees" and inflammation Drop

- Prolonged fasting in multimodal OA therapy: Exploratory study of 7–21 day fasts reducing knee/hip pain and improving function in patients.[PMC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10301411/)

- Short-term fasting as OA adjunct: 2025 review on how 3–5 day fasts quickly lower inflammation markers, complementing your monthly routine.[Frontiers in Nutrition](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1604872/full)

- IF for metabolic knee OA: Discusses extended fasts activating stress resistance and ketosis for pain relief, especially in insulin-resistant cases.[PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7920092/)

- Fasting triggers mitophagy in chondrocytes: 2025 study linking fasting to mitochondrial cleanup in joint cells, slowing OA progression.[Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07541-x)

- Fasting study on knee/hip OA: Clinical data showing pain scores drop 30–50% after 10-day fasts, with sustained benefits.[Spa Dreams summary](https://www.spadreams.com/studies/fasting/study-on-the-effect-of-fasting-for-knee-and-hip-arthritis/)

Ketones as Anti-Inflammatory Heroes

- BHB alleviates cartilage aging and inflammation: Shows ketones inhibiting pro-inflammatory pathways in OA cells.[Experimental Gerontology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S053155652300061X)

- Keto reduces inflammatory pain in OA models: 2021 study on how ketosis lowers allodynia (hypersensitivity) via immune modulation.[Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-80727-x)

- Very low-calorie keto for OA symptoms: 2024 trial noting BHB's role in cutting inflammation during weight loss.[Nutrients journal](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/16/19/3236)

- Keto as OA preventative via anti-inflammation: Thesis overview of ketones reducing ROS and cytokines in joints.[SEU scholar](https://firescholars.seu.edu/honors/144/)

I didn’t make this progress in a day.  The short form of what to do:

- Keto diet – real keto, not keto treats.  Check your ketones, it’s not a keto diet for you if you aren’t in ketosis

- Fasting – start with intermittent fasting, work to longer fasts (if you have fat to lose).

- Carnivore – at least as an elimination diet.  30 days of string carnivore (I’m partial to the simple Beef, Bacon, Butter and Eggs version).  If you are in ketosis before starting (from keto), this should let you know if you have foods you are eating that are inflaming you.  Or if it’s just needing to be in ketosis. 

- Exercise – motion is lotion is true.  You have to use your joints, put load on them.   Start small and work up

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs.
