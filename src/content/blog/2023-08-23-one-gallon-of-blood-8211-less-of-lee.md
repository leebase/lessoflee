This past time I gave blood, I completed my first gallon. I saw on the wall others who had given 13 gallons, so I have a ways to go :). Giving blood saves lives. If you can, I urge you to donate blood. Don’t just assume you can’t – look into your own country’s policies.

I usually make some provocative statement: Lower your A1c instantly and lose weight by giving blood. It’s true and absurd at the same time. Seriously, it is true – look it up. But yes, giving blood doesn’t make your diabetes any better.

I have fun making this point because of how passionately people will respond to me about the absurdity of advocating donating blood to lower A1c. Why fun? Because it helps establish a hugely important point – not everything that lowers your A1c is actually helping your diabetes. Like taking meds. I am an advocate for doing the hard work to bring healing. So many people get up in arms with my claims that MANY people can overcome Type 2 Diabetes. (Read The Diabetes Code by Dr Jason Fung to hear an expert doctor teach on how to reverse this disease).

What!? Taking medications and insulin shots doesn’t help with Type 2 Diabetes? Well, no. They help lower your blood sugar and that’s a very good thing. But it doesn’t make you better. It’s symptom management, not healing and recovery.

But there is healing and recovery to be had. A very low carb diet/keto/carnivore — fasting — exercise — stress management — losing weight: all of these things can help you heal from Type 2 Diabetes. You can regain insulin sensitivity, resume producing a healthy amount of insulin – to such an extent that you won’t need medications. Not only does that reduce your blood sugar, but it makes you healthy in other ways…overcoming metabolic syndrome/insulin resistance syndrome.

If you just take the meds, your disease will progress. That’s what it means when they say: “Type 2 Diabetes is a progressively degenerative disease”. You take some meds, then more meds, then more meds with a little extra insulin, and then more and more as you continue to suffer the ravages of metabolic syndrome.

So, give blood to save lives. Eat in a way that doesn’t spike your insulin and blood sugar, exercise, sleep and manage your stress to save your own life.

ps: Following Dr Fung’s (and others) teachings I got off all my meds and went from an A1c of 11.2 to 5.2 (normal) and I’ve been non-diabetic for two years and counting. As always, consult your own health care team.
