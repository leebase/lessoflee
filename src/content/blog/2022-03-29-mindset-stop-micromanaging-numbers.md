I see this a lot. New diabetics…or newly energized to do something about it diabetics…obsessing over every blood sugar reading. The problem isn’t “being concerned”, the problem is the focus is wrong which leads to wrong analysis which leads to actions being wrong. We end up trying to manage a symptom instead of working on healing the real problem.

First…the body is simply way more complicated than any reading will tell you. Perhaps a diversion to “weight” will make this clear. It’s very easy to weigh yourself. It’s very difficult to come to the right conclusion on what any particular weight measurement is telling you. Even a month of measurements doesn’t tell the whole story.

Last month I gained weight. I’m trying to lose weight, did I do wrong? There is a built in assumption that my weight should have gone down and that something I “did wrong” caused it. Then comes the “wrong analysis”. Maybe I fasted too much. Maybe “starvation mode” caused me to gain weight when not eating. That’s nonsense, but when you are making analysis based on a single number, in this case weight, you can easily fall prey to such faulty thinking.

Another piece of information is that I had been strength training. Doing so causes muscle growth, and bones to become more dense. Another data point is that I lost 1.5” from my hips and 1” from my belly in that same month. The weight measure wasn’t telling the whole story.

Back to our blood sugar reading. It is not the whole story. Exercise can raise your bs. Stress can raise your bs. Fighting off illness can raise your bs. Eating improperly can raise your bs. If you start trying to do things like “eat peanut butter before going to bed to get a better morning bs reading” – you are doing wrong things inspired by micromanaging the symptom instead of working on healing the real problem.

The real problem is insulin resistance. High bs readings are the symptom. Work on healing the insulin resistance and the bs readings will reflect the healing…in time. Eating sugar before going to sleep is going to raise your insulin levels and shorten the time you get each night to be in a low insulin state. That low insulin state helps heal the insulin resistance. You may (or may not) get a lower morning bs reading, but you haven’t helped yourself.

You heal your insulin resistance in the following ways:

Cut way back on carbs

Lose weight (fat weight)

Strength train

Get better sleep

Reduce stress

Go longer between eating giving your insulin levels a chance to fall

I’m dealing with this tendency myself right now. I got my lipid panel done and I’m obsessing over the numbers. Some improved, some got worse. What did I do wrong? What do I have to do to “make my numbers better”. That’s the wrong mindset. What do I need to do to improve my heart health? That’s what I need to focus on and trust the numbers to improve in time.
