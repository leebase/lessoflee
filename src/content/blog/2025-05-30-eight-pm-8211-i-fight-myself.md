It’s 8pm and I’ve been hearing the “let’s eats” for about an hour.  Only a small part of it is hunger as I finish the first 24 hours of the fast.  This is the work of fasting, the battle – the discipline.   All by itself, the discipline of telling yourself No is valuable.   Almost every good thing in life comes from being able to say No when you want to say yes.  Or to say yes, keep going – when you want to quit.

Hand to hand combat with my own brain is going on.  It’s the only role I have.  My body is performing all manner of work on my behalf.  Insulin is dropping allowing insulin resistance to heal.   My liver is just about through passing out the sugar it stored to keep me running while the fat burning processes start waking up.  I don’t have to lift a finger – all these processes are automatic, evolved over millennia.  I just have to prevent myself from throwing a wrench in the gears – or food into my stomach.

Fasting is to the body like sleep is to the brain.  Your body needs time away from digestion to work on cleanup and repair.

My mind, though, is accustomed to the pleasure and rhythm of eating.  It is the denial of pleasure that is the struggle.   It comes in waves – resist for a short time and the urge passes.

I want to feel the benefits of lower inflammation.

I want the power that comes with discipline.

I want to shed the excess fat.

I want to wear normal sized clothes.

I want to be a former diabetic again.

I want to be able to play with my grandkids.

I want to be able to take care of my wife.

I want to live without pain.

I can eat and be satisfied.

I can “not eat” and be satisfied.

But the “I want”’s are served by saying no – for now.

It’s morning the next day.  I made it through.  Went to bed early and had a terrific night’s sleep.  My foot pain is reduced this morning.  Could be a placebo effect.  It’s a lot easier to fast when I’m working – but there will be battles come the night. 

You can’t fail a fast – you merely end earlier than planned.  BUT – I have much to gain by continuing.

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs.

p.p.s. Educate yourself to fully participate in your health strategy.

p.p.p.s. It’s glucose your liver stores in the form of glycogen, not “sugar”
