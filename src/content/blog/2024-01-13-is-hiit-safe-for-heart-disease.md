Like many of us who have or had Type 2 Diabetes, I also have heart disease. My CAC score is concerningly high. My doctor said that there wasn’t anything to do but slow down the progression. I don’t know if that’s true, but lots of places I’ve read said the same thing and I’m still looking for a definitive “do xyz to reverse the calcium in your arteries”.

Meanwhile, everything I can do to help my heart health, I’m doing. One of those things is HIiT, High Intensity Interval Training. One of our forum's “you are getting healthy the wrong way police” has chimed in with dire warnings for me and you and everyone else. It’s bad, BAD she tells us. Nobody should exercise to 95% of max heart rate. It’s completely dangerous.

Ok. Even though I talked to my own doctor who knows all about my heart health and got the ok….and even though I’ve read a lot about the health benefits. We are going to play “my citation is better than your citation” as none of us are heart researchers.

Here is a paper by the Journal of the American Heart Association. I don’t implicitly trust these official organization, but we are playing “my citation is better than yours”.

It’s a paper that reviewed many studies specifically about the use of HIIT FOR THOSE WITH HEART DISEASE. The very type of group one would think “well, obviously, none of these people should stress their heart”.

And it is true that nothing is good for everybody – but we are debating that NOBODY should. And here we find that even heart disease patients benefit safely from HIIT.

Journal of the American Heart Association

High‐Intensity Interval Training for Patients With Cardiovascular Disease—Is It Safe? A Systematic Review

[https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/JAHA.118.009305](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/JAHA.118.009305)

“Conclusions

HIIT has shown a relatively low rate of major adverse cardiovascular events for patients with coronary artery disease or heart failure when applied within CR settings.”

Defining HIIT, they chose definitions with GREATER THAN or equal to 85%

“HIIT programs were limited to interval durations of up to 4 minutes, with the intensity classified as being ≥85% of heart rate (HR) peak or a surrogate physiological index (namely, ≥80% peak aerobic capacity [VO2peak] or a rating of perceived exertion ≥15).52 MICT programs included continuous aerobic exercise of intensity 60% to 75% of HR peak (or 50%–65% VO2peak; 12–15 ratings of perceived exertion). Studies were included if adjunct exercise was applied (eg, supplemental resistance training), but it needed to be applied equally for both groups if the study involved an MICT comparison group rather than usual care.”

And if you look in the table: Table 2 Exercise Intervention Characteristics and AE Data

You will see that MANY of the studies used 90-95% max heart rate as their definition of HIIT.

Does this prove anything? No. There will never be definitive proof and someone is always going to find an opposite view from a different authority.

Do talk to your own doctor before engaging in any new health improvement program. But ALSO educate yourself. Then place your bets because no matter what you choose the “Your Getting Healthy the Wrong Way” self appointed police are going to chime in.
