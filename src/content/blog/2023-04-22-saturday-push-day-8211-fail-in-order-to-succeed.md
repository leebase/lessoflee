It’s Saturday and time to push the limits, to make my world just a little bit bigger. I have my first actual 5k race coming up next month, so I decided to work on my speed. Speed being a relative term – I am about as fast as a snail or perhaps a middle-aged turtle ![🙂](~/assets/posts/2023-04-22-saturday-push-day-8211-fail-in-order-to-succeed/1f642.png)

I actually felt like going out for a run this morning. However, as soon as I had the thought to strive to increase my pace, my inner voice piped up, “What if by trying to run faster, you fail to complete the run without walking?”. I have these inner dialogues all the time. I’m quite chatty with my own self. “So what if I do walk?”, I tell me. I try to do things all the time and fail. And fail. And fail again until I eventually succeed. And then fail some more until one day what used to be my limit is now completely within my norm.

I did make it without walking and set a personal best time of 47 minutes. Yep, my “run” is no record-breaking pace. Two years ago, I was walking with a cane due to osteoarthritis and completely unable to run at all – at any pace. So I’ll take it.

As my app told me I’d reached the 4k mark, I picked up my pace. Or I tried to. Then with half a kilometer to go, it was time to kick to the finish. Only I couldn’t. I failed to make the last half kilometer a race to the finish. Instead, I was just able to keep going as my stomach cramped up a bit. I wanted to stop, but I did make it to the end without walking.

Did I fail? Yes. Did I succeed? Yes.

Before you leave with my trademark phrase, I encourage you to take a moment and reflect on your own personal goals and challenges. Ask yourself, what areas of your life could benefit from pushing your boundaries? It could be in your fitness, career, relationships, or any other aspect of your life. Embrace the possibility of failure, and remember that it is through these small, incremental challenges that we grow, learn, and ultimately, succeed.

How did I go from walking with a cane to running a 5k? By doing more and going farther.

How much more? A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.
