What can you do good for your body that takes only 4 minutes? Tabata – a form of high intensity interval training.

It’s astounding to reflect that a couple years ago I needed a cane to walk around and now I’m able to run sprints. Real, honest to goodness sprints. I didn’t start with sprints, of course.

HIIT (High Intensity Interval Training) has benefits and is worth adding to your health regime. It’s time efficient and has good health effects long after the workout. Pretty much any form of exercise that gets your heart rate up to 95% or so can be used. Before I got to where I could sprint, I was able to do HIIT with a recumbent cross trainer machine at the health club. You sit and push your legs and arms at the same time.

Walking is a wonderful health practice. But walking wasn’t going to get me to where I wanted/needed to be. I walk every day at least a half hour. I LOVE my walks. They are as much for my mental health as for my body. However, you really do need to push yourself if you want to cause your body to adapt. When I started, 15 minutes of walking with my cane around the neighborhood WAS pushing myself. Then 30 minutes. Then 45. I also started doing squats and lunges (with my Supernatural VR workout). I was so weak, I could barely do it. I even fell over a couple of times.

If you are injured, you need to rest to heal. If you are weak you need to push your body to make it stronger. Not using your knees “because I have arthritis” wasn’t going to heal my arthritis. I didn’t start with jumping jacks or sprinting. But I did walk in pain. I can’t tell you where that fine line is between “the pain that brings healing” and “pain that is injury”. But know that there is a difference.

Managing my arthritis pain used to be about “what natural pill or cream can I use instead of diclofenac or other NSAIDs like ibuprofen”. I rarely need or take anything for pain any more. I’m not pain free, but I have learned to build my body back up with proper nutrition and exercise and not merely reach for a pain reliever.

“McKenzie Method -Treat Your Own Knee” and “Built from Broken” by Scott Hogan are two books on the subject.

You don’t need to do exactly what I do or have done. Find your own way, push your own body. Your more healthy and more able future “you” will be so glad you did.

I found my own way when working on my type 2 diabetes, which is now in remission/reversed/healed. Starting with where I was and then making progress. I’ve gone from not being able to walk across my own lawn to running sprints. I got there by doing more and going farther.

How much more? I little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.

PS: As always, consult your own doctor.
