You’ll see me write “veggies optional” in my food posts of late.  It represents my current take on the “carnivore diet” for my own uses.   Dr Ken Berry would describe it as the Proper Human Diet, others might call it Keto-vore.  It’s definitely forbidden from someone with a strict carnivore interpretation.  It is definitely a subset of a keto diet as “keto” is not a food but a body process where you are burning fat for fuel instead of carbohydrates.  A really good thing for diabetics.   Any talk of food and diet is sure to cause controversy, so get your popcorn ready .

What’s all the fuss…how can anybody think veggies are unhealthy for you?  Whole books have been written on the topic (I recommend The Carnivore Diet by Dr Shawn Baker).  Let us consider two clear examples:  peanuts and gluten.  Everybody knows someone who has an peanut allergy or is gluten intolerant.    Peanuts and wheat are plants.  I’m not saying NO ONE should eat these plants, but it’s clearly demonstrated that SOME people very much have a problem with SOME plants.

What you might not know is that a LOT of people have problems with plants without ever understanding that they do.  Lectins and  oxalates are just two such chemicals plants have that can cause problems in humans.  There are many others.  This shouldn’t come as too much of a surprise as you can’t just go out in the forest and eat every plant that you see.  Only SOME plants are edible by humans, most will make you sick or kill you.

But not veggies – right?  Veggies are what we all should be eating more of.  Everyone knows that.  Well, turns out that isn’t true.  Vegetables also have defense chemicals that cause problems for SOME people.  How do you know if you are one such person?   That’s the value of going carnivore as an elimination diet.  Not necessarily forever, but just long enough to see if you are one of those folks who would greatly benefit.  All diabetics would benefit from the “no carb” aspect of such a diet.

I’m not where some carnivore advocates are who take the position that all plants are bad for everyone.  We can argue all day about what our ancestors ate and which tribe of modern primitives eat no plants.  I think it’s fairly easily established that there are examples of very healthy people who are vegans, carnivores, paleo, Mediterranean diet, high protein keto, high fat keto and pretty much anything BUT the standard American diet.   What people stumble on, is the equal reality is that there are people who DON’T do well on every form of diet.  Plenty of vegans are obese or have many health problems that go away when they reintroduce animal foods.  Some people are salt sensitive or even allergic to beef.  Some folks can eat bread all day long and never become diabetic.

So – diet wars aside – it’s about finding out how your own body responds to food.   I did a month of strict carnivore last June as an attempt to break a five month stall.  It was an amazing success….AND…it just so happened that I FINALLY saw my triglycerides fall from the stratosphere to normal and my HDL rise to normal.  My ratio went from 9 to 2.1 — that ratio has been far better at predicting heart disease than elevated LDL ever has.  You want your triglyceride/hdl ratio to be below 2.0.

I didn’t have any improvement that I could note in terms of autoimmune diseases.  I can’t attribute anything about that month to my arthritic knees.   Certainly didn’t make them worse, but no “revolutionary improvement”.  At least not in the 30 days I gave.  Others have seen dramatic improvements in their own health.

Having read a couple of books on the topic and watched countless youtube videos by the likes of Dr Ken Berry, Dr Chaffe, Dr Paul Saladino, Dr Shawn Baker (to name a few)…I have come to understand just how nutritious meat, eggs and fish are.  Not simply proteins and fats.  All the vitamins you need.  

So now when you see my meals, you may note the presence OR the absence of veggies.  I have not bought into “plants are trying to kill you” of so many carnivore enthusiasts.  But I also no longer see plants as being the sole source of the nutrients my body needs.  I like some plants.  I’ll continue to eat some.  I no longer any plants “because you need to eat your veggies”.  

For me, veggies are optional.   Fruit has been optional ever since I learned I was a type 2 diabetic.  But that’s a different post.
