This week’s thought is that I’m not “running a race” as much as I’m “weightlifting for my heart”. The heart, after all is a muscle and like all muscles, you must put it under stress to kick off an adaptive response that makes the muscle stronger, better and more healthy.

It’s a four day holiday weekend and I’ll be traveling on Saturday, so my Push Day came a day early. So thankful that spring is in full glory with temps in the 70’sf/21c. Off I went to run a 5k, and I came in 10 minutes earlier than the race last week. Mind you, I came in the bottom 10% of participants, I’m no speed demon.

I’m not in love with running and I’m under no delusion that I’ll win races. Running is simply a very easy way to get my heart rate up. Getting your heart rate up is “weight lifting” for your heart muscle. There are many ways to achieve this. How you get your heart rate up isn’t important. That you do, is. So for me, nothing is as easy or effective for amping up my heart rate than running, no matter than my running is quite slow.

I made reference that last week I ran for an hour with my heart right below or above the “red line” for an hour. The “red line” was a reference to running in Zone 5. Zone 5, often colored red in a chart, is not a danger zone. It’s simply the highest, maximum effort. Zone 4 is 80-90% of your maximum heart rate and zone 5, the “red zone”, is 90-100%. Today, my run was 15 minutes in zone 5 and 21 minutes in zone 4. It is a push day, after all. As my fitness improves, the same distance and speed will be achieved with a much lower heart rate. Just like a weight that is hard to lift today, can become easy over time. And every day is not a push day as rest and recovery are equally vital.

Why push so hard? Well, it’s really the “same hard” as when I was walking around the neighborhood with my cane. I don’t mean to say that my heart rate from walking with a cane was high, but that it was hard for me to do. By doing that which was hard, I was able to do a little more. Fast forward 2 years, and I’m running quite slowly but able to push my heart rate up. I’m not working harder, I’m just working the same hard. If I was still JUST walking 15 minutes around the neighborhood, THAT would not be hard. So I’m working the same hard, and am able to do more with it because my body is adapting due to me pushing it beyond my comfort zone.

Is it safe? That’s a great question. There is nothing inherently dangerous about getting your heart rate up to 100% effort. It’s not wise to go from sedentary to high intensity exercise in a day. And if you have major heart problems, it’s certainly advisable to talk to your doctor. The “a little more, a bit farther” encapsulates this method of safely strengthening your muscles, improving your health and reclaiming your life. Not one big leap, but small incremental, yet persistent, effort over time that improves what you can do, what your body can take.

What is surely NOT safe? Taking it easy on yourself. You want your heart to continue to decline, your muscles continue to weaken, your balance to leave you? Then just don’t put any stress on your heart or muscles. Again, always talk to your doctor as no advice is universally fitting for everybody at every stage of their life and health.

I highly recommend “muscle building” your heart. I can testify to this – the future you will thank you for the ways your push yourself today. Whether it’s reclaiming health and abilities as I’m doing or slowing down aging, you will be more happy, more alive, feel better if you do more and go farther.

How much more? A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.
