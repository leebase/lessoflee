Yes, I said it.  I’m on the road to cease being diabetic – for the second time.  The fact that it’s the second time should put a pause for those experiencing clinically violent knee-jerkism over the concept of being free of Type 2 Diabetes.  “Once a diabetic, always a diabetic” goes the old, incorrect saying.

4 years ago my A1c was 11.2.  I was on 3 meds including the $1000/mo Trulicity and scheduled to go on insulin.  I read The Diabetes Code by Dr Jason Fung, kicked in gear and in 3 months I was off all the meds.  Three months after that, I was at a normal A1c of 5.4 – I stayed non-diabetic for two and a half years

I brought Type 2 Diabetes back the same way I developed it in the first place.  I ate foods that make me fat and diabetic – the Standard American Diet.  You aren’t always a diabetic, but there’s nothing magical about healing.  If you heal from a broken leg you could break it again.  But while it’s not broken, you don’t go around saying “once a cripple, always a cripple”.

How do I KNOW for a fact that I’m about to become non-diabetic again?  Because there are two phases to healing from this disease.  The first phase is all but guaranteed to happen, and in no longer than three months.  Simply stop eating carbs.  It’s like having a deadly peanut allergy.  You can be completely free of dying by simply not eating peanuts.  Same with Type 2 diabetes.  If you don’t eat carbs, you aren’t going to have elevated blood glucose.  I fasted for 9 days, and my glucose readings where in the mid eighties, and I’ve been eating carnivore ever since.  My last A1c was 7.1, which is an average blood glucose of 154.  My morning readings right now are around 107.  That’s the high with the dawn phenomenon.    

Why three months?  You completely replace your blood cells about every three months.  If you fasted for three months, you absolutely would be guaranteed to have an A1c that is non-diabetic.  If you eat carnivore for three months, you are ALMOST equally guaranteed of the same thing for the same reason.  You aren’t flooding your system with glucose such that as you get new blood cells, they don’t get glycated.

This is phase one and does not yet represent healing.  If you eat zero carbs, you aren’t going to have Type 2 Diabetes level blood glucose.  You’d have to TRY to eat so much protein to have your body create enough glucose to be a diabetic.  I’m not sure even then.  But short of TRYING to remain diabetic, ceasing eating the carbs will do the trick.

Along with ceasing to glycate your red blood cells due to excess glucose in your blood – eating carnivore and fasting lowers your insulin levels.  That’s the engine that drives healing.  Type 2 Diabetes is a condition of insulin resistance due chronically elevated insulin from eating carbs past your tolerance for years and decades.  Out of control blood glucose is just a symptom – one that carries it’s own devastating health consequences.  Healing from insulin resistance is the whole ball game.  Most people can heal – some wait too long before making the changes that could have brought them healing.

The difference between carb abstinence and healing from insulin resistance?  It’s the ability to eat SOME carbs SOME of the time and have a normal person’s response.  Your glucose rises, insulin is released, and the glucose is moved into cells.  But at best – you only heal back to your own starting point.  You never get someone else’s ability to eat carbs seemingly without consequence.  If eating too many carbs made you sick before, it will make you sick again. 

Because you are still a diabetic?   No, because you refuse to eat within your own personal tolerance for carbs.  At first, as a Type 2 Diabetic, you might have very little tolerance for carbs.  So have much more than others.   Even within those that are Type 2 diabetics we don’t have the same carb intolerance.   But as you remain in low insulin statues, you will begin to heal and YOU will gain FOR YOURSELF a greater tolerance for carbs.  The freedom to have the occasional treat without harmful blood glucose spikes is the beginning.  Keep going, allow the healing to progress, maybe for years, and you may well heal back to your original starting point.  

It’s wise, though, to simply adopt the low carbohydrate way of eating as your baseline for life.

- Always consult your doctor for your own specific needs.

- Educate yourself to fully participate in your health planning.
