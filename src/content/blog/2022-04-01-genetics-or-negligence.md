Let’s talk genetics. Clearly genetics play a role in our lives as it relates to T2D and obesity. Growing up I used to tease my dad for his big gut. I was skinny and ate all kinds of junk foods and drank sodas non-stop. “I’ll never be fat like you, dad”.

Well, that turned out to be wrong. I became as fat as my father and then more so. I felt “well, that’s what happens to us Harrington's. We start out thin and we get fat as we age”.

My former doctor told me “diabetes isn’t your fault, it’s genetic”…as she added a third medication and told me I’d be going on insulin at our next meeting in 3 months. I shouldn’t feel bad – I was never going to avoid diabetes…it was my genetic destiny.

Or was it? Take a look at the following set of photos. In the first, I’m morbidly obese. Genetics, right? Obesity runs in my family.

But then…what happened to my genes as I became the guy in the next photo? By changing what I ate and exercising…I stopped being the guy that was fat “just like my father”. Did I get a gene transplant?

But wait…maybe the genes were my destination all along because I became the guy in the third photo. Even fatter than before and now a type 2 diabetic with A1c of 11.2.

This is the version of me that my former doctor told that my diabetes was genetic and not my fault. Take these meds and soon you’ll also need insulin shots.

Once again, though, I changed my diet and started exercising again and got off all the meds and never needed insulin. On the day my doctor took me off the last med I asked her, “why isn’t what I did what you are teaching people?”. Her reply was “not everyone is willing to make the changes you did”.

Genetics may set the stage…but my own actions determined my outcome. I’ve had the same genetic makeup the whole time. Whether I became obese and diabetic or whether I became healthy — was a product of my actions.

I don’t have the option of trading genes with anybody else. I will always have the genetic predispositions that I have. If I eat the standard American diet, I get morbidly obese and diabetic. That’s my genetics…..as expressed through my actions.

Change my actions, my diet, fasting and exercise…and I transform my health. My genes are not my fault. My type 2 diabetes is most definitely my fault as living healthy is my responsibility. My poor health was through negligence.

This is both a hard truth and a liberation. I am empowered to make changes and improve my health. And I have. And I will continue on improving my health.
