Type 2 diabetes is a deadly disease that can rob you of your health before it takes your life. It can cause blindness, amputations, kidney dialysis, heart attack, and stroke. But, the good news is that it can be defeated. As someone who battled with type 2 diabetes, I define "winning against type 2 diabetes" as reclaiming my health.

At first, I did not take my diagnosis seriously, and my condition continued to worsen. I went from taking metformin and glipizide to adding the $1000/month Trulicity and was scheduled to begin insulin shots. The thought of going blind or experiencing any other type 2 diabetes complication was a wake-up call for me. I realized that I needed to take control of my health to avoid these devastating consequences.

Reclaiming my health meant more than just aiming for an A1C of 7 with medication. It meant tackling the root cause of my diabetes, which was my diet and sedentary lifestyle. My goal was to not only get off medication but to also avoid insulin shots. Although it was challenging, I changed my diet, started exercising, and read and learned about ways to improve my health.

I discovered that medication alone cannot bring health. It's essential to take medication when necessary, but we also need to put in the work to regain our health so that we don't need them. If I had learned this lesson earlier, I might have prevented developing type 2 diabetes. When my blood pressure was high and my waistline expanded, I should have taken action to improve my health instead of solely relying on medication.

Taking medication is better than living with the symptoms of a condition, but it's essential to use medication as a stepping stone to regain our health. Winning against type 2 diabetes is an ongoing process that requires us to continually improve our diet and fitness. It's not easy, but it's worth it to avoid the life-threatening complications that come with type 2 diabetes. 

 Reclaiming my health has been an empowering journey that has brought me numerous benefits beyond just managing my blood sugar. I feel more energetic, sleep better, and have even lost weight, which has boosted my confidence and overall well-being. Most importantly, I now realize that a pill can never give me all the things that being healthy does. Therefore, I urge you to focus on winning against type 2 diabetes by adopting a healthy lifestyle, rather than just managing your blood sugar. With perseverance and determination, you too can experience the same benefits and live a happier, healthier life.
