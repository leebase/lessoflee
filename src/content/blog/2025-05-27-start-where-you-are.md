In my journey to regain my health, I’ve developed mantras along the way. One of them is: Start where you are, make improvements. I was following this advice before I ever codified it into a saying. I improved what I ate—not all at once, but one substitution at a time. Learning one new dish I could cook and enjoy. Same with exercise. I didn’t go out and run a marathon on day one (or ever). I went for a short walk. Then a longer walk. Then a faster walk. I kept building up, despite being humbled by women pushing baby strollers blowing past me on the trail.

As I began to see solid improvements—then amazing victories (got off all diabetes meds and took my A1c from 11.2 to 5.4)—people would ask me how I did it. By that time, I was a good ways down the road, and I couldn’t just say “do what I’m doing now,” because it would’ve been too far ahead for them. So, I came up with start where you are, make improvements as a mantra to reflect how I’d been living and progressing.

Now, I’m the one who needs to hear it again.

I’m looking at my own past accomplishments and having to tell myself: You can’t jump straight to the best discipline, the best eating, the best fasting, the best exercise that I achieved over three years. I’ve been trying to restart, and keep having to restart.

So now I’m telling myself again: Start where you are. Make improvements.

I KNOW so much. I know how to eat, exercise, and fast perfectly—or at least far better than I currently have the discipline to follow. So it’s time to forget about what I used to be able to do and just do better. Rebuild the discipline. Don’t let perfection be the enemy of progress.

I will not judge myself for failing to eat to my highest standards. I will try to eat better today than yesterday. I will build upon the improvements.

My health writings began as a way to hold myself accountable. If I tell people what I intend to do, then I’ll have more motivation to follow through. I don’t recommend this for everyone, but it helps me. It has remained, first and foremost, a chronicle of my journey. I have nothing to sell. I do not have a coaching business. I began to have success, and it inspired people—adding a responsibility, a ministry even. I wrote a lot based on “see me—these things I’ve learned may help you.” It’s HARD to continue writing about what I’ve learned when I’ve NOT been the example.

Well, I have been an example—of what not to do.

I still know everything I learned from the 30 books I read and countless hours of research. I said—and I still believe—Type 2 diabetes can be healed for a LOT of people. I was one of them. But I’ve also said: Your body tells the truth. What I ate that led me to being diseased and disabled in the first place would do so again if I return to those ways. I hope that both my success and my struggles can stand as testimony—and invitation. You can improve your health by what you eat, how you fast, sleep, exercise, and manage stress. It’s not magic. It’s health.

I hope this post unsticks me from writing. To be able to write, “Today I had a smaller piece of cake,” as a victory—even when I know I shouldn’t eat cake at all. That’s a big step back from what I once achieved. But it’s where I am right now. And it’s where I must start.

And then… make progress.

p.s. Always consult your health team for your specific needs.
