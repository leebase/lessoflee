June is Men's Health Month and thought I’d share about my health transformation over the past year.

“Diabetes is genetic. It’s not your fault.” My Dr tells me as she gives me the news that my A1c was 11.2 and adds the $1,000 a month Trulicity to the Metformin and Glipizide I was already taking. She schedules me to start on insulin at our next meeting in three months.

It may not be my fault, but it is my responsibility.

Diabetes starts at 6.5, at 11.2 it struck me that blindness could very well be in my future…or any of a number of the terrible health consequences of type 2 diabetes. Going blind is one of my primal fears. Fortunately, it was of the right kind that spurred me into action.

At our next meeting, instead of putting me on insulin, I was taken off the last of the meds. I’ve been managing my condition via diet and exercise and my A1c is now 5.8 – well below diabetic and on the way to normal. I’ve lost 60lbs and 8.5” from my waist. I've also gone from being barely able to walk down a flight of stairs and unable to walk across a grass lawn – to hiking through the woods up and down tree rut strewn hills and across sand and dunes to enjoy an afternoon by the lake.

I’d like to be proud of my accomplishments, and I am – but I’m keenly aware of the huge blind spot I had for health that led me to be in such a poor condition in the first place. I write this as I know I’m not alone in how I got here.

There are so many priorities in life, so much to take care of. It’s easy to just keep going along without realizing how your health is declining. You ascribe it to getting older. Then one day, you are in your mid fifties and feel like a case of “early onset elderly-ness”.

The things I’ve done to turn my health in a positive direction are what I should have been doing all along: eating right and exercising. For me it took a health shock to knock off the blinders from my eyes. I’m just happy that I woke up in time to do something about it.

Self education and problem solving – the key to my success in my career are the same two skills anyone needs to make dramatic improvements in their health. The 15 minutes of time you get with your doctor once a year isn’t enough. The medications will never bring you health and vitality.

You put the work in and your body can begin to heal. You problem solve as no one else is exactly the same as you. For every choice there are a myriad of conflicting experts, friends and family. You have to make your best bets and problem solve along the way.

To the young I hope to stand as an early warning. Value and prioritize your health. Don’t just get by, become strong and filled with vitality. For those with repair to do – take heart that the body can heal. Start where you are. Forgive yourself and make progress. Do good today and better tomorrow.

Change is hard. Poor health is even harder. I wish I could grant everyone a day of experiencing the revitalized health I feel. If you could understand how much better life can be, the journey wouldn’t look so daunting.

I hope to read next June, the success stories of those who take off their blinders today and began improving their health.
