I confess, I was raised to think that certain jobs were “a man’s job”. Taking out the trash, mowing the lawn among them. Mowing the lawn was my job. The day came when I was working two jobs and often couldn’t get to the task in a timely manner. My wife started mowing the lawn and decided that she liked to do so. And that’s how I stopped mowing the lawn.

Years later as my health declined, I couldn’t even walk across the lawn, let alone mow it. No longer was the task something my wife did because she preferred too…rather she was the only one who could.

For the last two years I’ve been reclaiming my health and abilities. I’ve gone from walking with a cane, struggling with stairs, needing a handicap sticker to park close with may car – to slowly but surely reclaiming my abilities. No more walking with a cane, no more handicap sticker. Eventually I began to be able to run again – that took a LOT of work just to get those first running steps with both feet in the air at the same time.

My wife is out of town helping out with our first grandchild. The lawn needed to be mowed. It has been so many years since I mowed the lawn. Today’s workout was sitting right there before me. Mowing the lawn is back among the things I CAN do.

And so I did.

Two years ago my A1c hit 11.2, my weight 336lbs, the $1000/month Trulicity was added to the two meds I was already taking – and I was soon to start daily insulin shots. That’s the day I said ENOUGH! I was tired of being sick and tired. I was fed up with becoming more and more disabled. Some may wonder “why not just take the meds, it’s so much easier”. For days like today when I regain my life one ability at a time. Meds cannot accomplish what eating healthy and exercise can do. So I took the meds and worked hard to not need them. And I no longer do. My a1c is 5.2 and I’ve been off all meds for a year and half. It’s been work and there is much to do.

The results are glorious. Nothing tastes as good as being healthy and able feels.
