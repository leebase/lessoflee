There is a reason why I push myself – it is necessary to build muscle (and more). It's that bit of extra effort that brings the most benefit. When you get to the point where you want to quit, and then push yourself further, you tell your body that it needs to rebuild itself stronger. Comfort is the enemy of progress and health.

Push – and then recover. It's actually during the recovery phase that the rebuilding occurs.

Pushing ourselves to our limits is necessary to trigger the adaptive response. The adaptive response is the process by which our bodies adapt to the physical stress of exercise. This adaptation can lead to numerous benefits, including increased muscle mass, improved cardiovascular endurance, stronger bones, and increased metabolic efficiency, among others. However, triggering this response requires pushing ourselves beyond our perceived limits.

Take it from me, someone who went from an A1c of 11.2 on three medications to a 5.5 off all medications for more than a year, someone who went from walking with a cane and driving with a handicap sticker to running a 5K. All of this was possible because I pushed myself beyond what I thought was possible.

How much more? Just a little bit more.

How much farther? Just a bit farther.

Always
