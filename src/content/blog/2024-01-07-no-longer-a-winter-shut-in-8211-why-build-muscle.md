It’s snowing in Chicago – and that used to make me practically a shut in. I have osteoarthritis in my knees and used to walk with a cane. My balance was such that I couldn’t walk across my lawn in the summer. Snow and ice? Time to stay inside.

I eventually started on my health recovery journey to reverse my type 2 diabetes. I started walking around the neighborhood with my cane. 15 minutes, 30 minutes. Then winter approached and I needed to find a way to keep the exercise going.

I came up with a workout called Supernatural- a subscription service for the Quest virtual reality headset. I could do this from home. There were squats and lunges – which I could barely do at first. I even fell over a couple times in the beginning.

I was working out for my diabetes but a strange thing occurred. As my legs started getting stronger, I noticed I could walk up and down the stairs in my house much more easily!? It finally dawned on me that my walking and balance problems were not about arthritis – it was muscle weakness. The pain was arthritis. The pain led to inactivity and that led to weakness. And as I changed my diet, greatly reducing the carbs, my pain level went down quite a bit.

With balance, I can stand on a scale without having to hold onto the wall. I can take a shower without fear of falling. I can put my pants on without having to sit down. In time I no longer needed the cane. In time I could walk outside in winter.

This is my third winter since embarking on the journey. I have no fear of walking outside. Sure, we all need to be careful about icy conditions. I am joyful that “stay in the house” is no longer required.

Health is so worth the effort. Muscle is vitality. Weakness is disability. I am grateful I didn’t accept my doctor’s perspective that “diabetes isn’t your fault, it’s genetic”. I’m glad that the specter of needing insulin shots and going blind scared me into action.

Meds manage symptoms – at best. Getting sick more slowly is a good thing. Getting healthy is far superior.

Inactivity leads to muscle loss. Exercise can bring it back. It’s hard work to change your diet and exercise. I can do hard things. You can too.

p.s.  Always consult your own health team for your specific needs

p.p.s. Educate yourself to fully participate in your health strategy
