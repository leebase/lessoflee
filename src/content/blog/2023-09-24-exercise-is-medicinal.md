It’s Saturday Push Day and I am still working at a moderate exercise level in the wake of having a micro stroke in my eye. I’ve talked to my eye specialist about any limits on exercise intensity given my eye situation – he deferred to my primary care physician. I asked my PCP and he deferred back to my eye specialist. So I’m left to figure out on my own on how much I can push myself. A foundational principle is: Exercise is Medicinal.

I both like and hate that phrase. The point is to communicate that exercise builds up health. If I said, “I had a micro stroke in my eye, and I’m taking medication” – people would be happy that I’m taking care of my health. But to say “I had a micro stroke, I’m going out to exercise” and people want to warn me to be careful. And I agree, I should be careful. A guaranteed way to be careless with one’s health? Don’t exercise. To take care of my health I need to eat well, sleep well, handle stress well – and exercise well.

Each week, I purposely push my heart rate up to 95% of my maximum and beyond during my workouts. Why? Because I am focused on rebuilding my heart health. High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) offers unique benefits that go beyond what more moderate exercise can provide. HIIT increases my metabolic rate, improves cardiovascular fitness, enhances insulin sensitivity, and helps with muscle preservation and fat loss. Moreover, it's a time-efficient option for busy individuals. By incorporating HIIT into my routine, I am actively working towards achieving optimal health and well-being. All forms of exercise have their place. I walk every day, and I engage in aerobics and strength training.

So for now, my focus is on incorporating moderate exercise into my routine and closely monitoring my blood pressure. I understand the importance of gradually pushing the limits and increasing the intensity of my workouts. However, it is crucial for me to do so in a manner that doesn't elevate my blood pressure to dangerous levels. I aim to stay well below the danger zone, which for me would be a systolic (top number) blood pressure of 180. I'm nowhere close to that; in fact, my blood pressure tends to decrease with moderate exercise. I’m also paying attention to how I feel and if anything in my vision changes. By carefully managing the intensity of my exercise sessions, I can continue to challenge myself while prioritizing my cardiovascular health and overall well-being. I am not advocating this approach for anybody else, and I wish my own medical team had given me more specific guidelines.

I wish there were more concrete directives from my doctors on this. Sad truth is doctors know a lot more about disease and symptom management than they know about building health. It’s sad that our nature has been to distrust exercise and put our faith in medications. I am out there every Saturday pushing myself as part of my “balanced diet” of exercise to build up my heart health. That micro stroke could have just as easily been a brain stroke or heart attack. The dangerous life is the sedentary one.

How about you? I’d love to hear your stories of rebuilding your health and pushing your boundaries. For others, I want to encourage you to join in.

We are building up our health and reclaiming abilities by doing more and going farther.

How much more? A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs
