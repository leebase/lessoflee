It’s that time of the month.  For the eighth time, I’m doing a 5 day, water only fast.  I’ve written before, and I’ll link at the end, of the health benefits.  This post is more about “what’s it feel like”.

To start with – I’ve become a fasting athlete.  What I do today isn’t something I could have done a year ago – or at least, not without a lot of suffering.  It’s exactly like getting into shape to be able to run a 5k, or a 10k, or even a marathon.  You can’t do it at the beginning, but with training, you can get there.   And I’m there.  I have trained, and I now have the habit of a once a month extended fast.

I do my fasts some time near the end of the month.  Yesterday was a celebration with family – a wedding shower for my middle daughter.  I enjoyed the meal and ate completely off plan.  I wasn’t excessive, but neither was I just having a nibble of this or that “forbidden” treat.  I’ve been eating close to carnivore all month so I was in a low sugar state before the meal, and I started my fast right after.  Fasting is a tool and it’s flexible and I make it work for me.   I was up 4lbs that night.  Potato skins, onion rings, loaded baked potatoes and cookies can do that to you.   But it’s already all off, and I’m even lower than that after day one of my fast.

My wife is out of town spending time with the kids which makes this another great time for me to do my fast.  I don’t have to shop or cook or clean up dishes.  I’m not spending any money on food this week.  Fasting is free.  Fasting frees up time.  

It’s around dinner time of the first full day and I am hungry.  Your body still has the ghrelin cycle and you’ll get hungry around the times you normally eat.  Normally you eat and the hunger goes away.   If you don’t eat – your body goes to your fat stores and “eats” them, and the hunger goes away.   If you try this and you haven’t upregulated your fat burning pathways – it’s a different experience.  Just like going out and running a 5k is a lot different if you haven’t gotten fit first.

For me, the hunger isn’t that bad.  It’s pretty much how you feel if you have to work through lunch to get ready for a client presentation.  You’re hungry, but you don’t die.  I do deal with boredom and habit.  Have you ever gone to the movies but were broke and didn’t buy the popcorn or bring snacks?  That’s more what I feel.  At times, particularly at night when I’m watching tv – I can experience “I want to eats!” – that’s not about hunger.  I like eating.  I like watching tv and eating.  I miss it when I’m fasting.

Right now my bs is 106 – that’s 27 hours after my last meal and my bs is still above 100.  My breath ketones are 4.1 which is “just below reliable ketosis”.  I still have to do the extended fasts to get into that blazing fat burning ketosis.  My bs doesn’t fall below 100 until day 2 or so.   I spent a lot of years piling on the metabolic dysfunction.  I’ve made a lot of progress in healing, but you have to do what you have to do.   It doesn’t matter if the fasting app says “ketosis starts at hour 16”…but your own body says “I’m not in ketosis yet”.

I finally learned that lesson last month when I did carnivore for a month.  A month of zero carbs and my triglycerides finally came down into the normal range.  That’s supposed to happen fairly quickly when you go low carb.  But for ME, low carb wasn’t LOW ENOUGH carb.

It may sound extreme if you haven’t read up on extended fasting.  But what do you think all that fat on your body is for?  It’s to feed you when there’s no food.  Only there’s always food.  After all this effort for 15 months, I am still very obese.  I’ve about 75 more pounds to lose to get to a “normal” weight.  I’m not even aiming for that low at this point – I can’t imagine it.  I am aiming to lose about 50 more.  A five day fast should cause your body to eat up 3lbs or so of fat.

I’m doing the hard work because there’s a lot of health that needs to be regained.  Diabetes is in my rear view – but a heart attack may well be looming.  By every health marker that is associated with heart disease….I have them all.  Or had them all.  I’m making improvements, but more to do.   My dad died at 64 of heart disease.  I’m 57.   What I’m doing is not extreme – getting in such poor health and waiting so long to do something about it — THAT was extreme.
