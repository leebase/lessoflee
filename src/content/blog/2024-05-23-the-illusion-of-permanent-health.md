I’m heading into the city for a work conference. Lots of walking on uneven concrete, standing and stairs. I’ve lived in this city, worked in this city, or commuted to this city for decades. I work from home these days, but I still remember the lifecycle of my health as experienced walking in the city.

I’ve been young and a master of the sidewalk. Timing the lights and weaving in and out of thousands of fellow Chicagoan’s as I blew past them. Even as I aged, I still had my “Chicago Walk”. Until I didn’t.

I became old before my time. In my mid fifties I was morbidly obese, highly diabetic and arthritic. I walked with a cane, slowly. I struggled to make it across the 8 lanes of Wacker Drive before the light changed.

At 59 I walk just fine. I have arthritis pain but no longer need a cane. In fact, last year I ran 3.5 miles through the streets of Chicago. Ok, people walked past me as I was running – but I’ve come an immense way from the disabled man driving with a handicap sticker.

I also reversed my type 2 diabetes. I no longer need medication to control my blood sugar. I was highly diabetic and cruising down a sure road to terrible health consequences. Now I’m a former diabetic.

Which brings me to today and the title of this post. I am so happy that I can walk in the city, up and down stairs and not need to plan ahead for accommodations.

I have healed a great deal but have not arrived at a permanent state of good health. There is no such thing. Everyone has to maintain their health.

I am not “still a diabetic” because “once a diabetic, always a diabetic”. I am not still crippled because “once disabled, always disabled”.

If I stop moving, walking, exercising and eating healthy – all of my diseases can redevelop. This isn’t because “I’m still a diabetic” – it’s simply how health works.

I also haven’t even arrived at good health yet. I’m just “less unhealthy”. Lots more work to do.

For how long?

Always. As long as I desire to feel good and live vibrantly. Permanent health is an illusion, most people understand this. What I hope people grasp, is that many chronic diseases are also temporary if you put

In the hard work to eat healthy and move healthy.
