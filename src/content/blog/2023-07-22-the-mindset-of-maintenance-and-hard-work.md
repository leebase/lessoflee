I put up an AI generated photo of me, but this is the real version. It took a lot of work, to get to this point. My health recovery journey has been going for over two years. I’ve hit some good milestones, like getting off diabetes meds and bringing my A1c from 11.2 to 5.2. I have lost a great amount of weight and put too much of it back on. I have a LOT more work to do, more milestones to achieve: to get to a healthy weight, to heal completely from metabolic syndrome, heal from osteoarthritis, put on muscle.

One might reasonably ask – “your title says maintenance, but you have not arrived at your goals”. Great question. In the process of this two year journey I’ve stopped viewing that there is an end state. There will be no time I’ve “lost all my weight” so “I don’t have to diet any more”. My “blood sugar is under control” so I “don’t have to eat diabetic food”. I can walk up and down stairs so I don’t need to push myself in exercise. To have a healthy body, I will always need to be working hard on what I eat and how I move, sleep and handle stress.

Then why do all that hard work, if you will always have work hard? I have come to realize that I have always had to work hard, for everything: marriage, raising kids, education, career, managing my finances. I haven’t arrived in any of those areas such that I can simply start coasting. I am someone who works hard – but I hadn’t been the type to work hard on my health. The result was poor health.

Thus, I have adopted the “maintenance mode mindset”: maintaining the hard work of working on my health as my lifestyle. This is no tragedy brought on by the disease of Type 2 Diabetes. The disease was brought on by thinking everything ELSE was worth working hard on, but that I could just eat whatever I wanted and stay on the couch.

The good news is the great reward that comes from being healthy. You don’t quite appreciate your health until you’ve lost it. When you put in the hard work, you start reclaiming your health and that is awesome. I may not look like the AI generated muscle man, but I’m feeling so much better; barriers are being lifted, abilities regained.
