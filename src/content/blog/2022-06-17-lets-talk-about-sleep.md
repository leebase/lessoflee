Last night was a bad sleep night for me and came after three terrific (for me) nights in a row. However, it’s no mystery.

It was the final game for the NBA Championship so I was up late watching the game. I continued drinking caffeinated diet sodas the whole night while watching the game. Normally I don’t drink anything but water during the fasting window between the end of dinner and my first meal the next day.

I was also engaged with social media right up until I went to bed. So it’s no surprise that I had trouble getting to sleep.

I can’t always make a good sleep night happen, but I can certainly do stupid things to make a bad sleep happen.

Things I try to do:

- Exercise
- Start winding down a couple hours before bed
- Stop all social media an hour before bed
- read for that last hour
- dark bedroom
- cool temperature

I haven’t been taking any sleep remedies this month. I’m trying to live up to my new philosophy to take what ever I find that is helpful…in the least amount needed for the least amount of time. I’ve been tired by bedtime of late so haven’t needed any helps. I am trying to not treat “medicinal-style” supplements as if they are vitamins. I will be writing a post expounding on the notion of “medicinal-style” vs vitamins later.

No matter what I do (so far), I can never get 8 hours of sleep. I’m happy with 6 or more with 3 of that in “deep sleep”. I still get up several times a night to pee. Hopefully that will stop some day.

Sleep is an important part of health. Good sleep is an important part of weight loss and an important part of healing insulin resistance. Sleep is also important for the “building muscle” part of strength training.

I am not a great sleeper but I’ve seen improvement as I’ve worked on developing and then following a good sleep hygiene. You can see the difference between a night where I didn’t follow my sleep hygiene and the night before when I did.
