Those of you following my journey know that I have mantras – this is one of my favorite:  Start where you are, make progress.  Sometimes we watch others from afar and think “well, they have xyz advantage that I don’t” and then what?  Then you don’t do anything?  I allow myself to be inspired by others, but I too can get the green eye of jealousy/frustration when I see others do so easily what is hard for me.  When I was out on the trail walking, morbidly obese on arthritic knees, and EVERYBODY was passing me by.  When I worked really hard to be able to run again – and amazingly achieved the ability to have both feet in the air at the same time if only for a blink of an eye – I’m RUNNING – from having needed a cane to just walk.  And yet, while I was running, mothers pushing their babies in a stroller POWER WALKED right past me.  But hey, I was out doing the impossible.  How many people stop needing a cane to walk, struggle with stairs — and then recover to run at all? I may have momentary flights of envy and discouragement when I compare myself to others – but then I get back to MY task at hand.  Where ever I currently am, I just need to start from there and make progress.  There is no other option.  I can’t start from someone else’s place anyway.  I can’t start from how good I used to be.  I can only move forward from where I am.

This came to me as  I pondered the replies my “I Decided To No Longer Be Diabetic” post a review of my “greatest hits” replies from you guys that I save to re-inspire me all over again.  There is a strong “if Lee can do this, so can I”.  I very much want to keep that going.  And yet, what I’m doing right now for my health is clearly not beginner friendly.  I am using advanced health recovery techniques the likes of which Dr Fung and others apply in clinical settings to their most sick and insulin resistant patients.  And yet, there is so much here for all to draw inspiration from.  The main lesson is – we can turn our diabetic disease state around and be healthy.  It is NOT a disability and death sentence if you put in the work.

So – let’s discuss why I’m doing this particular protocol when I did nothing nearly as difficult and got off all my meds, and took my A1c from 11.2 to 5.6, lost 85lbs and went from walking with a cane to running a 5k.  If I did all that without a single day fast let alone 7 day fasts on repeat, why am I doing so now?  Time.  I was 55 when I started my health recovery journey.  I had made remarkable progress in three years.   I’d lost the 85 lbs, but had another 80 to go.  My a1c is great, but my still protruding belly loudly proclaimed that I was still diseased. And then I had a year of relapse into old eating patterns and gained back ALL the weight AND type 2 diabetes with an A1c of 7.1.  I’m now 60 years old.  Taking 3 years to get back to where I was with another 2 years to go from there to get to a health weight means I’d be 65.  And that’s assuming that I could go all that time with nothing but progress.  And it seems like forever.  And I have the looming heart attack I keep trying to work to postpone and/or survive and recover.  Thus I needed to come up with a protocol that achieve my health goals much faster – and I already had the tools to do it.  Start where you are, make progress – for me, four years into my journey – is a lot better position than when I first started.

Fasting and Carnivore.  There is no faster way to lose fat than fasting.  None.  And it just so happens to be an incredible health protocol above and beyond fat loss.  One guy pushing 500lbs fasted until he hit his goal weight more than a year later. Under doctor's supervision, he was completely fine. I wasn’t going to try to do the same.  That’s too daunting even for me.  I’d already done a year of once a month 5 day fasts – at my Dr’s recommendation.  He actually recommended 2 such fasts per month. So, I choose 7 days to push myself a bit farther and a week is around number.  I takes me until day 4 to get into deepest ketosis and I want to stay there a few days to burn the fat at the highest rate possible.  

Carnivore is a zero carb diet.  It’s also a fasting mimicking diet.  It’s healthy.  I’d only ever done a single month of carnivore and I had the best health results ever.  My triglycerides came down into normal for the first time and my hdl rose to normal for the first time.  I had never intended to just eat carnivore forever. I learned some lessons but did not continue on.  However, it’s in my tool kit.  I chose BBBE (beef, bacon, butter and eggs) as the simplified way of doing the diet.  I am expanding a bit, but more on that another time.   So, two weeks of BBBE and about the time I get tired of the “same old same old” food, it’s time to fast again.

I’ve done two such cycles already and the results are fantastic.  I’m down 29 lbs, and my blood glucose, even when eating, is phenomenal.  It’s like I’ve NEVER been diabetic. I’m paying attention, taking all the measurements (glucose, ketones, weight) I can and will tweak as I go.  But so far, this advanced protocol is working wonders.

If I stick to this protocol – I can hit my goal weight in 8 to 12 months.  That still seems like a long time, but it’s not forever. Being able to see the progress week to week is super motivational.  This is not a crash diet.  This is a scientifically formulated success protocol for significantly insulin resistant people.

This is me, starting from where -I- am and making progress.  It is not the blueprint for anybody else where THEY are necessarily.  I’m working with my age, my mindset, my propensities and the tools I have developed along the way.  To emphasize, I reversed T2D, lost a lot of weight, and stopped needing a cane to walk – and a host of other good health outcomes with a very low carb/keto diet, intermittent fasting, walking and exercising.  Just like I went on to do sprint intervals to get my heart rate into zone 5 — I had worked up to that.  I couldn’t possibly have done those from day one.

Which leads to another of my mantras:  I can’t…Until I can.  The “…” is you starting where you are and making progress and amazing yourself with what you’ll actually be able to do.

Incorporating lean protein add ons to increase satiety.  The result is still high fat, but not AS high of fat.  And more protein.  And I allow myself “flavor veggies” to help me stick to the diet.
