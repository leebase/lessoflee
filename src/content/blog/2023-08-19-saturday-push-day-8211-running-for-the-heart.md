I’ve been criticized for running as I have osteoarthritis. “Running is bad for the knees”. Well, I’m sure that improper running for unhealthy durations without the necessary recovery can certainly be harmful. Anything done to excess or improperly can be harmful. You know what else is harmful? Sitting on the couch. Your knees must be used to heal.

But why do I run? A couple reasons, but for this post I’ll focus on my heart. Diabetes and osteoarthritis are not my only health concerns. I’ve been a heart attack just waiting to happen. Your heart and lungs are your cardiovascular system, and they respond to exercise just like your muscles do. Nothing gets my heart rate up to the highest levels like running. Even as slow as my run is, it gets me to the 95% of max range. There are wonderful adaptations that occur when you push your body that hard. One doesn’t have to run to achieve this, but running needs no health club, no special equipment.

One of the most exciting changes in my body is the resting heart rate falling from the mid 80’s to the low 60’s with many nights in the 50’s. It’s a strong indicator that I’ve been improving my heart health. Who knows, I may avoid that heart attack, or at least put if off and have a better chance at surviving.

On Saturdays, I try to push my limits. It’s not all I do for exercise. I walk at least 30 minutes every day. That’s the floor. I aim for a couple days a week of strength training (confession, I’ve been lax in this all year). I do my Supernatural (Virtual Reality workout) aerobics workout 2 to 3 times a week.

Every day isn’t a push day. In fact, any day that has high intensity workout or strength training needs recovery. It’s during the resting phase that your body builds itself back stronger.

Today I ran for a mile, ran sprints, and walked. I wanted to run 3.5 miles without stopping, but at the 1 mile mark I was already at 95% of max. Time to walk. And then sprint and walk and sprint and walk some more.

Join in the body recovery plan. One doesn’t have to run, but if you want your body to improve, you do need to push yourself. You have to do more and go farther.

How much more? A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.
