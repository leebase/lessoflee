“You are doing it wrong”. “You are going to hurt yourself”. “I did  years ago and messed up ”. No matter what you try to do to improve your health, people will be quick to throw out warnings. Every step of my health recovery journey I have faced these reactions. The latest was when I showed my squats video. The first thing I thought when I saw myself was “ooh, I gotta work on my form”. But that’s ok. I will learn as I go. None of us can start with perfection.

There is one guarantee – if you don’t work on your fitness, your muscle health, and your cardio health – you WILL absolutely face terrible consequences. You MAY sprain a muscle, or hurt your back if you don’t lift weights properly, but you can recover and continue to learn. Particularly if, like me, you start easy and just keep increasing the weight over time. How much more? A little more.

If you can hire a trainer, that’s fantastic. If you have a sports medicine doctor – how wonderful. Or perhaps you have a friend or relative who knows a lot about exercise and they have the patience to teach you. But some folks would make people so afraid to make a mistake, or put such time or financial expectations that folks just give up before they even start. One is not LIKELY to injure themselves, but they certainly could. One is GUARANTEED to hurt their health by doing nothing.

I’m reading books and watching many YouTube videos. One can only do so much “head learning” but experiential learning only happens by actually trying. I was never an athlete so I didn’t know my body that well or how it could improve. I remember in my 30’s a friend invited me over and we did the Body For Life workout that was very popular at the time. Did I personally read the book? No, my friend had. We proceeded to lift every single muscle group to failure the very first workout. And the next day I felt like I’d been hit by a Mack truck. I was SO sore. I didn’t lift again for twenty years. I did a stupid thing. But the REAL tragedy was that I didn’t just learn my lesson and continue on.

There are so many things to learn about health: what to eat; how to cook; how and when to fast; how to run; how to lift weights; what supplements are helpful. I could go on and on. This is not the work of a day, it’s the work of a lifetime. You simply have to give yourself the freedom, the space to learn. You will make mistakes along the way. You will try some approaches that won’t work out for you like they did for someone else. You’ll ruin a few meals trying to learn to cook. You might pull a muscle or sprain an ankle. It’s ok. Really, it is.

My grandson is learning to walk already at 10 months old. He’s terrible at it – lol! Of course no one, including me, thinks that way. Nobody expects that you just one day start walking and doing so expertly. He falls a lot. It doesn’t deter him and we all clap and cheer him on. Why do we ever stop having this attitude about learning?

You want to turn your health around? You need to give yourself the space to learn. You’ll have to learn how to filter out all those “be careful, that’s dangerous” messages. Everything is dangerous. Somebody somewhere has a story about how  had terrible  outcome for them. Such people will leave you thinking the safe thing is to do nothing. And for sure, THAT is a guaranteed way to remain in poor health and to decline many years before you need to.

Type 2 Diabetes is in my rearview. My walking cane is in the closet gathering dust. I used to take antacids all day long – no more. No more daily pain meds and creams. My anxiety has vastly improved. I’ve lost a lot of weight with much more to go. I am regaining abilities I thought I’d lost forever. It’s worth all the hard work, the learning, the making of mistakes, and the keep going forward. You won’t know everything you need to do. You won’t have all the character you need to have at first. Give yourself the space to learn as you go.

p.s.  Always consult your own health team for your specific needs
