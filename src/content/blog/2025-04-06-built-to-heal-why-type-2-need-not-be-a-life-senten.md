I was donating blood this weekend and the kind lady pricked my finger and it bled.  Is it going to bleed forever?  No, of course not, it will clot and scab (if it had been more than a needle prick).  You know what that is?  That’s your body healing itself.  All through your body are healing processes ready to fix what’s ailing you and make you better.  The trick is to STOP INJURING YOURSELF.  That’s the task we diabetics face in changing our diets.  

She replied:  nobody has ever explained it to me that way.  Which reminded me that I need to continue writing and sharing to help give folks the mental models that can help them work WITH their bodies natural abilities to heal itself. 

And make no mistake, you can indeed heal from Type 2 diabetes.   You can heal from obesity.  You can heal from arthritis.  Read Ben Bickman’s “Why We Get Sick” and you’ll see that a significant amount of our chronic diseases all have the same root cause – hyper insulinemia.    Type 2 diabetics don’t have too little insulin, we have too much.  

We have too much because we continue to injure ourselves with the foods we eat.  Stop eating highly processed, hyper palatable manufactured “food like substances”.  Eat meat, fish and non-startchy veggies.  Fast.  Let your body heal itself.

I know some people grapple with the concept of ever healing from Type 2 diabetes.  It’s because they have the wrong notion of what it means to be healed.  It doesn’t mean “I can eat anything I want without consequence”.   It’s that “food” that is the disease.  We aren’t supposed to be eating it.  It’s not what we evolved to handle.

During the process of healing you may need to eat a lot less of foods that are indeed otherwise healthy for OTHER people.  Fruit, rice, potatoes — in moderation are typically fine.  Just not for someone who’s metabolism is in a diseased stated.  But in team, you’ll heal to be able to tolerate such foods in moderation.

For how long?  Forever.  You can be healed forever – if you forever stop injuring your body with the foods you eat.    I haven’t been doing so well with the forever part.  But I will claw my way back.

Oh – just a reminder – whether or not diabetics can donate blood is specific to your location.  If you would like to help save lives by donating blood – don’t assume you can’t help “because I’m a diabetic”.  Check for your own local rules.

p.s. Always consult your health team for your specific needs.

p.p.s. Educate yourself. Be your own best advocate.
