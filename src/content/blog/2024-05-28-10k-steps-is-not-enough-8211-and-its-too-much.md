I have no performance goals when it comes to exercise.  I have no idea what my max bench press is.  Pretty sure it’s “not that much”.  How many steps do I take?  I pay attention, but there is no number I’m trying to hit.  Some people advocate “10k steps a day” or some other metric of “if you do this, you’ll be healthy”.  Sometimes whatever is advocated is too much or unrealistic.  Often times, frankly, it’s a very low bar that has nothing to do with what it takes to go from unhealthy to healthy.  The level of activity a person did all their life that has kept them healthy, is not what someone needs to do to regain health and mobility.   The same is true with diet.  

Did you know the 10k steps a day was made up as a marketing ploy by a company selling a step counter device?  Yep, true story.  Doesn’t make it a bad thing to aim for, but it’s not some scientific truth.

You want the truth?  The answer to “how much of anything” is needed if you want to improve your health and fitness is … more.  When you start walking from being sedentary, 10k steps might be way too much.  Start where you are, do what you can, and a little more.  Always a little more.  It’s at the “more” side of the effort that your body gets the message that adaptations are necessary.  That first 15 minute walk is a victory.  But a year later?  Not so much.

If you keep on upping the ante, keep going a bit faster or a bit farther for a bit longer, eventually it will become hard to do.  Whatever you doing – if you keep pushing for more – it will eventually become hard. That’s where you want to be.  You want to start where you are, keep progressing until easy is a thing of the past and then you live in country of hard.  Place citizenship in the land of hard.

My walks used to be hard.  Walking with my cane around the neighborhood was difficult.  I still walk every day – helped along by my dog.  However, walking long ago ceased being hard.  So I walk for enjoyment and mental health and find OTHER ways to keep pushing my body.

Why?  Why isn’t an enjoyable walk enough?  10k steps was way too much and now that I can do so, I’m saying it’s not enough.  I need to keep improving.  I have more health to recover, more abilities to reclaim.  I haven’t even arrived at healthy.  I’ve become less-unhealthy.   I am not strong, but I am less weak.  Old age is coming, and likely a heart attack.   Both are going to need me going into them as strong and as healthy as I can get.

I aim to keep going and build up my base so that the inevitable decline happens much later in life.  If I do have a heart attack, I want to survive it.  I want to recover.  And, of course, I hope to avoid it.

I speak from the perspective of a middle age man who struggled to walk up and down the stairs in my own home, and parked in the handicapped places (with appropriate sticker) because I NEEDED to be that close.  Even so, I had to use the shopping cart as a walker and get in and out of the store quickly.  I’ve already experienced disability that came on me from neglecting my health.  I know how hard I’ve had to work to overcome just to be at “weak and slow” instead of sick and disabled.

Maybe there is a point of being fit enough that not only can I do the daily needs, but I have enough extra buffer to handle a fall or an illness.  If so, I haven’t arrived and therefore I stick to that need for doing more.

How much more? A little more.

How far? A bit farther.

Always.

3 years and counting in my health recovery journey.  A lifetime more to go.

p.s.  Always consult your own health team for your specific needs

p.p.s. Educate yourself to fully participate in your health strategy
