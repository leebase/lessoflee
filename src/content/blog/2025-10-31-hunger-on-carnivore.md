There are plenty of folks who make it seem like eating meat means one is never hungry.  Maybe that’s true for some, but if there exists a person who can overeat meat, eggs and bacon, I’m that person.  

Or am I?  

I’m 11 days past my 9 day fast.  11 days of eating BBBE (beef, bacon, butter and eggs).  I’m not 100% but darn close and my strays are in the keto direction, not carbs or sweets.  I’ve stayed in ketosis since the fast.

It’s noon and I’m hungry. I ate breakfast around 9am.  I shouldn’t be hungry. But it’s more of an “hey, it might be nice to eat something” – not a “OMG, I need food NOW!”. It’s the type of hunger I feel after day 3 or so of a fast.  It’s the memory of food and the pleasure of eating, not ravenous hunger.  Habit, not need.  And it’s far easier to say “I’ll wait awhile”.  

My post dinner snacking has been far less of a battle.  This is my Waterloo.  The time of day when good intentions meet old, unhealthy habits Lee.  I’ve done fairly well, and when I’ve given in to eat, I’ve kept to carnivore foods. 

So will I get to the point where I can just eat when I’m hungry and be free of the feeling the rest of the time?  Who knows.  Is it better than my experience when eating “the food that makes me fat and diabetic”?  Oh yes.  Noticeably so.  Where meat and fat satiate, carbs fuel my appetite. 

Does hunger matter?  Of course it does.  But one can learn to manage hunger. And moving to a far lower carb intake has made the task easier.  Carb addiction is real and I still feel to tug to not just eat, but to eat the foods that make me sick.

My knees are already thanking me every day for how much less inflamed I am eating carnivore.  And the lower intensity of the hunger feelings make it easier to not over eat.

Here’s a little science about this: 

What’s happening physiologically:

1. Stable insulin and leptin – No post-carb insulin crash, so the hypothalamus never sends urgent “feed me now” alarms.

2. Ketone-mediated satiety – β-hydroxybutyrate acts directly on the brain to suppress the neuropeptide Y pathway that causes intense hunger.

3. Fat oxidation buffer – You’re running on body fat between meals, so your blood energy levels don’t drop.

That muted, negotiable hunger—“I could eat, but I don’t have to”—is the hallmark of full fat adaptation. It means:

• You can delay a meal without discomfort.

• Fasting windows become effortless rather than forced.

• You’re likely matching calorie intake to real energy need, not habit.
