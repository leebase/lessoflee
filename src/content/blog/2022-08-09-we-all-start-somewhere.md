I am reluctant to accept the notion that I have become, in some ways, a role model. I know that I have become an advocate of hope in the reality that we can regain much of the health that we’ve lost. Mostly, though, I’m simply someone living my health journey in public. I came here with no answers, no message, and simply shared:

“Ok folks, here I am. It's been 8 years since I lost that incredible 80lbs and got fit. I'd love to report that I kept it all off, but I haven't. So, as in 9 years before when I started my journey, I am announcing before any success...that I intend to get healthy. Again. Last time I started at 315, this time I start at 336. More importantly, this time I start as a diabetic with out of control A1C of 11.6 and osteoarthritis in my knees. This time I have to accomplish the task in my mid fifties.”

I think I was wrong and my A1c was 11.2. I was taking Metformin, Glipizide and Trulicity.

In addition I had developed osteoarthritis in my knees. I had daily acid reflux and heartburn. I was on blood pressure meds. I couldn’t walk very well and had difficulty with stairs. I had a handicap sticker for my car and not too long before had walked with a cane. I was prone to bouts of anxiety that affected my sleep.

I had not yet read The Diabetes Code or any of the other 18 books I’ve read since then. My self control, discipline and will power was such that EVEN THOUGH I knew all I needed to know to lose weight and get fit and EVEN THOUGH I had actually done it….for the last 9 years I kept putting weight back on and lost more and more of the fitness I had worked so hard to develop. I had given away all my fat clothes and endured the indignity of not only regaining the weight, I had to buy ever bigger clothes. And then again. And then again.

I did this all in public. My family, friends, church members and coworkers – they’d seen my progress and had cheered me on. And then grew quiet and concerned as I reverted and lost all that ground.

About 2 and a half years before I was told by my doctor that I was now a diabetic. My A1c was 8.5 and I was given metformin and Glipizide. I wasn’t concerned because “I know I can go low carb and lose the weight and get my sugars under control”. But I didn't do it. I’d start and then give up. Time and again. Eventually I just stopped caring or paying attention. By this time Covid had made us all house bound. My eating really got out of control. I ate chips and ice cream every day. My weight continued ballooning.

That was 15 months ago. That was the state I was in when I came here and said I was going to regain my health. Sound familiar? Isn’t that someone you can relate with?

Everything that came since then I had to develop or recommit to. I didn’t have the self control or motivation or will power that I have now. I simply started.

I didn't know all I would go on to learn. I simply started.

I couldn’t come close to doing the level of exercise I now do daily. I simply started.

I started and started again, and started again. I did good, I did bad. I started doing good more often than bad.

Now I’m down to a normal A1c of 5.6, I’ve lost 74lbs. I’ve increased my strength and reclaimed so many abilities. The photo, those stairs are in the hotel I’m staying at. No problems carrying my luggage up and down. I sleep so much better. I haven’t taken an antacid in a year. I haven’t taken any diabetes pharmaceuticals in a year. I no longer take pain pills or anxiety meds or rub pain creams as a daily practice.

How did I get here? I started. How will I get where I’m going? By continuing to improve and learn as I go. To do better more often and badly more rarely.

If you think you can’t follow the me that is today….follow the me of 15 months ago. Just start.
