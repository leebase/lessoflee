He didn’t even exist April of '21 when I began my health reclamation journey. But now he’s here and I have one more person counting on me. My wife needs me. My three adult children need me. I'm not speaking of “they’d be sad without me” but “their lives would be so much harder without me”. They count on me. I have many roles in their lives, and provider remains a significant need.

I’ve worked hard my whole life to provide. It’s a central part of my ethos. I’ve done fairly well by my family. I spent a lot of time and energy in pursuit of earning the money to pay the bills. I didn’t put that kind of effort in taking care of my health. How very stupid of me.

I'm sure I am not alone in seeing to “all” the important things in life while taking my health for granted. In fact, I was negligent with regard to my health.

I was on track for an early death. Who knows, that may still come to pass. How will I provide from the grave? Yes, I did have the mindset that life insurance was the answer to providing for my family. But was money all they needed from me?

And yet, as ridiculous as that thinking was – I hadn’t counted on living sick long before dying. Morbidly obese. Walking with a cane. Living in pain. Diabetes primed to steal my eyes and my feet. Being in poor health was going to be worse than death as far as providing for those that depend on me.

I’m not battling the disease of diabetes. I’m not fretting over my favorite foods that I can’t eat any more except as occasional treats. I got past that when I realized that what I eat and how I move could restore my health. My diabetes got so bad that it finally scared me enough to live the way I should have all along. I am not managing a disease, I am building health.

Shockingly, I have actually been improving my health, reclaiming abilities, reducing pain. I'm far from perfect and I “backslide” here and there. But I press onward. I strive both for those that depend on me and because it’s awesome to feel so much better, to remove limitations on my life.

I have much to live for and plenty to give. My grandson enjoys his Pawpaw's magic tickle fingers. I want to be around to give him so much more. And to do that I will push my body more and farther.

How much more? A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.
