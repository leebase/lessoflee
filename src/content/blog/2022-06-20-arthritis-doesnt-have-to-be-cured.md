Arthritis doesn’t have to be cured for your life as someone who has osteoarthritis to greatly improve.  

I have OA in the knees.  My world had become smaller and smaller as I became less able to walk.   I have had to walk with a cane.  I have sized up walkers the way I’ve looked into sports cars.  I have struggled to get off the train before the doors closed.  I could go on with stories.

Now I’m walking trails through the forest and across sand dunes.  My long walks are now 4 miles and I’ve started interval training with a modified running style I call “crouch jogging”.

There is still much I can’t do.  I can’t play tennis or basketball.  I can’t play badminton much more than just hit back and forth a bit.  Even the things I can do, come with the cost of pain.

Will there be a limit to what abilities I can reclaim?  I don’t know.   I don’t know if I’ve experienced ANY healing of my arthritis.  I don’t know if I will ever actually get rid of arthritis.

What I have done is improve in how I live with arthritis.   And because of this, I have reclaimed abilities and I am still reclaiming even more.  My small world is growing.

When I cut the sugars out of my diet for my diabetes, I discovered that my knees felt better.  Less pain.  I no longer take any daily pain meds whether prescription, over the counter or herbal supplements.  I no longer use or need any pain creams.  Not day to day.  At times, yes.  Not daily, not weekly, and sometimes not even monthly.

I started strength training – again for my diabetes.  I discovered that I began walking better.  All of my walking problems weren’t from OA pain.  My pain had led to me not using my muscles which lead to weakness.  Getting stronger has brought back ability.

Losing weight is a big help.   For every pound you lose, you are removing 4lbs of pressure on your knees.  I’ve lost 60lbs thus have reduced 240lbs of pressure on my knees.   I can have the same amount of OA, but if I get down to my goal weight of 215, that’s 480 lbs less pressure on my knees – and may well be enough to make my OA a minor inconvenience.  Or certainly less so than it has been at 336lbs, which was my weight a year ago.

And maybe, just maybe, eating a low inflammation, highly nutritious diet combined with more muscle strength, combined with improved cardio vascular ability, combined with a healthy weight, combined with the autophagy that comes from fasting….maybe that will allow my body to actually heal the arthritis.   I don’t know.

But whether I experience healing or not – I am experiencing an expansion of my world as I recapture, regain ability after ability.   Less pain, more activity.  More life.  More vitality.
