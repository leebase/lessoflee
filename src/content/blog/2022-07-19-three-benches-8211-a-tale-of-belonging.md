A story of progress told with three benches. Most of my journey back to health has been focused on healing from Type 2 diabetes. All along I’ve also been hamstrung by osteoarthritis. Exercise is such an important part of regaining health, but how can I exercise when I can barely walk?

Here are fitness goals I wrote – seven months ago:

OA Arthritis in Knees

• Increase stability

• Not fall when walking on uneven surfaces

• Walk down stairs normally and without rails or one step at a time

• Easily pick up something from the floor

• Ability to jump as in a jumping jack or jump rope

• Ability to jog

• Be able to mow lawn

• Be able to walk across grassy field

This past weekend I went out for a run. That’s right…from not being able to walk on uneven surface, not being able to jog, I now run intervals. Mind you, it’s a quite feeble “crouch run” because the arthritis is still there and I can’t take the shock of normal running.

The first bench photo is a milestone. For the first time, I jogged to that bench without stopping to walk. It's about a half mile. Only 3 months ago I was celebrating my first few jogging steps where both feet were in the air at the same time.

I’m on the path and I’m jogging amongst the young and healthy. They whiz by me. I smile and one of them gives me a thumbs up. He sees the morbidly obese man with the pitiful “crouch jog” out working on his health…and he lets me know “good for you”.

I arrive at the second bench past the mile mark. Only stopped to walk twice. A long way to go to reach my goal of running a 5k. But I’m making progress.

People my age or older are also out on the trail. Passing me by. Smiles and nods. I’m one of them, I’m on the trail even if I’m walking or slow. I belong.

I reach the third bench 2 miles away from my home. My heart rate is 146 from the interval running up the hill to the bench. 15 minute per mile pace. Not winning any races, but it’s an improvement from my 28min mile walking pace where I started.

And now it’s back to home. I pass by a couple not much younger than me. They are walking with sticks and are more obese than I. They are slower than I. But they are on the trail. I give them a thumbs up and let them know…they belong.
