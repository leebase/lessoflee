I have pre-heart disease. A term I just now made up. It’s like pre-diabetes. I have the markers that let me know that if I don’t make changes, I will have the consequence of heart disease: heart attack, stroke.

Now…this should come as no surprise for the same thing that leads to type 2 diabetes leads to heart disease. Even before you get to be a diabetic, you are developing heart disease. It’s not the diabetes causing heart disease. It’s the “metabolic syndrome”/“insulin resistance syndrome” causing both.

The good news is that the steps we take to put diabetes into remission are the same steps that begin to heal your diseased arteries.

Which brings us to “Lee, you eat all that fat, how’s your cholesterol”. Mind you, it was my decades of Standard American Diet that brought on the metabolic syndrome…not my low carb high fat diet.

My cholesterol is indeed up. It was already high and gotten 11% higher and the LDL is 20% higher. My old “diabetes forever” doctor wanted me on statins.

But LDL cholesterol isn’t the problem and lowering it doesn’t cure heart disease. (Read The Clot Thickens or The Great Cholesterol Myth)

And yet, other, better indicators do indeed inform me that I am pre-heart-disease. How am I doing?

My triglycerides are down near 60%. They are still high, but are much better. My triglycerides / HDL ratio is bad. And that’s a good predictor of heart disease. See [https://www.docsopinion.com/triglyceride-hdl-ratio/](https://www.docsopinion.com/triglyceride-hdl-ratio/)

Above 4 is strongly correlated to heart disease and is a better predictor than LDL, the much maligned “bad cholesterol”. My ratio is 4.7 – so it’s bad. But it’s down from 6.5. The food you’ve seen me eating has improved my ratio by 38%. Clearly, I have more work to do.

Remnant cholesterol is another, superior, metric to LDL. See [https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/news/newsroom/news-releases/study-suggests-remnant-cholesterol-as-stand-alone-risk-for-heart-attack-and-stroke](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/news/newsroom/news-releases/study-suggests-remnant-cholesterol-as-stand-alone-risk-for-heart-attack-and-stroke)

And here again, I have clear signs of developing trouble. But my remnant cholesterol has improved by 21%.

Now why is LDL what our doctors focus on? Because there are drugs to lower LDL. Which they do effectively. But which don’t improve health outcomes much at all (read the books).

So yes – I am I not afraid of eating cholesterol or animal fats. My intermittent fasting, low carb eating is helping me with my pre-heart-disease just as it’s put my T2 diabetes into remission.
