There are long term health issues like type 2 diabetes and osteoarthritis that I deal with. Then there are the short term or instantaneous happenings. I have had 3 of the latter this year, two of which have had happy resolutions.

This spring I lost my upper singing range. Out of nowhere, I just couldn’t hit my tenor notes. I don’t have a great voice to begin with, but I love singing particularly in church. One day I had my voice, the next day part of it was just gone. It took about three months for my range to return. Other than resting my voice and drinking tea, I didn’t do anything proactive. Didn’t know what I could do. But, in time, my body fixed itself. Praise God!

A couple weeks ago, I had a micro stroke in my right eye. In an instant my sight was changed. If I didn’t have 2 eyes, I would not be able to read normal sized text. I will be getting injections this Saturday. I have been refraining from the most intense levels of exercise for a while. But there’s really no “action steps” I can take to do anything about the eye. High intensity exercise is part of how I’m healing my body, I am cautiously building back up to it.

I had a sore back out of nowhere, and on day 2 the pain flared so bad, that I was knocked to the garage floor with no way to get up for a few minutes. I was in great pain and dug my long unused cane out of the closet to get around with. I got treatment at my chiropractor and took it easy for a few days. I did take anti-inflammatories that day and the next. I avoid taking them most of the time as acute inflammation is part of the healing process. I did slow walks, and then normal walks. Yesterday I was able to go on long walks and do my VR aerobics. The chiropractor gave me some exercises I will be doing to strengthen the muscles that help the lower back. At least that’s something I can do!

What can I do all the time? Eat healthy foods (for me that’s animal products and minimal veggies and berries) along with intermittent fasting. Exercise – pushing more and going further. Doing what I can to get a good night’s sleep and handle stress better. This is the set of activities that are in my control and absolutely build up my health. I started this journey to fix my type 2 diabetes and happily found out that it also helped my osteoarthritis. I’ve regained so many abilities that I thought I’d lost forever.

Who knows how much better I can get. I was told by my doctor “type 2 diabetes isn’t your fault, it’s genetic” as she added the $1000 per month Trulicity the the metformin and Glipizide I was already taking. She told me that I’d be going on insulin. That’s when I kicked into gear. She didn’t tell me I could get better, but I read The Diabetes Code by Dr Jason Fung and embarked on at least TRYING to. I’ve been off all the meds for more than 2 years and never did have to take insulin shots.

My ortho said my knees needed to be replaced. I was walking with a cane and was in pain, stairs were difficult. I wasn’t even trying to fix my knees – my doctor didn’t tell me I could. But when I changed my diet and started exercising and fasting for the diabetes, my knee pain when down and my leg strength went up. I now can run 5k’s and sprint intervals. Stairs are no problem for me anymore. I wouldn’t dream of replacing my knees any more.

I may have been late to this understanding – but I’m on board now. There are things under my control that bring health. Diabetes meds never made my diabetes any better. Pain meds and creams whether natural or pharma – never made my knees better. I am not anti-med, but I’ve learned that to bring health to my body, I need to eat healthy, move healthy, sleep and handle stress in a healthy way. It won’t prevent every possible health issue, but it’s a LOT better than the alternative.

I am not even to the point of being healthy yet. I am “less unhealthy”. I’m making progress and it’s worth the hard work.

I wish all who read this their own happy healing stories.

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs

p.p.s. Educate yourself and be a full participant in your health strategy
