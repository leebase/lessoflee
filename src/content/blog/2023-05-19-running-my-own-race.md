I ran my first race this Thursday along with 30,000 other runners. I’ve come a long way from walking with a cane and driving with a handicap sticker.

I am somewhat generously calling what I do “running”. People walked past me as I was running. It only took a few minutes after the start for the runners to long leave me in the dust. At least it wasn’t that crowded for most of the race.

I was at a race, but the only person I was racing against was myself. It didn’t bother me to see walkers passing me. They have their race and I have mine. If you take a gander at the chart of my heart rate you can see that, for me, that very slow run took extreme effort. I ran with my heart at the red line for an hour.

I had no idea what to expect as I’d never participated in anything like this. I work from home sitting in a recliner all day. I went into our down town office and had to sit in an office chair, stand up for long (for me) time, and then walk a almost 2 miles (3km) to get to the race. Then a hour of standing before the race began. Let me tell you, I do still have arthritis. I was already in pain before the race even started. I wondered how I could do it.

It was fun being there with all the people and a handful of my office mates. I’m sure the adrenaline from the excitement helped. It became as much mental as physical. Even with an Apple watch telling me how far I’d run, I had no real sense of where I was on the course or how much longer I’d have to keep going. I have two apps when I’m running, they give very different distance values. After this race, I know which one is right. But during the race, I kept having to tell myself “just keep going”, I can run this way forever. Of course, I couldn’t but I had to not think of the end, but be prepared to run and run and run.

Boy, when I hit that last turn and could see the finish line – THAT was thrilling. I was running around a 10-11minute kilometer for the first 5km, and my last was just over 6 minutes. I have a lot of room to improve the time. And I will. After the race, I then had to walk those 2 miles back. My teammates were already at the bar celebrating.

Some might wonder why, why do this? To keep improving my health. There was a time when taking a 30 minute walk around the neighborhood with my cane was pushing it. Every step from there to here came from doing more and going farther.

The day will come with this race will be to me like those days of walking with a cane were because I’m going to keep pushing.

How much more? A little more.

How much father? A bit farther.

Always.
