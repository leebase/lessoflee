I have embarked on a 21 day water only* fast.  I’m comparing it to a marathon because people cheer folks on who embark on the much more difficult and much more health challenging task of running a marathon.  Meanwhile, a 21 day fast for someone who doesn’t have a medical reason not to fast at all — is amazing for your health.

I have written extensively on the health benefits of extended fasting and will link below.  This post will not delve into them.  And yes, I have my doctor on board and will see him on day 10 of the fast for labs and checkup.  He’s been my doctor for a couple years and worked with me for my 14 day fast.  And while I’m talking about doctors – fasting is NOT inherently dangerous, I’m just being wise and cautious.  Meanwhile running a marathon is hard on the body – but great for the mind.  Likewise, the mere challenge of a 21 fast is great for the mind.

I have been struggling with getting back on track for a year now.  I regained all my weight.  I am not trying for the first time, I have been struggling to get restarted time and again. A 21 day fast is both daunting and, frankly, exciting.  But let me not over play the “I’m climbing the mountain because it’s there” angle.  I have genuine health goals that this fast will be a fantastic tool for.  My number one health issue is morbid obesity.  Number two is type 2 diabetes.  Yes, I’m once again diabetic.  The two are not “exactly related” for everyone – but I’m in the majority of people where they are deeply connected.

The most obvious power of fasting is fat loss.  Every form of diet, every way that people lose fat short of liposuction – happens by getting the body to consume/burn the fat.  EVERY one.  Even having gastric bypass or taking Ozempic/Mounjaro work because ultimately the body consumes the fat.  There is no more powerful way than not eating.  Even a gastric bypass isn’t faster because eating tiny amounts is still more than eating nothing.

Thing is – your body is running on sugar most of the time in the modern world.  It takes time for the body to turn over into running on fat.  You have to use up all the energy use just ate, then the glycogen (stored glucose) in your liver and muscles. By measuring my ketones when fasting, I know that I don’t personally get into full fat burning mode until day three of a fast.  At my doctors recommendation, I used to do a 5 day fast once a month. I did this for more than a year.  It’s a wonderful practice, but 2.5 days of the fast is the turnover.  And those days are the hardest.  Imagine a marathon that started out with the difficulty of the last two miles and got easier as you ran.  That’s fasting.

In four months, I could do a 5 day fast each month for 20 days of fasting.   Of that, I’m enduring 10 days of the most difficult challenge in fasting and only getting the “all day fat burning” of 10 days.  A single 20 day fast has only the 2.5 initial turnover and “this sucks most” experience, and 17 days of running on nothing but fat.  17 optimum days of fasting vs 10.  2.5 of the most difficult days vs 10.  I’m not crazy.  I’m an experienced faster who has lived this out before.  Fasting is still very hard, but I’d have a much harder time running a marathon.

My goal is to lose 10 lbs of fat.  A day running on fat burns about half a pound.  I’ll lose more weight than 10lbs, but it’s the fat I’m paying attention to.  I have approximately 100lbs of fat to lose to get to a normal weight for my height.  That’s 200 days of fasting that is the  absolute soonest I could get there.  It’s been done before – but no, I’m not going to fast for a half a year.  I’m simply putting into perspective WHY I’m taking the strongest medicine and not “eat everything in moderation”.  I’m 60 – I want to be at healthy weight and live to 90.

I’m just wrapping up day 5.  I feel great.  I have my “magic knees” as my osteoarthritis appears to disappear by day 4 or 5.  In actuality it’s just that inflammation all across your body goes way down.  I’m still dealing with hunger but it comes and goes in waves.  It is less sharp than the first couple days.  I feel more tired than normal on a 40 minute walk – which lets me know to take more salt. I feel wonderful at the beginning of the walk.

On day 4 my blood glucose reading was 84, and my blood ketones 3.7 — I’m in the therapeutic level of ketosis.

- I drink mostly water with electrolytes (salt, magnesium, potassium) but I do drink non-caloric diet sodas once or twice a day.   Sue me.  I chose the boost I get from taste and pleasure over worrying about the chemicals.  I’m in deep ketosis so the sodas haven’t sabotaged my fast.

- Always consult your doctor for your own specific needs.

- I’ve built “fasting muscles” over years including knowledge both book learning and experiential understanding.
