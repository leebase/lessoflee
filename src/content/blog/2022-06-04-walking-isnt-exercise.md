Hear me out, this isn’t a post critical of walking. I love my walks. I try to get in a walk or two every day. However, exercise should have one or both of the following: elevates your heart rate, strains your muscles.

When I first started my latest health journey last year, walking did count as exercise. Walking should lower your blood sugar, but it raised mine. Your muscles release blood sugar when you are experiencing high intensity exercise. My sad state of fitness was such that my muscles were releasing glucose just because I was walking.

Your body adapts and gets better and more efficient. So while walking may be all that you can do at first, before long it’s not enough. Not enough for your exercise as it won’t raise your hear rate and doesn’t build your muscles.

It’s good in many other ways. I think walking is it’s own category of “good thing to do for your health”. Walking is great for mental health. Walking is a core life need so it’s never a bad idea to continue walking so that you CAN continue walking. The whole “use it or lose it” applies to walking.

However, I want to continue making progress in my physical health and I need to exercise in such a way as to raise my heart rate. If I “fast walk” – you know, the funny butt wiggling version of a walk – that gets my hear rate up. Today I was filled with excess energy and was compelled to go for a morning walk. I felt so good and had my running play list in my headphones and I started jogging. My version of jogging which is kind of like “squat running”. My arthritic knees are still a problem, so I was kinda crouched and running like I would on an elliptical machine. I look silly, I’m sure. But my heart rate got up which was the point.

I’m thrilled with the progress I’m making toward being able to jog/run again. Walking isn’t exercise but I love my daily walks. And I’m working to up my walking game to put it in the arena of exercise.

Saw this momma and her babies on my walk this morning. You can see that I’ve gotten faster. Now at 14:29 pace. Not breaking any speed records but I’m making progress. The next two graphs are when I mixed in jogging and my normal stroll-that-isn’t-exercise.
