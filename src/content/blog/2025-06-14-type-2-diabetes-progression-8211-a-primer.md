The beginning of diabetes is insulin resistance in your fat cells and/or liver. This causes your body to increase production of insulin.  For awhile the more insulin does the job.

But it’s a negative feedback loop.  More insulin leads to more insulin resistance leads to more insulin.  This is a disease state and the rest of your body is being harmed by the elevated insulin.  This is happening before you are labeled diabetic because the extra insulin keeps your blood glucose in check.

Until you can’t create even MORE insulin.  You pancreas is healthy at the point.  It’s a super star of insulin production.  But when it can’t create even more, your blood glucose finally starts to rise.

This is pre diabetes and then diabetes.  It’s not a switch or a line that’s crossed.  It’s a progression.  If you are pre-diabetic, you are already in the disease state.

Now your body is being harmed by both elevated insulin AND elevated glucose.  Diabetes meds NEVER do anything about the elevated insulin, at least, not in a way that heals Type 2 diabetes.

Eventually you get fatty liver and then fatty pancreas.  Both are disease states that have follow on effects for the rest of the body.

Fatty pancreas diminishes the ability to create insulin.  Now you are in big trouble.  Your fat cells are highly insulin resistant and your pancreas is nolonger making hero amounts of insulin.   You can start to look like a type 1 diabetic – where the body can’t make insulin.

This is where people say “your beta cells have been burnt out”.  I’m not saying that’s NEVER true, but it’s further down the line.  If you lose the fat from your pancreas, the beta cells recover.

Usually you are doing something synergistic.  You are losing weight, you are healing insulin resistance and losing the fat from your pancreas and it starts producing insulin again.

This is brought about by low carb, keto diets, fasting and exercise.  It can also be brought about by starvation – also known as calorie restriction.  Ok, it’s not starvation, calorie restriction is simply permanent hunger.

I rattle cages by saying Type 2 Diabetes isn’t a disease.  We all know it really is.  If you don’t do something about it.  It can be healed by changing what you eat, fasting and exercise.  So, I prefer to think of Type 2 Diabetes as a health condition that can be overcome.  I’ve done so, and I’ll do it again.
