I'm known for my smile. People compliment me all the time about it. Occasionally, someone will point out my jacked teeth. Both observations are true. I have imperfect teeth and a wonderful smile. It just depends on what someone chooses to focus on.

A Moment of Truth

This thought crossed my mind when someone commented on my fatness in a photo I posted about giving blood. It's true, I am fat. That's not a judgment, it's the truth.

The Journey of Self-Acceptance

Being fat doesn't diminish our worth. I'm open about my journey, and some people are going to focus on my fatness and not how hard I've already worked and how far I've come. You might be surprised, but I'm going to agree with the comment. I am fat. And that's not a good thing. It's why I haven't stopped working on my health after overcoming Type 2 diabetes.

Facing Health Challenges Head-On

Hitting an A1c of 11.2 was a wake-up call for me about ALL of my health. Being fat, by itself, is harming my health and potentially stealing years off my life. I realized that simply taking my meds wasn't making my diabetes any better, nor improving my overall health. It would have been much worse to NOT take the meds AND to not work on my health. That's how people lose their feet, destroy their kidneys, or go blind.

Yet, all by itself, my fatness is working against my heart health and contributes to inflammation.

Embracing the Journey

It's got to go. Some days I do better working on it than others. Getting a rude comment every now and then isn't going to hurt me nearly as much as if I spend too much time basking in my improvement instead of continuing on to get to a healthy weight.

Sometimes I look in the mirror and am thrilled at my progress. More often, I see a lot more work that needs to be done. The key is to remember that both sides of the story—the smile and the struggle—coexist, and embracing them is part of our journey towards self-improvement and personal growth.
