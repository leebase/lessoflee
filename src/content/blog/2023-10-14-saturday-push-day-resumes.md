It’s been six weeks since the micro-stroke in my right eye. Out of an abundance of caution, I refrained from high intensity exercise. The blood in my eye that obscures my vision continues to be absorbed and my ability to read is returning. Neither my eye specialist nor my primary doctor had direction to give me for this topic. So, I dropped down to moderate exercise and paid attention to how I felt, my blood pressure and my vision. What would have been great is to track the intraocular pressure, but there is no way to do that at home. Then I slowly upped the intensity making sure that all remained well.

I walk about an hour or more a day – aided by the grand dog who is staying with us for awhile. Walking is wonderful for your health, but there are benefits from pushing yourself that walking doesn’t give you. Think of what it would be like if all you did for strength training was lift 2lb dumbbells. Sure, that is better than nothing and might be challenging for some at first. But there is more muscle building power that only comes from lifting ever more heavy weights.

Do I have heart disease? Yes I do. As is common for type 2 diabetics because the same process that develops diabetes wreaks disaster on your heart and circulatory system as well…independent of elevated blood sugar. I’m still needing to take blood pressure medication and even so, it’s higher than is optimal.

I have seen improvement. My resting heart rate has fallen from the 80s into the low 60s. That’s a testament that I am making progress. So just as you lift heavier weights to keep building muscles, I push my heart rate to the highest levels once a week to accomplish a similar thing for my heart.

Another way I can see progress is how much harder it is to get my heart rate up that high. The more cardio fit you get, the lower the heart rate for a given amount of exertion. Kind of like how taking walks used to raise my blood sugar when people are told walks lower it. Well, my sedentary state was so bad, that just walking caused release of glycogen from my muscles…something that happens during intense exercise. Usually. Apparently my body thought just walking slowly around the neighborhood counted as intense exercise in the beginning of my journey.

When you start from being a couch potato, your limit is your ability to get oxygen to your cells efficiently. You get “out of breath” easily. As your cardio fitness improves, you can go harder and longer but then your muscles start complaining. And that’s a good thing. Pushing your muscles is what spurs them to rebuild themselves stronger. It’s a virtuous cycle.

You never arrive. Muscle is expensive energy wise. If you aren’t using them, the body will reduce their size and strength. I could tell the difference in only six weeks of reduced exercise intensity.

That’s life, though. Ups and downs. Trials and victories. Progress and setbacks. I’m happy to continue my journey as I have many more abilities to reclaim. I look forward to the day I start building past any level I’d ever been before.

I’m putting up a 30 second time lapse of my hour long workout. I use the Supernatural workout app in my virtual reality headset. Of course it’s moving my body that counts. The app is just a way to make it fun and engaging.

I’m also putting up a photo of two 80 year old men. Neither my father or grandfathers, nor my mother or her mother made it to 80. Only my mother made it to her 70s. The frail looking man at least made it to 80, so much respect to him. However, I not only want to make it to 80, I want to be vital and capable like the muscular man. It’s not about muscles for looks, but muscles for quality of life.

So today I pushed my limits to kick off the body processes that will build me back just a bit better as I rest and recover. I intend to continue making progress by doing more and going farther.

How much more? A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs
