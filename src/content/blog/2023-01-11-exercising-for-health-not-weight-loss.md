You may have noticed that I’ve been writing a lot about exercise lately.  With Type 2 diabetes in my rear view, I have been pressing on to reclaim my health.  I had let my health deteriorate quite a bit while working on “more important” things in my life.  I have a lot of healing to do and from there pressing on to setting myself up for a healthy, active old age.

When it comes to fitness, we often hear about the importance of losing weight or burning calories. While these goals may be important for some people, they shouldn't be the only focus when it comes to exercising. The truth is, exercising is much more than just a means to lose weight or burn calories; it's a way to improve our overall health and well-being. In fact, I don’t pay a bit of attention to calories burned and believe my weight loss efforts begin and end in the kitchen.

One of the most important benefits of exercise is that it can help to improve cardiovascular health. Regular physical activity can help to lower blood pressure, improve circulation, and reduce the risk of heart disease. Additionally, exercise can help to improve metabolic health, build muscle and reduce insulin resistance, which is key in overcoming type 2 diabetes.  I may no longer be a type 2 diabetic, but I have a long way to go in healing the underlying factors of metabolic syndrome….of which type 2 diabetes is just a late stage aspect.

Exercise can also help to strengthen the muscles and bones, which can help to prevent falls and injuries as we age. With strengthened muscles, we also maintain our ability to perform daily tasks and enjoy physical activities in old age.

In addition to these physical benefits, exercise also has a number of mental health benefits. Regular physical activity can help to reduce stress, anxiety, and depression. It can also help to improve our mood, self-esteem, and overall sense of well-being.

When it comes to exercise, one of the most important things is to find something that you enjoy. Whether it's going for a walk, cycling, swimming, or playing a sport, the key is to find an activity that you look forward to and that you can stick to in the long-term. That way, you're more likely to stick with it and see the health benefits.  I love my Virtual Reality workouts with the Supernatural app.  What I do isn’t what someone else should do.  But finding something you enjoy is key.

As the famous fitness expert, Jack LaLanne once said, "Exercise is king, nutrition is queen. Together you have a kingdom." and also another quote from him "The only way you can hurt the body is not use it. Inactivity is the killer and, remember, it's never too late". Also, Herman Ponzer's "Burn" book quotes "Exercise is the key to unlock the full potential of your body's ability to burn fat, build muscle, and improve overall health."   He wrote that after doing a brilliant job of establishing the exercise actually makes little difference in the overall “burning of calories”.

So, next time you think about exercise, remember that it's not just about losing weight or burning calories. It's about improving your overall health and well-being, both physically and mentally. The benefits include improving metabolic health, building muscle, reducing insulin resistance and maintaining the ability to perform daily tasks and enjoy physical activities in old age. And remember, find something that you enjoy and stick with it, and you'll be sure to see the benefits.
