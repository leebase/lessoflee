Most of us type 2 diabetics have other health issues, particularly heart disease. We hear “diabetes causes….” when the reality is “chronically elevated insulin causes…”. By the time over eating of carbs and the elevated insulin to handle them causes diabetes…it’s already been causing damage in many other areas of the body, including the heart.

I have heart disease. My coronary artery calcium score (CAC) is very high…top 80% high. This is a direct measure of calcium plaque building up in the arteries.

Now, to the topic of the post. I am tempted to try and “lower my CAC score”. Most sources of information (including my doctor) say that you can’t. The best you can do is slow the progression. I am hearing that some people have indeed lowered their scores.

It doesn’t matter. What I want is a healthy heart, not a better score. What it takes to improve heart health is not a mystery.

Eat a low insulin diet. It’s the chronically elevated insulin driving the disease process. Thus, eat as few carbs as you can. Also, fasting helps lower insulin. Extended fasting offers further benefits – and I do this once a month.

Exercise the heart. This is why I am not satisfied with merely taking a relaxing walk through the neighborhood. I want to get my heart rate up. I want my blood flow to increase and the arteries to be able to expand. The heart, like your biceps, gets stronger and better with use.

As always – check with your doctor. I did. He’s on board with me engaging in intense exercise. And I’ve been doing it for many months now.

Heart health is why I don’t use my hard earned improvement in blood sugar control and improved insulin sensitivity to eat more of my favorite foods. I can, and I do, eat them from time to time. I know that I have a non-diabetic's response to those occasional indulgences. And yet, there is more health to reclaim than just reversing type 2 diabetes. I want my heart health to continue to improve.

Getting to a healthy weight is important for heart health. I’ve made a lot of progress in this. I have much more work to do. I have a good 50lbs more to lose. Even then I’d still be over weight…at 215lbs. I just can’t conceive of getting below 190 which is what the BMI tables say I is what I should weigh.

I know I have improved my heart health. I can see it in my resting heart rate, which is in the 50s. I can see it in how much lower my heart rate is when walking and how much faster my heart rate recovers after intense effort. Still waiting to see improvement in my blood pressure.

It’s important to get various health indicators: A1c, blood pressure, lipid panel, CAC score, weight, and body measurements to name a few. Use the indicators to motivate the pursuit of health. Don’t be enticed to “chasing to the numbers”.
