I strive to be transparent with the victories and setbacks on my health journey. Today falls into the latter category, as I encountered an unexpected challenge on day 9 of my fasting journey due to a sudden drop in blood pressure. Thankfully, I’m okay, and this experience has provided valuable lessons and areas for improvement that I plan to address in the future.

The day began without the benefit of a good night's sleep, leading me to leave church early due to unusual fatigue, particularly in the back of my neck. After taking some salt and mineral tablets and attempting a short walk with my dog, my condition did not improve. Considering the possibility of ending the fast, I opted for some bone broth to see if it would help. During this time, I noted my blood pressure was significantly lower than usual—unfamiliar territory for someone accustomed to high blood pressure. When reaching for a second cup of broth, I felt faint and subsequently fell, though I did not lose consciousness. The fall, while not severe, left me with a few aches and pointed clearly to my low blood pressure as the culprit.

I immediately sat down and had my wife make some eggs. I drank 2 12oz bottles of water with LMNT electrolytes. My blood pressure was rising within a half hour. By three hours I was back into the normal range.

I’ve always said that one should end a fast if you feel bad. I didn’t think I was “pushing through”, but apparently I was. I now have a reference point for how I feel when my blood pressure goes low and will be monitoring more closely in the future.

I am contacting my doctor tomorrow to discuss this. He knows all about my fasting, and gives me his input.

I will take some time to think through this experience. There will be lessons to learn and improvements to how I fast going forward. This is as it should be. I’ve talked about how I lifted weights one time in my 30s in a stupid way and was so wrecked and sore the next day that I never lifted weights again for 20 years. If you exercise and injure yourself, you shouldn’t conclude that exercise isn’t for you. You learn the lessons on how to do so safely in the future.

And that is my plan. This hasn’t happened to me before in the years I’ve been fasting. But I don’t want it to happen again, so I will work with my doctor to learn the lessons and make any needed improvements.

The fact that I’m okay does not detract from the seriousness of the incident. I proceed with a mindset that underscores the gravity of taking care of one’s health, acknowledging that every step in this journey offers an opportunity for growth and understanding.

I am prepared for the “I told you so’s”. Anything can be done poorly and I am convinced that doing nothing is far more dangerous. But neither am I unwilling to own up to mistakes. This was preventable. I will learn and do better.

Despite the setback, I did my body good today and through this fast. I hope you do your body good.

p.s.  Always consult your own health team for your specific needs
