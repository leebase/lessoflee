I have glaucoma and it’s progressively destroying my eye sight.  In the last year I have gone from one med to two.  After a few months of taking two meds my eyes became bloodshot and itchy.  I thought I had pink eye, and went to the eye doctor.  It was a side effect of the second and stronger medication.  My eye pressure went up, and my eyes were red and sore all the time.  I tried adjusting what I took and my glaucoma specialist gave me a good talking too.  “You don’t decide, you let me decide”.  “But my eyes are red and itch”.  “We will change your meds again as your eye nerve is getting worse”.  How helpless it feels to see your health deteriorating and your only hope is for this or that med to SLOW DOWN the process of going blind.    For all my research and talking to eye specialists, there is nothing else to do.   As I drove home from my latest eye appointment, I reflected on this and how happy I am that I am not trapped in med dependent Type 2 diabetes.

I was on the same “take meds, more meds, even more meds” with Type 2 diabetes as well.  After all, it’s a progressively degenerative disease.  First was Metformin, then Metformin plus Glipizide.  A couple years and one pandemic later, it was Metformin, Glipizide, the $1000/month Trulicity and at my next appointment the doctor was going to put me on insulin as well.  My HbA1c was 11.2 even on all the meds.  Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed at an HbA1c of 6.5 – so the doctor was having to keep upgrading the amount and strength of the meds as I headed toward any or all of the consequences of this devastating disease:  heart attack, kidney failure, blindness, amputation.   Fortunately, there was indeed something I could do to actually improve my health and not just helplessly devolve into terrible outcomes.   When I left the diabetes specialist that day, April 20 2021, the fear of going blind finally kicked me into action.

I read the book The Diabetes Code by Dr Jason Fung and learned the simple (but hard) changes that could reverse my Type 2 Diabetes.   Cut sugar out of my diet (keto, NSNG), intermittent fasting and exercise.  In three months my blood sugar control had improved to where the doctor removed all of my medications.  Three years later and I remain free of being dependent on drugs who’s best case is to delay terrible health outcomes.   I only wish there was a similar protocol that could undo my glaucoma.

I didn’t just stop taking meds, I improved my health such that I didn’t need them any more.  If my diet and exercise and fasting turn out to help my eyes, that would be wonderful and I’d love to not simply degenerate into blindness.  So far, nothing I’ve read and none of the doctors have given me any hope that this particular health problem is reversible.  

I can’t stress enough how important it is to do all you can do to regain your metabolic health.  It is so much better to not need medications to live.  I haven’t even addressed how medications can conflict.  I saw my mother (and her doctors) having to decide to take the med that helped the heart but hurt the kidneys or helped the kidneys at the expense of the heart.  Go for health, go for all the health you can build.  Take the meds if you need them, of course.  I’m certainly choosing to go blind more slowly than quickly.  It’s still terrifying.  Even if meds were free (and for some they are) – you can’t get health from medication.  

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs.  

p.p.s. Educate yourself to fully participate in your health strategy.
