I started writing solely for my own journey.  My first post was just after my A1C hit 11.2, the $1000/mo Trulicity was added to the Metformin and Glipizide I’d been taking for years.  At my next appointment, they were going to start me on insulin.  I wrote as a tool of accountability, as I had done 10 years before when losing weight was my only need and goal.  I knew there would be hard work and I could only hope that there would be results.   And the results came.

As the results came, it became clear that some people had started paying attention to my journey.  I love to hear the “you inspire me” – particularly when it’s accompanied by “and so I made my own changes and my health improved”!   Congrats to every one of you.

Along the way I also heard, many times, why not just take the meds and have a cookie or two?  If the meds get your blood sugar under control, what’s the point of all that hard work?  It’s a reasonable question.  Why stop eating or at least greatly reduce all your favorite foods?  Why is it that kale is good for you and ice cream isn’t?  It’s not fair.  And who has time to do all that exercise?  Lee isn’t even satisfied with walking free from a cane.  Look at him push and push to get his heart to the max.  Taking a pill is just fine.

And it is.  Seriously, it truly is.  

Until it isn’t.

Right now I’m dealing with an abscess on my back.  It’s like I have this mound of “something” (puss, white blood cells, baby spiders?) growing in my back.  It’s about the size of half a tennis ball.  It wouldn’t pop.  My skin is taught and painful.  It’s uncomfortable to sit and to lay down.  After a couple weeks, I go to immediate care to get it drained.  They can’t but give me antibiotics.  After several days and the “tennis ball” continues to grow.   So I call my regular doctor who can’t see me for a week.   I ask as for ANY doctor who can see me soonest — BUT — they need to be able to drain the abscess.  I don’t need another doctor to say what the first already told me.  And @#$#@$@# I go to a new doctor and he can’t drain it.  I have to setup a surgical consult and it’s going to take a week to get to that.  And what to you bet that he’ll say “oh, you need more than I can do in my office, we’ll set up a surgery a month from now”.

So frustrating.  I’m in pain now.  I know what needs to be done now.  I just need a doctor to do it.  I hate having to deal with the medical establishment!

Which – brings me to the topic of this post.  It is worth every hard thing I’ve had to do to rebuild my health.  The healthier I am — the FEWER times I need to deal with doctors and hospitals.  The healthier I am, the fewer worries I have about this med for one condition interfering with another med to a different condition.   Not to mention regaining my ability to walk and even run!  I sleep better, feel better, am mentally better.  

We sell ourselves short when we don’t do what we can and put our faith in medications.  Mind you, I take my meds when I need them, but there’s a LOT that can be done to get better.  And yes, it’s hard.  But worth it.

Start believing in your own worth.  You are worth eating the foods that will bring you health.  Whole, natural foods.  And if you are a diabetic, whole natural foods that have no or very low carbohydrates.  Your health is worth saying no to that highly processed commercial “food like stuffs” – no matter how delicious they taste or how cheap they are.  You are worth getting outside and taking a walk.  You are worth doing more than walking when you can.  You are worth lifting a heavy weight. 

How much are you worth?    More. 

How much more?  A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs.  

p.p.s. Educate yourself to fully participate in your health strategy.
