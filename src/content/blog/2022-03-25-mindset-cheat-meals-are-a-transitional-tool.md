We all know the cheat meal. “Eat good” all week and allow yourself one meal a week to “cheat on your diet”.

I think cheat meals are a great tool – for a while. It’s hard to change your eating, and knowing “in a little while I get to eat something I really like” helps you stick to your “diet”.

However, I think mindset needs to change over time. If you really hate what you are eating…a cheat meal isn’t going to work long term. Invest the time in learning to to cook meals you find delicious that are in line with healthy eating. Then you will be back to enjoying your every day food with little need to cheat.

Now comes the concept of the “meal of indulgence”. This is different than cheating. It’s part of your plan. There are foods you like that aren’t healthy for you to eat all the time. But the occasional indulgence is fine and won’t set back your health.

I save my “meals of indulgence” for family events. Birthday's, anniversaries, holidays. It’s nice to be able to enjoy time with family and loosen up the standards for a desert or a meal.

For us type 2 diabetics (current or former) we have an individual level of tolerance for carbs. How often we indulge and how much we eat of our treats is gated by our specific level of insulin resistance. That is…if we wish to maintain our health, our insulin sensitivity.

Still…the occasional indulgence is a mental health good practice. It’s part of the plan. Cheating – cheating is a transitional tool.

PS – some people have a far stronger addiction response to their forbidden foods and it may not be wise for them to ever indulge.
