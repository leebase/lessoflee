As someone who has gone through the journey of getting healthy through diet and exercise, I can confidently say that there is no downside to improving your health. For years, I relied solely on medication to manage my chronic conditions, but I realized that medication was not enough to truly achieve optimal health and well-being.

I understand that when people hear about the benefits of lifestyle changes, the first thing they say is "that doesn't work for everyone." But what does "work" really mean? Can anyone honestly say that cutting out junk food, processed foods, and sugary drinks wouldn't improve their health? Sure, these foods may be convenient, taste good, and are everywhere, but they are making us sick. We should be outraged that these foods exist, not that we have to stop eating them.

Will everyone who adopts a whole foods, low-carb, nutrient-rich diet get off their medication? Maybe not, but is there any doubt that real food that doesn't come with a barcode and that we've eaten for thousands of years will lead to better health? And what is the downside to better health?

Similarly, will everyone who engages in regular exercise, including walking and strength training, get off all their medication? Probably not, but whose life wouldn't improve with better mobility and muscle tone? What is the downside to working hard and consistently for years on end, even if it doesn't cure all of your ills?

And let's not forget about the importance of getting enough sleep and managing stress. What is the downside to taking care of ourselves in these ways?

I started working hard to improve my health almost two years ago, and while I have successfully gotten off diabetes medication and lost 60 pounds, my blood pressure is still in the hypertension stage 1, and I still need and take blood pressure medication. But does that mean that my hard work hasn't "worked"? Absolutely not.

Improving my diet and exercise routine has brought so much positivity into my life, including putting type 2 diabetes into remission, decreased arthritis pain, the regaining of my abilities to walk and run, and so much more. And even though I still need medication, I know that I am doing everything in my power to live my healthiest life.

So, to those who say that lifestyle changes don't work for everyone, I say that they have misunderstood what it means to get healthier. Even if getting healthier doesn't lead to getting off medication, there is still so much to gain and nothing to lose.

I encourage everyone to do the work to get as healthy as they can. There is no downside to improving your diet and exercise routine, getting better sleep, and managing stress. And while medication can be a crucial part of managing chronic conditions, it is essential not to rely solely on medication and to work towards true health and well-being through lifestyle changes. Let's all strive to live our healthiest lives and encourage those around us to do the same.
