I’ve got good news and bad news from my labs. Mostly excellent news, which I’ll get to later. First, the bad news — and what’s actually good about the bad news.

My fasting insulin came in at 30. Thirty! The chart says that’s the “high end” of normal… but let’s be real: it’s nowhere near healthy. Ideally, fasting insulin should be under 10. So what does 30 mean?

It means I remain highly insulin resistant.

⸻

The Rice Experiment

I’d been fasting or carnivore for six weeks. My insulin was low. I’d lost 30 pounds and brought my glucose under control. So I decided it was time for an experiment:  How much of my glucose control is actual healing—and how much is simple carb avoidance?

(Not knocking carb avoidance. It’s a powerful tool. But the long-term goal is to heal insulin resistance.)

So I ate one bowl of rice — 3/4 cup cooked, about 150g. Not even a big bowl.

My glucose rose and fell in a very encouraging way. But glucose isn’t the whole story. It isn’t even the main problem. Glucose is the late-stage symptom of insulin resistance syndrome (metabolic syndrome).

The real question was: How much insulin did my body have to produce to control that small bowl of rice?

Answer: a crap ton.

This wasn’t my fasting insulin while carnivore or after extended fasting. This was 14 hours after a single bowl of rice — and my fasting insulin was still 30.

And that is exactly why I’m fasting and eating carnivore.

⸻

Reading the Signs

My pancreas clearly works fine — but it’s having to work HARD to handle something as simple as a bowl of rice.

And honestly? I didn’t need this lab to know I’m deeply insulin resistant.

I regained 85 pounds in one year. That doesn’t “just happen.” That happens because you have a severe metabolic problem.

Another sign? Normal arms. Normal legs. Huge belly. That’s insulin resistance in physical form.

Now I’ve got the lab numbers to complete the picture.

⸻

The Good News

I can do something about this. I am doing something about this.

Getting sugar control back in just a few weeks is huge. Losing 10% of my body weight in seven weeks is huge. And yes, ironically, I’m still huge — 310 pounds, heading for 185 (what the BMI chart calls healthy for my height). But that’s the point:

My fat is actually a good proxy for the remaining work to heal insulin resistance. My visceral fat — the stuff around the middle, in and around the organs — is both the result of IR and the driver of more inflammation and more IR. It’s the vicious cycle I’m breaking.

And this is why I’m using the two most powerful tools available:

• Extended fasting

• Carnivore / zero-carb eating (okay, trivial carbs)

My rhythm is simple: 1 week fasting → 2 weeks carnivore → repeat And it’s working wonderfully.

Some people chastise me for being too extreme. But look again at what ONE BOWL OF RICE required from my body. Moderation was never going to work for me at this level of IR. Garden-variety keto wasn’t enough either — great for glucose control, not nearly enough to actually heal insulin resistance.

⸻

Why This Protocol Makes Sense

The only way to heal insulin resistance is to give the body time in low-insulin states. Extended fasting is the most effective way. Carnivore (or near-zero carb) is the second. Not everyone needs this level of intervention. Not everyone is as insulin resistant as I am. But for me? It’s the right tool for the right battle.

And the good news is this: healing has already begun.

It wasn’t long ago that even my glucose wouldn’t come down. I spent weeks trying to get one reading under 200. I watched my glucose shoot over 400 from a cheeseburger and fries.

That’s the world I came from. Look where I am now.

There’s still a lot of work ahead — but I’m excited about where I’ll be a year from now.

⸻

How much more? Sometimes the answer is: A little more.

And sometimes the answer has to be: As much as it takes to get the job done.
