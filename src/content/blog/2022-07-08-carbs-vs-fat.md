The Carnivore Code book and my month of trying a carnivore diet has transformed how I see food. In one month my weight loss stall was broken (lost 15lbs), and my HDL and Triglycerides came into the normal range for the first time.

I didn’t intend on sticking with the diet beyond the 30 days, but I can’t ignore the significant improvement in my health.

Last night’s dinner was roasted beef with potatoes, carrots and onions. The potatoes are already on my “shouldn’t eat those” list. But I now also realized that the nutrition isn’t solely in the vegetables. How can I eat this wonderful “meat juice” without pouring it over potatoes? Well, I poured it into a cup. A nice beef broth filled with collagen and fat from the slow cooked meat. Collagen is good for skin, hair and knee joints etc.

I used to cut off the fat and feed it to the dog. Ewww…fat. Now I eat the fat and the gristle understanding that there are uses for it in my body. The fiber in the carrot just passes through as none of my body is made of fiber. But the protein and the fat – everything in the body is made of protein and fat.

“But you need those carbs for energy”! Umm….tell me again about how fat is bad because it has twice as much calories per gram as carbohydrates or proteins? So, energy is good for you if it comes from carbs and bad for you if it comes from fat. This meal lasted me 19 hours until the next.

Ah, but there is a difference. Carbohydrates spike your insulin much more than either proteins or fats. Fats cause the least rise in insulin. And for the type 2 diabetic, carbs spike your blood sugar where fat does not. So – one energy type makes your diabetes worse and the other does not. It’s not hard to chose when you come to understand what’s going on.

Oh…and protein and fat satiate you for hours after. Carbs put you on a hunger roller coaster.

Losing weight is hard enough. You have to eat less than you burn. Yep – even folks on keto need to eat less than they burn. The difference is eating less because your are full and don’t feel the need to eat — and needing will power 24&#215;7 to eat less. Hunger wins over time.

Tonight’s dinner was hamburgers and chicken topped with cheese and bacon. Some veggies for color and taste, and delicious mushrooms. Not a perfect carnivore meal, but that’s not my goal. My goal is to promote my continued health recovery. For me, I have to be pretty close to zero carbs. At least for now at my current level of insulin resistance.
