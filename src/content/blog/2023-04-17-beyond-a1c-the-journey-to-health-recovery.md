Two years ago, my A1c level was a staggering 11.2, and I relied on three medications to manage my type 2 diabetes. Today, my A1c has dropped to 5.2, and I no longer need any medications. On paper, it appears as if I've fully recovered – but the journey to complete health doesn't end with the normalization of blood sugar levels. In this blog post, I share my experience and emphasize the importance of a comprehensive approach to managing type 2 diabetes and overall health.

[![](~/assets/posts/2023-04-17-beyond-a1c-the-journey-to-health-recovery/image.jpg)](https://lessoflee.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/image.jpg)

**The Limitations of Medications and the Importance of Lifestyle Changes**

Contrary to what some might assume, I am not "anti-drug" or "anti-doctor." However, I've come to realize that type 2 diabetes is more than just a disease characterized by high blood sugar levels; it's a state of declining health caused by chronically elevated insulin levels. Medications can treat symptoms, but they don't give you health. By making lifestyle changes, I was able to see improvements in my osteoarthritis, balance, and overall well-being.

**Personal Health Improvements Beyond A1c**

Medications can treat symptoms, but they don’t give you health.  I was amazed to see improvement in my osteoarthritis.  I had to walk with a cane and had difficulties walking down stairs.  I worried about falling in the shower and had to pee sitting down as my balance was that bad.  I now can run a 5k, and have much better balance and stairs don’t trouble me.

How is this related to diabetes?  Changing my diet and strength training that I did for my diabetes helped in these other areas as well.  Something a pill or a shot would never do.  I also no longer have heart burn when I used to pop Pepcids all day long.  I don’t snore and I sleep better. I have far fewer headaches.  My knees aren’t cured buy I have far less pain.  I no longer take pain meds or creams.  

I used too…of course.  A pill for this and a cream for that.  It seriously didn’t occur to me that I was simply declining in my health because I wasn’t eating, moving and sleeping in a health promoting way.  I used to consider people “health nuts” that were careful about what they ate and exercised.  For a guy who thinks of himself as being pretty smart – that was VERY stupid of me. 

I left the doctor on that day, 2 years ago, determined I was going to do whatever it took to get my health back.  My ACTUAL health, not a litany of pills, shots and creams that simply handled symptoms.

**Blood Sugar Isn’t the Only Problem**

If you are taking drugs to control your blood sugar, you are helping yourself greatly compared to those who don’t take care of their blood sugar:

Health complications from high blood sugar levels (hyperglycemia) in diabetes:

- Nerve damage (neuropathy): Prolonged exposure to high blood sugar can damage nerves throughout the body.

- Kidney damage (nephropathy): High blood sugar can cause damage to the filtering units in the kidneys, potentially leading to kidney failure.

- Eye damage (retinopathy): High blood sugar can damage the blood vessels in the retina, leading to vision loss or even blindness.

- Foot damage: Nerve damage and poor circulation in the feet can lead to serious infections, ulcers, and sometimes amputation.

- Skin conditions: High blood sugar can cause various skin conditions, including bacterial and fungal infections.

- Hearing impairment: Diabetes may increase the risk of hearing problems.

- Alzheimer's disease: Type 2 diabetes may increase the risk of Alzheimer's disease and other dementias.

But – here is a list of all the health complications that type 2 diabetics get – that aren’t about your blood sugar, but about the chronically elevated insulin.  These disease processes are still going on if all you’ve done is lower your blood sugar with meds or insulin.  This list is why I haven’t stopped working on my health even though I’m allegedly in the A1c “green zone”.

Health complications associated with chronically elevated insulin levels (hyperinsulinemia) before type 2 diabetes occurs:

- Cardiovascular disease: Increased risk of coronary artery disease, heart attack, and stroke due to inflammation, atherosclerosis, and other factors.

- Weight gain and obesity: Elevated insulin levels can lead to weight gain by promoting fat storage and reducing fat breakdown.

- Metabolic syndrome: A cluster of conditions including high blood pressure, high blood sugar, excess body fat around the waist, and abnormal cholesterol levels.

- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS): Hyperinsulinemia can contribute to hormonal imbalances in women, causing irregular periods, acne, and excess hair growth.

- Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD): Excess insulin can cause fat to accumulate in the liver, leading to inflammation and liver damage.

- Hypertension: High insulin levels can cause blood vessels to constrict, leading to high blood pressure.

- Increased cancer risk: Elevated insulin levels have been linked to an increased risk of certain cancers, including breast, colon, and pancreatic cancer.

Again, managing both blood sugar and insulin levels is crucial for reducing the risk of these health complications. Lifestyle changes such as a healthy diet, regular exercise, and maintaining a healthy weight can help improve insulin sensitivity and prevent further complications.

**Conclusion: Taking Control of Your Health**

My journey to regain my health is far from over. I still have a lot of work to do, and that involves feeding myself healthy foods, moving in healthy ways, and managing stress and sleep effectively. When I asked my doctor why the approach I took isn't the standard treatment, she explained that not everyone is willing to make the necessary changes. However, I firmly believe that more people would take control of their health if they knew they could reclaim it through lifestyle changes and not just rely on medications. Remember, the journey to health recovery goes beyond A1c – it's about taking a holistic approach to managing diabetes and overall well-being.
