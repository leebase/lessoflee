I’ve embarked on a 21 day water only* fast.  I have completed day 8 and met with my doctor today.  All is well.

It’s been tough – I won’t minimize the challenge.  I will say, though, that the very difficulty of accomplishing this task is part of the appeal and the payoff.  It’s the same reason some run a marathon – because it’s hard and you develop discipline and prove you can tackle other hard challenges in life.

Knowing that the mental challenge is much more difficult than the physical hunger once you get past the first couple days, I created a list of 100 things to keep in me motivated to continue:  [https://lessoflee.wordpress.com/keep-fasting-helps/](https://lessoflee.wordpress.com/keep-fasting-helps/)

One such is:  Hunger is a wave, I am the shore.  The hunger comes and goes, but I remain.  Another I like is: I’m earning my future self’s gratitude.    Keeping in mind the good I’m doing for my body – despite how hard it is.  The challenge will end, and that version of Lee will have the benefits – while the struggle will be but memory.

I had my blood work done on day 6 and met with the doctor today to discuss.  All is well. My liver and kidneys aren’t having any problems and my electrolytes are within range.  I’m also monitoring my blood pressure as I went low the last long fast I did and feinted on day 9.  I had donated blood on day 2 of the fast – a lesson learned that I won’t repeat.  I also now know what low blood pressure feels like – and I’m monitoring.  This time, my bp is running a bit high.  I have an infection in my back that might be the cause of that.  The doctor prescribed an antibiotic and it has no bearing on the fast.

My “magic knees” started appearing on day 3 and by day 5 it’s like I don’t have osteoarthritis at all.  Feels WONDERFUL.  I used to walk with a cane and struggle with stairs.  Those days are behind me, but I’m still affected by arthritis.  I no longer need pain meds or lotions and potions either.   But I walk slow and get up and down gingerly.  With my magic knees I feel like doing jumping jacks!

I was cold the first three days, but that’s normal.  I’ve had a quesy stomach the last couple days too.  I’ve drank one cup of bone broth each day and that feels good on the tummy.

Why keep going?  I certainly don’t HAVE too.  I GET too. Taking 2.5 or more days to get into full fat burning mode is a real incentive to keep going once I’ve gotten past it.  Each day at this point is the same level of difficulty.  It’s not getting harder.  From what I’ve read, I’ll soon experience the hunger going away completely.

My blood glucose is in the 80’s — normal.   My ketones are nice and high.  I’m in the therapeutic zone – the place where folks suffering from epilepsy try to get to, to heal.  I know my body is cleaning out misshapen, old and dead proteins via autophagy, building up the immune system, new brain cells and more.  And I’m consuming about a half pound of fat each day.  I have 200 days worth of excess fat, so I’m keeping going.

Mentally and emotionally I feel good.  I haven’t been sleeping as long, but I don’t feel tired.  I haven’t felt in extra mental clarity – but then again, I feel mentally clear normally.

Thanks to all who are encouraging me and cheering me on.  Thanks also to those who are worried for me or have serious doubts, questions even challenges.   Those help me, so bring them on and I’ll answer.

- I drink mostly water with electrolytes (salt, magnesium, potassium) but I do drink non-caloric diet sodas once or twice a day.   Sue me.  I chose the boost I get from taste and pleasure over worrying about the chemicals.  I’m in deep ketosis so the sodas haven’t sabotaged my fast.

- Always consult your doctor for your own specific needs.

- I’ve built “fasting muscles” over years including knowledge both book learning and experiential understanding.
