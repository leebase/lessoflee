If you spend five minutes on "Health Twitter," you’ll see a war being fought.  On one side, you have the Insulin Model heavyweights (Dr. Jason Fung, Dr. Ben Bikman, Dr. Pradip Jamnadas). Their stance: Carbohydrates spike insulin; chronic high insulin drives resistance. Stop eating carbs, and you fix the problem.

On the other side, you have the Mastering Diabetes crew (Cyrus Khambatta, Robby Barbaro). Their stance: Saturated fat gums up the "lock" on your cells, preventing insulin from working. Stop eating fat, and you fix the problem. Here is the inconvenient truth that drives purists crazy: They are both right.  Or to be more precise – both plans work.  As each camp tends to claim the other side is “wrong” – they can’t both be “right”.  But – way too many testimonies of success on both sides for denial of this truth – Type 2 Diabetes can be reversed, and by more ways than just one.  Both camps are successfully reversing Type 2 Diabetes (T2D) every day. But how can two diametrically opposite protocols achieve the same result?

The "Overflowing Sink" Theory

To understand why both work, we have to look at T2D not as a "carb problem" or a "fat problem," but as an energy toxicity problem. Imagine your cells are a sink. In T2D, the sink is overflowing.

 * The Insulin Model (Low Carb/Keto) turns off the Hot Water tap (Glucose/Carbs).

 * The Mastering Diabetes Model (Low Fat/WFPB) turns off the Cold Water tap (Dietary Fats).

When you shut off one tap completely, the drain (your metabolism) can finally catch up. The water level drops. Sensitivity is restored.

Where The Sides Get It Wrong The animosity between these camps comes from a fundamental misunderstanding of the other side’s mechanics.

 * What the Low-Fat Camp Gets Wrong: They claim that "Low Carb causes insulin resistance" because if a keto dieter eats a banana, their blood sugar spikes. That isn't pathology; that’s physiological insulin resistance (adaptation). The muscle cells are sparing glucose for the brain because they are happily burning fat. It is a temporary, functional state, not a disease. Carnivore/Keto DOES NOT cause or worsen insulin resistance.

 * What the Low-Carb Camp Gets Wrong: They often dismiss the Mastering Diabetes success solely as "starvation" or "calorie restriction." While calorie reduction plays a role, the bio-chemistry of Intramyocellular Lipids (IMCL) is real. If you drastically reduce fat intake (to <15% of calories), you clear the lipid droplets inside muscle cells that interfere with insulin signaling. It does work, even if the carbs are high—provided the fat is near zero.  

The Verdict: Which Plan is For You?

Both paths lead to the same destination: a healed metabolism. The "best" diet is simply the one you can stick to for the duration needed to heal.  And no matter which path taken, you can have SOME amount of extra flexibility but you can never go back to the Standard American Diet with its highly processed food like substances and seed oils.  Such a diet made you sick in the first place and will make you sick again.

Choose the Insulin Model (Keto/Fung) if:

 * Physiology: You are hyper-insulinemic and carry lots of belly fat.

 * Palate: You enjoy savory foods, meats, eggs, and butter.

 * Psychology: You prefer the feeling of satiety (fullness) and stable energy that comes from fat adaptation. You hate being hungry.

 * Lifestyle: You are okay with skipping meals (Intermittent Fasting fits naturally here).

Choose Mastering Diabetes (Low-Fat/Plant-Based) if:

 * Physiology: You are perhaps "skinny fat" or reactive to dietary fats.

 * Palate: You love fruit, potatoes, rice, and grains and can't imagine life without them.

 * Psychology: You are disciplined enough to avoid all oils, nuts, and animal products (one "cheat" day with fat ruins the mechanism).

 * Lifestyle: You have ethical concerns regarding animal agriculture.

The Bottom Line

Don't get caught in the dogma. The enemy of metabolic health is the combination of high carbs and high fats (the Standard American Diet). This is the "swamp" where disease breeds. Pick a mountain. Climb it. Just don't camp out in the swamp.

I’m not saying both ways of eating are “the same”.  They are not.  There are other reasons why one might choose one approach over another.  I’m not ending the diet debates.  What I am doing is recognizing the obvious truth – one can overcome Type 2 diabetes by changing one’s diet and lifestyle.  There are two diametrically opposed plans – and both work.  One might be right for you or better for you than the other.  And these aren’t the only two.  Look into the Twinkie Diet where a professor proved he could reverse diabetes eating nothing but junk food and candy.  If you eat few enough calories – you can reverse this disease.  You’ll get sick and die of malnutrition eventually on the Twinkie diet, but only after you resolve your diabetes.  So – while CICO works, eating just anything isn’t equivalent to healthy even if it does bring your blood glucose under control.
