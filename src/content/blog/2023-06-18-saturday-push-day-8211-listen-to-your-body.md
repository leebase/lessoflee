Sometimes your body has different ideas than your goals. I went out to run a 5k and push myself that bit faster. About a kilometer in, I got some cramping in my shin. Do I push through? I had 4 kilometers to go. I ran for a bit longer and then I stopped and walked.

Sure, it’s a push day but I didn’t want to injure myself. I walked while my leg felt tight. After about another kilometer my leg was feeling fine and I arrived at “the hill”. So I sprinted up the hill. Of course, I couldn’t make it up the entire hill at a sprint, but I got my heart rate up around 95% of max. I did a couple more sprints on the walk back home.

I am about 2 miles away from my home at the farthest point. One of the advantages is that, no matter what, I can’t quit until I get back home. The last 20 minutes of walking was a push all by itself. I would have stopped, but, sitting in a field isn’t my idea of a fun morning.

I’m doing my weekly heart rate stresses, but I need to get back to strength training as well. I’ve been lax all year. I’ve also had too many “off plan” eating meals. You can’t really claim to be stalled when you know you are indulging too often.

Progress isn’t a one way journey. Would be nice if it was.

Still, I did my body good today by pushing myself. I also did my body good by stopping when an unexpected pain flared up. Don’t let your ego lead you to make bad health decisions. Pay attention to your body’s feedback.

You can do this too. Results and benefits don’t happen in a day. They do come, and life is so much better with health. Do more, and go farther.

How much more? A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.
