I’ve never failed so well before.  I embarked on a 21 day fast that I ended after 9 days.  Had I succeeded I would have extended my longest fast from 14 days to 21.  My number one goal was to lose 10lbs of fat (not weight but fat).  My second ambition was to get back on track with my discipline that I had lost over the last year.  How bad?  I regained all 85lbs I had worked so hard to lose and once again became diabetic with an A1c of 7.1 instead of the 5.4 I had achieved.  I had eaten myself sick.  I built the discipline and made the dietary changes, along with exercise to eat myself out of the disease of Type 2 diabetes.  For a couple years I remained free of Type 2 and continued building my body.  Then in an apparent blink of an eye, old Lee, with old eating, and the evaporation of my discipline came back.  I ate myself sick again.

Have I gotten your attention?  One of my mantras is that you can’t fail a fast.  And while that’s always true, this time is spectacularly so. Had I gone 21 days, I would have lost maybe 30 lbs of weight and gone down a size in clothes.  I didn’t get that win.  I did lose approximately 12 lbs after the weight bounce back.  4 to 5 of that will be fat loss. Still quite nice.  The big news is I feel back on track with my discipline.  I’ve chosen to try BBBE (beef, bacon, butter and eggs) for a solid month as my diet.  I’m 4 days in and have had challenges that I’d been falling for over the past year but haven’t since the fast.

The result is that I’m still in ketosis, and my blood glucose is fantastic.  My last A1c was 7.1 which corresponds to an average blood glucose level of 154.  My reading 3 hours after breakfast was 104 today.  That’s not the 84 readings I was getting during the fast, but if I keep this up another month I’ll be below the diabetic level and another two will get me back to a normal A1c.

So far I’ve kept my magic knees.  I don’t know if this is holdover from the fast or if I remain carnivore these wonderful knees will become my new normal.  Knee arthritis is described as one’s knees wearing out.  My ortho wanted to replace mine years ago.  I can’t speak for everyone else, but clearly mine are metabolic dysfunction and inflammation based.  I’m still over 300lbs and yet they feel great, it’s not that I lost weight and fixed them.

It’s only been 4 days so I don’t want to over sell my status.  But I can testify that “inside my brain” I feel confident, dedicated, back in the battle.  This is the mind I had those first two and a half years of my journey.  

Looking forward: I anticipate losing weight from the carnivore eating plan.  It’s too soon to see that yet.  I can say that adding butter really does make it easier to feel satiated. I might eat more at one meal, but without effort, I’m eating fewer over all across the day.  It’s a very limited diet, yet simple by design.  Less choices to make, less opportunities to fudge the lines.  After 9 days of fasting, I’m eating to my fill.  I’m going to abstain from Halloween candies, but I plan to have a full on Thanksgiving feast.  It will be the first carbs (should I hold to my eating plan) in a couple months.  I’m planning a post feast fast to get me back into ketosis.

Should everyone engage in multi day or week fasts?  Yes!  No, not really.  It’s a health practice for sure but nothing is the right option for everyone.  It’s hard.  It’s very hard.  Turns out that’s a feature, not a bug.

- Always consult your doctor for your own specific needs.

- Educate yourself to fully participate in your health planning.

- I’ve built “fasting muscles” over years including knowledge both book learning and experiential understanding.
