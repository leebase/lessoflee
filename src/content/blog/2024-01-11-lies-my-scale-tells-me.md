Scales are useful but they don’t always tell the truth. Strength training builds muscle. Muscle burns more fat all the time. So, when you start strength training, it should help you lose weight. Right? Wrong.

Why is this wrong? No, it is NOT because muscle weighs more than fat. It takes time to put on muscle, so when the scale goes up 8 pounds in a week…that’s not “extra muscle for the first few lifting sessions”.

I mean, come on – I’m trying to lose fat and gain muscle this year. Yet, as soon as I start lifting – my weight jumps up eight pounds!

The scale is simply lying. Or rather, the scale is telling you how much “everything in your body” weighs. Your muscle, your bones, your skin, the food you just ate that hasn’t digested fully yet. And yes, the biggest factor of all is water. None of that is the weight I’m trying to lose. I want to lose the fat. But the scale doesn’t measure fat, but total weight.

When you begin strength training you add water weight for two reasons. You are tearing down your muscle so the body builds them back up a bit stronger. To achieve this, there is muscle inflammation and the body recruits water as part of the healing process. Second, your muscle, like your liver, stores glycogen (a storage form of glucose). When you start strength training your body may to store even more glycogen in the muscles. For every glycogen molecule, there are two water molecules.

So, just as when you start losing weight on a low carb diet, it’s mostly water weight. When you gain quickly when lifting weights, it’s also water.

And water weight is not what we are looking to lose.

It can be frustrating to be eating well and exercising only to see your weight increase. In time your clothes will inform you that you’ve done the right thing for your body. Your body will gain muscle and lose fat. The scale may not tell that story in the short term.

Consider the scale a liar, and keep on doing what your body needs you to do. Don’t let the water weight gain discourage you. Be patient and your belt will become lose and your shirts will stop untucking. And yes, eventually even the scale will come down.

Even though I understand these things, my emotions aren’t always in line with my mind. I have to remind myself about the limits of the scale for telling me whether or not, on any given day, I’m making the progress I desire. I wrote this for me. I hope it helps others too.

p.s.  Always consult your own health team for your specific needs
