The weather is already cooling in Chicago, Winter is Coming!  This was my thought three years ago when I was a mere few months into my health reclamation journey.   I had already, astoundingly, put Type 2 Diabetes into remission.  I went from an A1c of 11.2 on 3 meds to below diabetic while being free of the need for meds to control my blood sugar.  To help with the diabetes recovery I had started walking again – walking with a cane around my neighborhood.  I was unstable, stairs were difficult for me, and I couldn’t walk across my grass lawn.  What was I going to do to keep walking and improving my health when winter was going to turn me into a shut in?

I saw an ad for the Meta Quest 2 Virtual Reality headset and a workout app called Supernatural.  It spoke to the computer geek in me.  It was an exercise I could do at home in the winter.  The best $400 I have ever spent is buying this VR gaming system and the $10/month for the app subscription is by far the most effective gym membership I’ve ever paid for.  It’s been three years and I’m still exercising with this setup.  

What I didn’t know was the huge transformation the Supernatural apps and the virtual coaches were going to have.  Ultimately all one is doing is waving your harms around, squatting and lunging. You don’t need any tech to do this.  Absolutely all the benefits that were to come – sprang from moving my body.  What Supernatural VR did was make moving my body FUN!  My drab work out space disappears and I’m transported to amazingly beautiful places: Mountain tops, rivers, beaches, the Great Wall of China, Machu Pichu, and even the moon.   There is a wide variety of music to workout to and they are always adding new routines.  This has kept my attention and interest for three years – far longer than any gym membership ever has – for me.

But what is this transformation I’ve hinted at?  When I started I still needed a cane to walk.  Stairs?  I had to put both feet on the same stair before carefully stepping up or down holding onto a rail and/or my cane.  I have osteoarthritis and there is pain in my knees.  I had to set the cane aside to work out, and I was not particularly stable.  I fell once or twice in the beginning.  Squats and lunges are part of the routine and I could barely do them.  I started on the shortest, low level, easiest routines and they were a challenge.   Still, it was very fun and because of this, I kept at it.  And as I kept at it, my legs started getting stronger.

Within weeks I noticed I was walking up and down stairs more easily.  I was hit with a profound realization – I had become weak!  Sure, I had arthritis which made walking and stairs painful, but the difficulty I had with stairs and my instability was simply muscle weakness.  The squats and lunges were bringing muscle strength back to my legs and with strength comes ability.

I don’t remember the specific day that I stopped using the cane.  It sits in my garage collecting dust.

Fast forward three years – I now do the hardest, longest, most difficult routines that Supernatural has.  Supernatural got me through that first winter and into spring stronger and more able.  I went on to add other forms of exercise including running.  It took a solid year of effort to just take my first running steps.  I eventually was able to run in a 5k in down town Chicago.  A far cry from not being able to walk across my yard.  

Thank you Meta and the Supernatural team, the coaches and routine designers.

I developed a mantra along the way as I kept improving from walking with a cane, struggling with stairs to running 5ks and sprint intervals.  

How much more? A little more.

How much farther? A bit farther.

Always.

The improvements didn’t happen in a day.  I have done 273 of these workouts, walked every day, and so on.  No grand gestures needed, no hero lifts.  Just doing the little I could and keep pressing for more.  Three years and counting in my health recovery journey. A lifetime more to go.  

p.s. Always consult your own health team for your specific needs.  

p.p.s. Educate yourself to fully participate in your health strategy.
